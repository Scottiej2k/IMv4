# Piacere: how to say what you like

## What it is

In English you say *I like pizza*. In Italian you can't say *io piaccio la pizza*. The verb **piacere** works the other way round: literally, *pizza is pleasing to me*.

So the person who likes something is **not** the subject of the sentence. The thing that is liked is the subject, and the person appears as a small pronoun in front of the verb: **mi**, **ti**, **gli**, **le**, **ci**, **vi**.

Think of it as: *to me, pizza is pleasing*.

## How it works

### The little pronouns

| Pronoun | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **mi** | to me | **Mi** piace la pizza. |
| **ti** | to you (a friend) | **Ti** piace la carne? |
| **gli** | to him | **Gli** piace il caffè. |
| **le** | to her | **Le** piace il tè. |
| **ci** | to us | **Ci** piace l'Italia. |
| **vi** | to you all | **Vi** piace ballare? |

These go **before** the verb, never after.

### One thing or many things?

- **mi piace** + one thing, or a verb in the infinitive: *Mi piace la torta. Mi piace cucinare.*
- **mi piacciono** + more than one thing: *Mi piacciono i biscotti. Mi piacciono il calcio e il nuoto.*

This is the only change: **piace** for singular, **piacciono** for plural. The pronoun stays the same.

### Adding emphasis: a me

If you want to stress who likes it, or make a contrast, you put **a** + the pronoun in front:

- **A me** piace. / **A te** piace? / **A lui** piace. / **A lei** piace.
- **A me no** (= I don't like it).
- **Anche a me** (= me too, I like it too).

You will hear these constantly in conversation. In this chapter, Marina hides her opinion and only whispers:

[[Anche a me]]

And Roberto refuses everything with one short phrase:

[[A me no]]

### Questions and negatives

To ask, just change your intonation: *Ti piace la pizza?* To say no, put **non** before the verb: *Non mi piace la carne.* You can also answer with **A me no**.

## From the story

[[Ti piace la pizza?]] — A simple yes/no question: *ti* + *piace* + one singular thing.

[[Sì! Mi piace la pizza!]] — Leo answers with the same structure, swapping *ti* for *mi*.

[[Mi piacciono il calcio e il nuoto]] — Two things liked, so the verb becomes *piacciono*.

[[Anche a me!]] — Kevin agrees: "me too." The *a me* form adds emphasis.

[[A me no]] — Chiara's answer all through lunch: "not me."

[[Mi piace cucinare]] — With a verb, always *piace* + infinitive, never *piacciono*.

[[A me piacciono tutte le cose]] — Marina's confession: plural things, emphasized *a me*.

[[Le piace la pizza?]] — Formal *Le* (to you, sir/madam) with a singular thing.

[[Mi piace il tè di Lucia]] — The chapter's last line, and the first honest thing Chiara says.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **"Io piaccio la pizza."** Never. *Piacere* does not mean "to like" as a transitive verb. Say **Mi piace la pizza.**
2. **"Mi piace i biscotti."** If the thing is plural, you need **piacciono**: *Mi piacciono i biscotti.*
3. **Using *piacciono* with a verb.** *Mi piace ballare*, not *mi piacciono ballare*. The infinitive counts as one thing.
4. **Putting the pronoun after the verb.** It's *mi piace*, not *piace mi*.
5. **Mixing up *gli* and *le*.** *Gli* = to him, *le* = to her. (With a capital **L**, **Le** is the polite "to you.")
6. **Forgetting that *Le* takes a third-person verb.** With a stranger: *Le piace la pizza?*, never *Le piaci*.

Fill in the blank with **piace** or **piacciono**, and the right pronoun.

**Answers**

1. gli piace — *A Leo gli piace la pizza.* (or simply: *Gli piace la pizza.*)
2. mi piacciono — plural *biscotti*.
3. piace — infinitive *ballare*.
4. le piace — *non le piace la carne.*
5. piacciono — plural *verdure*, formal *Le*.
6. ti piace — one thing, *il tè*.
7. Ci piace — *ci* = to us.
8. gli piace — *lo sport* is singular.
