# Necessity: *bisogna*, *avere bisogno di*, *è necessario*

## What it is

In this chapter the characters keep telling Kevin what he *has to* do. Italian has three main ways to express necessity, and they are not interchangeable:

1. **bisogna** + infinitive — "you have to / one must / it's necessary to". No subject at all: it works for everybody.
2. **avere bisogno di** + noun or infinitive — "to need (something / to do something)". This one *does* have a subject: *io ho bisogno di…*
3. **è necessario / è importante** + infinitive — "it is necessary / important to…". An impersonal statement, a bit formal.

And one extra, because it comes up all the time when we talk about health:

4. **mi fa male / mi fanno male** + body part — "my … hurts / my … hurt".

## How it works

### 1. *bisogna*

**bisogna** is a fixed, impersonal form. It never changes, and there is no subject pronoun with it:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Bisogna** mettere la crema. | You have to put the cream on. |
| **Bisogna** bere molta acqua. | One has to drink a lot of water. |
| **Bisogna** mangiare. | You have to eat. |

It is almost always followed by an **infinitive**. To say *who* has to do it, add **a** + person: *Bisogna mangiare, Kevin!* or *A te bisogna riposare.* Note: never *io bisogna*, never *noi bisogniamo*.

### 2. *avere bisogno di*

This is the verb you use for a real need, and it changes with the person:

| | |
|---|---|
| io **ho bisogno di** | I need |
| tu **hai bisogno di** | you need |
| lui/lei **ha bisogno di** | he/she needs |
| noi **abbiamo bisogno di** | we need |
| voi **avete bisogno di** | you (pl.) need |
| loro **hanno bisogno di** | they need |

It can be followed by a **noun** — *Ho bisogno di una crema* — or by an **infinitive** — *Hai bisogno di andare in farmacia.* In the negative it means "don't need": *Non ho bisogno di niente.*

### 3. *è necessario / è importante*

Impersonal and slightly more formal than *bisogna*:

- **È necessario** bere molta acqua.
- **È necessario** andare dal medico solo se la febbre è alta.
- **Non è necessario** comprare tutto.
- **È importante** riposare.

Use *è necessario* when you want to sound serious or official (a doctor, a pharmacist, a notice). Again, the verb after it stays in the **infinitive**.

### 4. *mi fa male / mi fanno male*

Here you talk about your body. The thing that hurts is the **subject** of the sentence, and the person is the indirect object (*mi, ti, gli, le, ci, vi, gli*):

| | |
|---|---|
| **Mi fa male** la testa. | My head hurts. |
| **Mi fa male** la schiena. | My back hurts. |
| **Mi fanno male** le spalle. | My shoulders hurt. |
| **Mi fanno male** i piedi. | My feet hurt. |

So: **fa male** + one thing (singular), **fanno male** + more than one thing (plural). *Mi fa male il piede* / *Mi fanno male i piedi.* You can also add *da* + time: *Mi fa male la schiena da tre giorni.*

## From the story

«Io invece non **ho bisogno di** niente.» — “Me, on the other hand, I **don't need** anything.” (`s02e23-1-016`)
— *avere bisogno di* with a negative and a noun (*niente*), with the subject *io*.

«**Bisogna** metterla perché il **sole** è forte.» — “**You have to** put it on because the **sun** is strong.” (`s02e23-1-013`)
— *bisogna* + infinitive, impersonal: no subject, singular form.

«È **necessario** bere molta acqua con questo caldo.» — “**It is necessary** to drink a lot of water in this heat.” (`s02e23-1-014`)
— *è necessario* + infinitive, the more formal way of saying the same thing.

«Sì, brucia. **Mi fa male** tutto.» — “Yes, it burns. Everything **hurts**.” (`s02e23-1-064`)
— *mi fa male* with a general subject: everything hurts.

«Mi **fa male** la **schiena** da tre giorni.» — “My **back** has **been hurting** for three days.” (`s02e23-3-015`)
— *mi fa male* + singular body part, plus duration with *da*.

«Il **sintomo** due: mi **fanno male** le **spalle**.» — “**Symptom** two: my **shoulders hurt**.” (`s02e23-3-034`)
— *fanno male* because the body part (*le spalle*) is plural.

«Allora **bisogna** prendere uno **sciroppo**.» — “Then **you have to** take a **syrup**.” (`s02e23-3-025`)
— *bisogna* + infinitive, used for advice at the pharmacy.

«La **ricetta** la fa il **medico**.» — “A **prescription** is written by the **doctor**.” (`s02e23-3-053`)
— the opposite structure: not necessity, but who does what.

«**Bisogna** ascoltare il corpo. Non serve comprare tutto il negozio.» — “**You have to** listen to your body. You don't need to buy the whole shop.” (`s02e23-6-017`)
— *bisogna* + infinitive; *non serve* is the natural negative partner ("there's no need").

«La **ricetta** non si dà. Si mangia.» — “The **recipe** is not given. It is eaten.” (`s02e23-7-024`)
— *si* + verb, impersonal, the same impersonal spirit as *bisogna*.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

- **"Io bisogna…"** — no. *Bisogna* has no subject. Say *Bisogna andare* or *Io devo andare*.
- **Conjugating *bisogna*.** There is no *bisogniamo*, no *bisogni*. It is always *bisogna*.
- **"Ho bisogno una crema"** — the preposition **di** is obligatory: *Ho bisogno **di** una crema.*
- **"Ho bisogno di mangiare" vs *Bisogna mangiare*.** Both are correct, but *ho bisogno di* means *I* need; *bisogna* is universal.
- **"Mi fa male le spalle"** — the verb must agree with the body part, not with you: *Mi **fanno** male le spalle.*
- **"Mi fa male la mia testa"** — the possessive is unnecessary and unnatural here. Say *Mi fa male la testa.*
- **Using the infinitive after *avere bisogno* without *di*:** *Ho bisogno andare* is wrong; it's *Ho bisogno **di** andare.*

## Practice

Rewrite each sentence using the structure in brackets.

1. Kevin needs a cream. → *Kevin ____ una crema.* (avere bisogno di)
2. You have to go to the pharmacy. → ____ andare in farmacia. (bisogna)
3. My shoulders hurt. → ____ le spalle. (mi fa/fanno male)
4. It is necessary to rest. → ____ riposare. (è necessario)
5. Does he need pills? → ____ pillole? (avere bisogno di, lui)
6. My head hurts and my feet hurt too. → ____ la testa e ____ i piedi.
7. You don't need anything. → Non ____ niente. (avere bisogno di, tu)
8. You have to eat, Franco says. → ____ mangiare, dice Franco. (bisogna)

### Answer key

1. Kevin **ha bisogno di** una crema.
2. **Bisogna** andare in farmacia.
3. **Mi fanno male** le spalle.
4. **È necessario** riposare.
5. **Ha bisogno di** pillole?
6. **Mi fa male** la testa e **mi fanno male** i piedi.
7. Non **hai bisogno di** niente.
8. **Bisogna** mangiare, dice Franco.
