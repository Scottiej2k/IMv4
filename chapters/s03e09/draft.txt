# The Imperative with Pronouns: *dimmi*, *fammi*, *seguitemi*

## What it is

In Italian, when you tell someone to do something, you use the **imperative** (*Guarda! Guardate! Andiamo!*). Very often, the thing you're telling them to do involves a person or a thing: *tell **me***, *give **me***, *follow **me***, *do **it***.

In English the pronoun comes after the verb, as a separate word: *give me*, *follow me*, *do it*. In Italian, with the *tu*, *noi* and *voi* imperatives, the pronoun **sticks onto the end of the verb** and becomes one word: *dammi*, *seguitemi*, *fallo*.

This chapter is full of them, because Kevin spends the whole day saying things like *seguitemi* and *dimmi* to his family.

## How it works

**1. Attach the pronoun to the imperative form.**

| Command | With pronoun | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *segui* (tu) | *seguimi!* | follow me! |
| *segui* (tu) | *seguitemi!* | follow me! (plural, *voi*) |
| *guarda* (tu) | *guardalo!* | look at him/it! |
| *gira* (tu) | *girati!* | turn around! |
| *aspetta* (tu) | *aspettami!* | wait for me! |
| *anda* → *andiamo* (noi) | *andiamoci* | let's go there |

**2. Indirect pronouns work the same way:** *mi, ti, gli, le, ci, vi*.

| Without pronoun | With pronoun | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *dici* (tu) | *dimmi!* | tell me! |
| *dici* (voi) | *ditemi!* | tell me! (plural) |
| *dici a loro* | *digli!* | tell him/them! |
| *telefoni* | *telefonami!* | call me! |

**3. The doubling rule.** Some imperatives are only **one syllable**: *da'* (from *dare*), *di'* (from *dire*), *fa'* (from *fare*), *sta'* (from *stare*), *va'* (from *andare*). With these, the pronoun doubles its first consonant:

| Command | Attach | Result |
|---|---|---|
| *da'* + *mi* | d-a-mmi | **dammi** |
| *fa'* + *mi* | f-a-mmi | **fammi** |
| *di'* + *mi* | d-i-mmi | **dimmi** |
| *sta'* + *mi* | st-a-mmi | **stammi** (*stammi bene!*) |
| *va'* + *ci* | v-a-ci → va**cci** | *vacci!* |

So *da' mi la mano* becomes **dammi la mano**, and *fa' mi vedere* becomes **fammi vedere**.

**4. Where does the pronoun NOT attach?**
- With the *Lei* (formal) imperative: *Mi dica!*, *Mi scusi!* — separate.
- With the negative *tu* imperative: *non mi dire!*, *non mi aspettare!* — separate, before the verb.

## Common mistakes

1. **Writing the pronoun separately.** English speakers say *\*mi dimmi*, *\*mi guarda*. In Italian with *tu/voi*: *dimmi*, *guardami* — one word.
2. **Forgetting the doubling.** *\*dami*, *\*fami*, *\*dimi* are wrong. It's always **dammi, fammi, dimmi, stammi**.
3. **Attaching with negative *tu***: *\*non dimmi niente* is wrong. Say **non dirmi niente**.
4. **Using *mi* where English says "me" but Italian needs *a me*.** For emphasis, Italian keeps the separate pronoun: *dillo **a me**, non a lui*.
5. **Attaching after *Lei***: *\*dicami* is old-fashioned. With strangers and officials: **Mi dica.**
