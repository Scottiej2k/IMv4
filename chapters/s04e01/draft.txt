# Futuro semplice: regular forms

## What it is

The *futuro semplice* is the Italian "will" form. You use it to talk about something that hasn't happened yet: a plan, a decision, a prediction, a promise.

In English you say *I will marry*, *she will dance*, *we will decide*. Italian does the same thing with one word: **mi sposerò**, **ballerà**, **decideremo**. The person is already inside the verb ending, so you usually don't need a pronoun.

This is the tense of this whole episode: a wedding that hasn't happened, dates that aren't chosen, a business that doesn't exist yet. Everybody in the story is talking about the future.

## How it works

Take the infinitive, drop the final **-e**, and add the ending. The ending is the same for **-are** and **-ere** verbs, with a couple of extra vowels for **-ire** verbs.

| | **sposare** (-are) | **credere** (-ere) | **partire** (-ire) |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | sposer**ò** | creder**ò** | partir**ò** |
| tu | sposer**ai** | creder**ai** | partir**ai** |
| lui / lei | sposer**à** | creder**à** | partir**à** |
| noi | sposer**emo** | creder**emo** | partir**emo** |
| voi | sposer**ete** | creder**ete** | partir**ete** |
| loro | sposer**anno** | creder**anno** | partir**anno** |

Two things to notice:

- The **-are** and **-ere** endings are identical. The **-ire** forms only add an **i** in the *noi* and *voi* forms (*partiremo*, *partirete*).
- The accents matter: **sposerò** has an accent on the final **-ò**. Without it, **sposero** is a different word (an old word for "husband"). Write the accent.

### Spelling changes

Some verbs change their spelling before the ending, but you still hear the same sound.

| Verb type | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-care**, **-gare** | add **h** to keep the hard sound | cercare → **cercherò**; pagare → **pagherò** |
| **-ciare**, **-giare** | drop the **i** | cominciare → **comincerò**; mangiare → **mangerò** |
| **-iare** (with a stressed i) | drop the **i** | studiare → **studierò** |

Reflexive verbs simply take the reflexive pronoun in front: *sposarsi* → **mi sposerò**, **ci sposeremo**.

### Plans, or just the present?

Italian often uses the present for things that are already arranged and on a timetable: *Il treno parte alle sette* ("The train leaves at seven"). The future is used when you're announcing a decision, making a promise, or predicting. In this episode you'll hear both, sometimes in the same conversation.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the accent.** *Io sposero* is wrong; it's **sposerò**. The accent is part of the spelling.
- **Mixing up future and conditional.** *Sposerò* = "I will marry." *Sposerei* = "I would marry." The *-ei, -esti, -ebbe* endings belong to another tense.
- **Dropping the h in -care / -gare.** *Cercerò* and *pagerò* are wrong: **cercherò**, **pagherò**.
- **Keeping the i in -ciare / -giare.** *Comincierò* is wrong: **comincerò**. The i disappears because the ending already contains one.
- **Using the future for everything in the future.** For a timetable already set, Italian prefers the present: *Domenica partiamo* is more natural than *Domenica partirò*.
- **Worrying about the irregular verbs.** A few very common ones (*essere* → **sarà**, *venire* → **verrà**, *andare* → **andremo**) don't follow these rules. You'll meet them in the next lesson; for now, recognize them when they appear.
