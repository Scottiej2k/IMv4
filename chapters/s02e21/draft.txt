# The impersonal *si*: "you do it this way"

## What it is

Italian has a neat little word — **si** — that lets you talk about what *people* do in general, without naming anybody. English has several ways of doing the same job: *you don't drink cappuccino after eleven*, *one doesn't do that*, *people say*, *they eat bread with their hands*. Italian almost always prefers **si**.

It is what you use for rules, customs, habits and general truths: how a thing is done, what is normal, what is not done.

> A tavola si mangia insieme.
> *At the table you eat together.*

Nobody in particular is eating. Everybody is.

## How it works

**si + verb in the third person singular.** That's the whole rule for the basic pattern.

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| si mangia | people eat / you eat / one eats |
| si beve | people drink / you drink |
| si dice | people say / you say |
| si fa | people do / you do |
| non si fa | you don't do that |
| si può | one can / you can |
| si aspetta | you wait |
| si impara | you learn |

The verb is *singular* even if you are talking about millions of people: **si mangia** = everybody eats.

**The subject is never stated.** You don't say *si mangia la gente*. There is no *io* or *tu* either: *si mangia*, full stop.

### Asking and answering

Questions and negatives keep the same form:

- **Si può?** — *Can one? / Is that allowed?*
- **Non si fa.** — *You don't do that.*
- **Come si dice?** — *How do you say it?* (This is Kevin's favorite sentence in the whole series.)

### Where the *si* goes: before the verb, always

- **Si beve** il caffè dopo il pasto. ✔
- ~~Beve si il caffè~~ ✘

### With *si può* + infinitive

A very common pattern: **si può** + infinitive = *you can / it's possible to*.

- **Si può** mettere l'ananas sulla pizza. — *You can put pineapple on pizza.*
- **Si può** parlare piano, per favore? — *Could you speak quietly, please?*

### A preview: si + plural noun

When the thing being done is the subject of the sentence — not the person doing it — the verb agrees with *that thing*, not with *si*. So you will meet **si** with a *plural* verb:

- In Italia non **si scrivono** le regole in un quaderno. — *In Italy, rules aren't written in a notebook.* (subject = *le regole* → plural verb)
- **Si imparano** guardando. — *They are learned by watching.*

Compare:
- **Si mangia** il pane. (*people eat bread* → singular, general)
- Il pane **si mangia** così. (*bread is eaten like this* → the bread is the subject)

At this level, don't worry about choosing between them. Just recognize that after **si** you sometimes hear the plural, because the thing is doing the talking.

### The difference from *noi* and from *tu*

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Si mangia | general, everybody | A che ora si mangia? |
| Mangiamo | we specifically | Noi mangiamo alle otto. |
| Tu mangi | you, one person | Tu mangi troppo. |

*Si mangia* is not *we eat*. If you mean your own family, say *mangiamo*.

## From the story

[[Dopo le undici non si beve il cappuccino.]] — The classic rule, in its negative form: *non si beve*.
[[Il pane si mangia con le mani, la pizza anche.]] — The thing eaten (il pane) is the subject, so the verb agrees with it.
[[Si può con il pane. Non con la pasta.]] — *si può* with the thing understood, not repeated.
[[Al bar si beve quello che è l'ora.]] — Gino's rule: a general truth about bars and time.
[[In Italia non si scrivono le regole in un quaderno.]] — *si* with a plural verb, because *le regole* is the subject.
[[Si imparano guardando.]] — Same pattern in one short sentence.
[[A tavola l'amore si vede nel piatto vuoto.]] — *si vede*: love is seen, it shows.
[[Si può mettere anche a destra.]] — Permission, politely generalized.
[[Si può rompere una regola di proposito?]] — A question of principle, with *si può* + infinitive.
[[Non si fa uscire senza salutare.]] — *Non si fa* = that is not done, a flat prohibition.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

- **Adding a subject.** *Si mangia la gente* is wrong. *Si mangia* already includes everybody.
- **Using the plural verb when the person is meant.** *Si mangiano il pane* is wrong; the verb stays singular: *si mangia il pane*. The plural only appears when the *thing* is the subject (*si scrivono le regole*).
- **Translating "you" with *tu*.** English "you don't do that here" is not *tu non fai così*; it's *non si fa così*.
- **Forgetting *non* goes before *si*.** *Non si fa*, never *si non fa*.
- **Putting *si* after the verb.** In modern standard Italian, *si* comes first: *si dice*, not *dicesi* (which is old-fashioned and only in signs).
- **Confusing *si mangia* with *mi mangio*.** *Mi mangio una pizza* means "I eat a pizza up," which is something else entirely.
