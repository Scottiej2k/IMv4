# Il trapassato prossimo

## What it is

The **trapassato prossimo** is the Italian past perfect: *I had done*, *she had left*, *we had never said it*. You use it when you are already telling a story in the past and you want to jump **further back** — to something that had happened *before* the moment you are describing.

In this episode, Gennaro tells Chiara about Lucia's life before Franco, and Nadia tells Matteo about a decision she had made months earlier. In both cases the main story is in the past; the trapassato goes one step further back.

«Cinque anni di avvocati, di carte, di silenzio» dice Gennaro. — “Five years of lawyers, paperwork, silence,” Gennaro says. (`s04e08-2-024`)

## How it works

Form it with the **imperfetto of avere or essere** plus the **past participle**.

| Subject | avere | essere |
|---|---|---|
| io | avevo | ero |
| tu | avevi | eri |
| lui/lei | aveva | era |
| noi | avevamo | eravamo |
| voi | avevate | eravate |
| loro | avevano | erano |

**With avere** (most verbs):

- *avevo detto* — I had said
- *aveva pensato* — she had thought
- *avevamo visto* — we had seen

**With essere** (movement, change-of-state and reflexive verbs). The participle agrees with the subject:

- *era andato* / *era andata* — he/she had gone
- *erano partiti* — they had left
- *ci eravamo abbracciate* — we (two women) had hugged

**One action before another past action.** The main event is in the passato prossimo or imperfetto; the earlier event goes into the trapassato:

- *Quando ho conosciuto Lucia, lei **era già** separata.* — When I met Lucia, she **was already** separated.
- *Non ho risposto perché **avevo già** deciso.* — I didn't answer because **I had already** decided.

**Già, mai and appena** love the trapassato:

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **già** | already | *L'offerta **era già** arrivata.* |
| **mai** | never | *Non te l'**avevo mai** detto.* |
| **appena** | just | ***Aveva appena** chiuso quando ha suonato il telefono.* |

With **mai**, remember the double negative: *non ... mai*. Italian needs both halves.

## From the story

«**Prima di allora** mia madre è stata sola dieci anni» dice Gennaro. — “**Before then** my mother was alone for ten years,” Gennaro says. (`s04e08-2-046`) — *Prima di allora* ("before then") is the classic signal; the event before it can be a passato prossimo with a time span.
«Non te l'**avevo mai** detto» dice Gennaro. — “I had **never** told you,” Gennaro says. (`s04e08-7-017`) — *non ... mai* + trapassato: the most common shape of a confession.
«Questo non l'**avevo mai** detto a nessuno» dice Gennaro. — “I had **never** told anyone this,” Gennaro says. (`s04e08-2-041`) — Same pattern with the object pronoun *lo*: *non l'avevo mai detto*.
«Avevo pensato che eri arrivata qui per prendere il posto di mia madre.» — “I had thought you'd come here to take my mother's place.” (`s04e08-4-048`) — What Chiara had thought, before she learned the truth.
«Perché **prima di allora** avevo perso due volte.» — “Because **before then** I had lost twice.” (`s04e08-4-073`) — A trapassato straight after *prima di allora*: the losing happened before Franco.
«L'avevo presa dalla casa di Milano otto anni fa» dice Gennaro. — “I had taken it from the house in Milan eight years ago,” Gennaro says. (`s04e08-7-016`) — A trapassato with a time expression: the taking was eight years ago, before he told her.
«A cena, io e Lucia ci siamo **abbracciate**.» — “At dinner, Lucia and I **hugged** each other.” (`s04e08-5-099`) — Not trapassato, but the same *essere* agreement: *abbracciate*, two women.
Ma Franco ha **già** chiuso la porta. — But Franco has **already** closed the door. (`s04e08-7-042`) — *già* with the passato prossimo: no "earlier past" here, so no trapassato.
«Perché la sua valigia è ancora a Francoforte.» — “Because his suitcase is still in Frankfurt.” (`s04e08-5-027`) — Present tense: don't jump back when there is nothing to jump back to.

## Common mistakes

**1. Using the trapassato when there is no "before".** If two events are simply both in the past, use the passato prossimo for both: *Ho aperto il bar e ho fatto il caffè.* The trapassato only marks an event **earlier than another past event**.

**2. Forgetting agreement with essere.** It is *era andata* for a woman, *erano partiti* for a group of men or a mixed group, *ci siamo abbracciate* if both people are women.

**3. Dropping the double negative with *mai*.** English says "I had never told you"; Italian needs both halves: *Non te l'avevo mai detto.*

**4. Putting *già* in the wrong place.** *Già* normally sits between the auxiliary and the participle: *aveva già perso*, *era già arrivata*. Not *già aveva perso* in ordinary speech.

**5. Confusing *prima* and *prima di allora*.** *Prima* alone means "before" in general; *prima di allora* means "before that moment", and it is a strong signal that a past-before-the-past is coming.

**6. Using the trapassato remoto.** You will meet *ebbe fatto*, *fu andato* in old novels. Do not use it in speech or in modern writing. The spoken past perfect is always *avevo fatto*, *ero andato*.

## Practice

1. Complete with the trapassato of the verb in parentheses: *Quando ho telefonato, Nadia ______ (già / uscire).*
2. Complete: *Non ______ mai ______ (io / vedere) quel film prima di ieri.*
3. Complete with **essere**: *Lucia e suo marito ______ (separarsi) nel 1998.*
4. Choose the right tense — passato prossimo or trapassato: *Ieri ______ (io / mangiare) una pizza, perché il giorno prima non ______ (io / cenare).*
5. Put *già* in the right place: *Aveva ______ capito tutto, ma non diceva niente.*
6. Translate: "She had never told anyone about the divorce."
7. Translate: "When I arrived, they had already eaten."
8. Join the two sentences with a trapassato: *Sono arrivato tardi. Il treno era partito.* → *Quando sono arrivato, ...*

### Answer key

1. *era già uscita*
2. *Non avevo mai visto*
3. *si erano separati*
4. *ho mangiato* … *avevo cenato*
5. *Aveva **già** capito tutto, ma non diceva niente.*
6. *Non aveva mai raccontato il divorzio a nessuno.*
7. *Quando sono arrivato, avevano già mangiato.*
8. *Quando sono arrivato, il treno era già partito.*
