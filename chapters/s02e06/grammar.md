# Direct Object Pronouns: lo, la, li, le — and mi, ti, ci, vi

## What they are

A **direct object pronoun** replaces the thing (or person) that receives the action of the verb, so you don't repeat the noun:

- *Guardo **la foto*** → *La guardo.* ("I look at the photo" → "I look at it.")

In this chapter, Nadia, Matteo and half of Via dei Tigli spend a whole day saying **lo so**, **non lo so** and **lo dicono tutti** — a crash course in Italian's most useful little words.

## How it works

The forms:

| Person | Pronoun | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| io | **mi** | me |
| tu | **ti** | you |
| lui/lei (thing) | **lo** / **la** | him / it (m.), it (f.) |
| noi | **ci** | us |
| voi | **vi** | you (plural) |
| loro | **li** / **le** | them (m.) / them (f.) |

**Position rule 1 — before a conjugated verb.** The pronoun sits in front of the verb, not after it:

- *Lo so.* (I know it.)
- *Non lo so.* (I don't know it.) — the *non* stays in front of the pronoun.
- *La guardo.* (I look at her/it.)
- *Mi ascolti?* (Are you listening to me?)

**Position rule 2 — attached to the infinitive.** With an infinitive, the pronoun joins onto the end as one word:

- *Non voglio dirlo.* (I don't want to say it.)
- *Nadia decide quando dirlo agli altri.* (Nadia decides when to tell it to the others.)

**Agreement.** *lo / la / li / le* agree with the noun they replace:

| Noun replaced | Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| il caffè | **lo** | *Il caffè **lo** prendo io.* |
| la tazza | **la** | *La tazza? **La** prendo io.* |
| i biscotti | **li** | *I biscotti? **Li** prendo io.* |
| le tazze | **le** | *Le tazze? **Le** metto qui.* |

**lo so / non lo so.** This is the fixed, everyday way to say "I know (it)" and "I don't know (it)". *Lo* stands for a fact, a piece of news, a whole idea — nothing more precise is needed. You will hear it constantly in Italy, exactly as in this episode.

## From the story

«Matteo, **ti** devo dire una **notizia**.» dice Nadia. — "Matteo, I have to tell **you** some **news**," Nadia says. (`s02e06-1-012`)
*ti* = "to you": the pronoun goes before the conjugated verb *devo*.

Matteo **la** guarda. — Matteo looks at **her**. (`s02e06-2-020`)
*la* replaces *Nadia*: the pronoun comes before *guarda*.

«No, no, no. **La** prendo io.» dice Matteo. — "No, no, no. I'll take **it**," Matteo says. (`s02e06-7-006`)
*La* (here in a capital letter at the start) replaces *la tazza*.

_Perché non **lo** dice?_ pensa Franco. — _Why doesn't he say **it**?_ (`s02e06-3-011`)
The *non* stays first, then the pronoun: *non lo dice*.

«**Lo** so da giovedì.» dice Franco. — "I've known **it** since Thursday," Franco says. (`s02e06-3-044`)
The most common sentence in this chapter: *lo* = the news about Nadia.

*La* replaces *la foto*.

«Nadia decide quando dirlo agli altri.» dice Lucia. — "Nadia decides when to tell the others," Lucia says. (`s02e06-3-110`)
Attached to the infinitive *dire*: *dirlo*, written as one word.

«Come **lo** sapete?» chiede Nadia. — "How do you know **it**?" Nadia asks. (`s02e06-7-034`)
*lo* before the conjugated *sapete*; the question word changes nothing.

«Il caffè **lo** prendi tu.» dice Nadia. — "You make **it** yourself," Nadia says. (`s02e06-1-065`)
Italian often names the object first, then echoes it with *lo*.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

- **Putting the pronoun after the verb.** English says "I know *it*"; Italian says *Lo so*. In front, always, unless it's attached to an infinitive.
- **Forgetting the agreement.** A cup is *la tazza*, so it's *la prendo*, not *lo prendo*. Ask yourself: masculine or feminine? singular or plural?
- **Saying *so* alone.** *So* by itself sounds unfinished in this meaning; Italians say *lo so*. Likewise *non lo so*, never *non so* when you mean "I don't know (that)".
- **Using *lo* for a person you would address with *Lei* or in a formal setting.** *Lo* can mean "him," but politeness has its own forms, which come later in the course.
- **Splitting the infinitive.** It's *dirlo*, one word, never *dire lo*.
- **Burying *lo* after *non* in the wrong order.** It's *non lo so*, not *lo non so*.
