# Irregular verbs: *stare*, *dare*, *dire*, *uscire*, *bere*

## What they are

Most Italian verbs follow a pattern. *Parlare* gives *parlo, parli, parla…*; *credere* gives *credo, credi, crede…*; *dormire* gives *dormo, dormi, dorme…*. Five very common verbs do not follow the pattern, or follow it strangely. They are **stare** (to stay, to be), **dare** (to give), **dire** (to say, to tell), **uscire** (to go out) and **bere** (to drink).

These five verbs are everywhere in daily Italian. *Come stai?* — *How are you?* *Cosa dici?* — *What are you saying?* *Esci stasera?* — *Are you going out tonight?* *Bevo un caffè.* — *I'm drinking a coffee.* You cannot speak Italian for ten minutes without one of them.

## How they work

Here are the present-tense forms. Learn them as a rhythm, not as a list.

| | stare | dare | dire | uscire | bere |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| io | **sto** | **do** | **dico** | **esco** | **bevo** |
| tu | **stai** | **dai** | **dici** | **esci** | **bevi** |
| lui / lei / Lei | **sta** | **dà** | **dice** | **esce** | **beve** |
| noi | **stiamo** | **diamo** | **diciamo** | **usciamo** | **beviamo** |
| voi | **state** | **date** | **dite** | **uscite** | **bevete** |
| loro | **stanno** | **danno** | **dicono** | **escono** | **bevono** |

Some things worth noticing:

- **stare** and **dare** both take an *-o* in the *io* form and an *-ai* in the *tu* form: *sto / stai*, *do / dai*.
- **dare** keeps the accent in the third person: *dà*, not *da* (*da* means "from"). *Dà* also has two *-a-* sounds: *diamo*, *date*, *danno*.
- **dire** takes *-c-* in the singular and in *dicono*: *dico, dici, dice, dicono*, but *diciamo, dite*.
- **uscire** adds *-sc-* everywhere except *noi* and *voi*: *esco, esci, esce, escono*, but *usciamo, uscite*.
- **bere** is the strangest one. Its endings come from *-ere* (*beviamo, bevete*), but every other form has a *-v-*: *bevo, bevi, beve, bevono*.

### Where *stare* matters most

*Stare* is not only "to stay." It is also how you say how you are, and where you are.

- **Come stai?** — *How are you?* **Come sta?** — *How are you?* (to someone you address with *Lei*).
- **Sto bene.** — *I'm well.* **Sto male.** — *I'm not well.*
- **Stare a casa** — *to stay at home*: **Stasera sto a casa.**
- **Stare zitto** — *to be quiet*: **Stai zitto un momento.**
- **Stare** with *d'accordo* — **Sto d'accordo** — and simply **Va bene**, **D'accordo**, are the everyday ways to agree.

### The little phrases built on these verbs

- **dire di sì / dire di no** — *to say yes / to say no*: **Io dico di sì.**
- **dire la verità** — *to tell the truth*.
- **dare il permesso** — *to give permission*.
- **dare fiducia a qualcuno** — *to give someone trust*.
- **uscire con** — *to go out with*: **Esco con Matteo.**
- **uscire il sabato sera** — *to go out on Saturday night*.
- **bevo un caffè**, **beviamo una birra**.

## From the story

[[Che cosa scrivi, Leo?]]
— *dire* in a question, with the *tu* form *scrivi* beside it; *dici* works the same way.

[[Io dico una cosa.]]
— *dire* in the *io* form: *dico*, not *dicio*.

[[Tu dici sempre di sì.]]
— *tu* form of *dire*, plus the idiom **dire di sì**.

[[Io le dico la verità.]]
— *dire* with an indirect idea ("to her"): the truth is what is said.

[[Emma, una domanda.]]
— a fragment, but the whole scene turns on *dico, dici, dicono*: who says what.

[[Io bevo il cappuccino sempre.]]
— *bere* in the *io* form: *bevo*, with a *-v-* just like *beviamo*.

[[Voglio bere il cappuccino con la pizza.]]
— after a modal verb (*volere*) the plain infinitive *bere* follows: *voglio bere*, *devo bere*.

[[Come stai?]] — while the text uses *Sto bene* and *Come stai* in the same shape:
[[«Io sto bene con te. Anche tu stai bene?»]]
— *stare* twice, first *io*, then *tu*; the vowel *-a-* stays throughout *stare*.

[[Nonno dà sempre fiducia ai ragazzi.]]
— *dare* in the third person: *dà*, with the accent.

[[Allora esci.]]
— *uscire* in the *tu* form: *esci*. The **noi** form *usciamo* means "let's go out."

## Common mistakes

1. **Using *essere* where Italian uses *stare*.** English "I am well" is not *sono bene* but **sto bene**. Same for **sto a casa**, "I'm at home."
2. **Forgetting the accent in *dà*.** Write *dà* (he/she gives), not *da* (from). *Mio padre dà sempre la focaccia* is right.
3. **Making *dire* regular.** Not *dio*, not *dici* with a different vowel — the correct forms are **dico, dici, dice**.
4. **Adding *-sc-* where it doesn't belong in *uscire*.** It is *usciamo* and *uscite*, never *usciamo* with a *-sc-*.
5. **Making *bere* regular.** Not *bero*, not *biamo*; the forms are **bevo, bevi, beve, beviamo, bevete, bevono**.
6. **Mixing up *uscire* and *andare***. *Andare* is "to go"; *uscire* is "to go out," to leave the house for fun. **Stasera esco** — not *stasera vado* on its own.
