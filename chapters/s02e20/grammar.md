# Verbs + *a* or *di* + infinitive

## What it is

In English you say "I **start** *to work*", "I **decide** *to stay*", "I **try** *to help*". Two verbs, one after the other, with **to** in the middle.

Italian does the same thing, but it has **two** little words for that "to": **a** and **di**.

- Kevin **comincia a** lavorare. — Kevin **starts** working.
- Kevin **decide di** aspettare. — Kevin **decides** to wait.

The second verb stays in the infinitive (*lavorare*, *aspettare*, *capire*). Nothing else changes. What you have to learn is which verb wants **a** and which verb wants **di**. There is no rule of logic — it is simply a habit of the language, so it is best learned verb by verb, the way you learned *casa* and *tavolo*.

## How it works

The pattern is always:

**verb 1 + a/di + verb 2 (infinitive)**

| Verb | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| cominciare | **a** | **Comincio a** capire. — I'm starting to understand. |
| continuare | **a** | **Continuo a** provare. — I keep trying. |
| imparare | **a** | Ho **imparato a** ballare. — I learned to dance. |
| aiutare | **a** | Ti **aiuto a** portare le casse. — I'll help you carry the speakers. |
| finire | **di** | **Finisco di** scrivere. — I'll finish writing. |
| decidere | **di** | **Decido di** farli a mano. — I decide to make them by hand. |
| cercare | **di** | **Cerco di** non litigare. — I try not to fight. |
| provare | **a** | **Provo a** pensare. — I'll try to think. |
| smettere | **di** | **Smetto di** parlare. — I stop talking. |
| ricordarsi | **di** | **Ricordati di** prendere il nastro. — Remember to take the tape. |

Three things are worth noticing.

**1. Only the first verb is conjugated.** The second one never changes: *Comincio a capire* (I start to understand), *Cominci a capire* (you start to understand), *Cominciamo a capire* (we start to understand). The infinitive stays put.

**2. Some verbs can take both — with a different meaning.** *Comincio a lavorare* = I start working. *Comincio a capire* = I'm beginning to understand. But *finisco di lavorare* = I finish working; the **di** here means the action is over, not started.

**3. Pronoun and reflexive verbs.** *Ricordarsi* becomes *ricordati* (remember!) or *mi ricordo di* (I remember). The *di* stays: *Mi ricordo di aver chiuso la porta.*

Notice also the difference from English in one common case: *aiutare* takes **a**, not a bare infinitive. English says "help me carry", Italian says **aiutami a** portare.

## From the story

«Roberto, tu **organizzi** la festa ogni anno. Perché parliamo solo oggi?» — “Roberto, you **organize** the festival every year. Why are we only talking today?” (`s02e20-1-014`)
— *organizzare* here is a normal verb, but notice the pattern it will enter later: **comincio a organizzare**.

«Abbiamo sei **stand**. Il Comitato ha **deciso di** fare così.» — “We have six **stands**. The committee **decided** to do it this way.” (`s02e20-2-007`)
— **decidere di** + infinitive: "has decided **to** do it this way".

«**Continuo a** provare.» — “I **keep** trying.” (`s02e20-2-010`)
— **continuare a**: the action goes on and on.

«Allora **decido di** farli a mano.» — “Then I **decide** to make them by hand.” (`s02e20-4-080`)
— **decidere di** again, with a pronoun object (*li*) glued to the infinitive: *farli*.

«Le ho messe nel furgone ieri sera. **Ricordo di** aver chiuso la porta.» — “I put them in the van last night. I **remember** closing the door.” (`s02e20-4-013`)
— **ricordarsi di** + infinitive: "I remember having closed the door." *Aver chiuso* is a past infinitive, but the *di* works exactly the same way.

«Kevin, **finisci di** scrivere e poi **comincio a** contare.» — “Kevin, **finish** writing and then I'll **start** counting.” (`s02e20-4-112`)
— Two verbs in one line, one with **di** and one with **a**: *finisci di scrivere*, *comincio a contare*.

«No, **finisci di** aiutare Roberto. Io aspetto.» — “No, **finish** helping Roberto. I'll wait.” (`s02e20-6-030`)
— *finire di* + *aiutare*: two infinitives, but only the first verb is conjugated.

Franco e Lucia **cominciano a** ballare sul **palco**. — Franco and Lucia **start** dancing on the **stage**. (`s02e20-5-084`)
— **cominciare a** with a plural subject: same pattern, no change.

«Ho **provato a** fare una cosa bella.» — “I **tried** to do something beautiful.” (`s02e20-5-074`)
— **provare a** in the past: the *a* never disappears.

«Non **smetto di** pensare al **premio**.» — “I don't **stop** thinking about the **prize**.” (`s02e20-3-077`)
— **smettere di** with a double negative: *non... smetto*.

«Nessuno **smette**. Ma la festa è domani.» — “Nobody **stops**. But the festival is tomorrow.” (`s02e20-3-057`)
— *smettere* used alone, without a second verb. You can always use these verbs by themselves: *smetto*, *finisco*, *comincio*.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

**1. Using the wrong preposition.** English speakers say *~~comincio di~~ lavorare* or *~~decido a~~ partire*. There is no feeling that tells you which is right; you have to memorize the pair. Tips: verbs of **starting, keeping on, learning, trying, helping** usually take **a**. Verbs of **finishing, deciding, stopping, remembering** usually take **di**.

**2. Conjugating the second verb.** *Smetto di parlo* is wrong. Only the first verb is conjugated: *Smetto di parlare*.

**3. Dropping the preposition.** English "I want to go" works without "to" in some languages, but Italian almost always keeps *a* or *di*. *Finisco scrivere* is wrong; *finisco di scrivere* is right.

**4. Translating the wrong "to".** *Aiuto a portare* — not *~~aiuto portare~~*. If you remember only one pair, remember *aiutare a*.

**5. Confusing *a* the preposition with the verb *avere*.** *Ho cominciato a* is correct: past participle + *a* + infinitive. *Ho cominciato avere* is not.

**6. Forgetting the pronouns.** With *aiutare* the person you help goes before the verb: **ti** aiuto a portare. Not *aiuto te a portare* in normal speech.

## Practice

Complete each sentence with **a** or **di**, then check your answers.

1. Comincio ___ capire Roberto.
2. Abbiamo deciso ___ aspettare.
3. Cerco ___ non pensare alla festa.
4. Leo ha imparato ___ contare le sedie.
5. Nadia ha finito ___ tagliare i cartoncini.
6. Tu mi aiuti ___ portare il microfono?
7. Ricordati ___ prendere la prolunga!
8. Ho smesso ___ lavorare alle undici.

**Answers**

1. **a** — *cominciare a*
2. **di** — *decidere di*
3. **di** — *cercare di*
4. **a** — *imparare a*
5. **di** — *finire di*
6. **a** — *aiutare a*
7. **di** — *ricordarsi di*
8. **di** — *smettere di* (note the past: *ho smesso di lavorare*)
