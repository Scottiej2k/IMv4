# Past time expressions: *già*, *appena*, *ancora*, *fa*, *scorso*, *sempre*, *mai*

## What they are

These little words tell you **when** something happened — or whether it has happened at all. In Italian they work together with the *passato prossimo* (the "have done" tense you already know), and they answer questions like:

- *Have you already eaten?* → **già**
- *Have you eaten yet?* → **ancora**
- *I've just eaten.* → **appena**
- *I ate two hours ago.* → **fa**
- *I've never eaten that.* → **mai**

The good news: you don't need a new verb tense for any of this. You only need to put the right small word in the right place.

## How it works

### 1. Position with compound tenses

With *avere* or *essere* + participle, **già, appena, ancora, sempre, mai** almost always go **between the auxiliary and the participle**.

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Ho **già** preparato tutto. | I've **already** prepared everything. |
| Ho **appena** finito. | I've **just** finished. |
| Non ho **ancora** mangiato. | I haven't eaten **yet**. |
| Non ho **mai** visto il mare. | I've **never** seen the sea. |
| Ho **sempre** portato io il cestino. | I've **always** brought the basket. |

Notice the negative patterns:
- **non … ancora** = not yet → *Non ho ancora firmato il modulo.*
- **non … mai** = never → *Non ho mai capito le bocce.*

In both cases *non* comes before the auxiliary, and the adverb sits after it.

### 2. *fa* and *scorso*

**fa** means "ago" and goes **after** the time expression: *due giorni **fa***, *un anno **fa***, *dieci minuti **fa***. It never goes before.

**scorso** means "last" and goes **after** the noun, agreeing with it like any adjective: *l'anno **scorso***, *la settimana **scorsa***, *l'estate **scorsa***, *il mese **scorso***.

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Sono arrivati tre settimane **fa**. | They arrived three weeks **ago**. |
| **L'anno scorso** siamo andati a Monza. | **Last year** we went to Monza. |
| **La settimana scorsa** ha piovuto. | **Last week** it rained. |

### 3. Meaning check

- **già** = already (it happened sooner than expected)
- **appena** = just, a moment ago
- **ancora** (in *non … ancora*) = yet; on its own it means "still, again"
- **sempre** = always
- **mai** = never, ever (needs *non* in front of the verb unless it's a question)

## From the story

Ha **già** nascosto cinque **uova di cioccolato** in giardino, dietro il muro vecchio. — He has **already** hidden five **chocolate eggs** in the garden, behind the old wall. (`s02e05-1-004`) — *già* sits between the auxiliary *ha* and the participle *nascosto*.

«Devo dirti una cosa,» dice. «Io **non** ho **ancora** comprato la **colomba**.» — “I have to tell you something,” she says. “I **haven't** bought the **colomba** **yet**.” (`s02e05-1-013`) — the classic "not yet": *non* + auxiliary + *ancora* + participle.

«Ho **già** speso troppo,» dice Matteo. «Questo lo so. Ma domani è **Pasquetta** e non parliamo di soldi.» — “I've **already** spent too much,” Matteo says. “That I know. But tomorrow is **Easter Monday** and we're not talking about money.” (`s02e05-2-045`) — a short, complete sentence where *già* changes the meaning to "sooner than I should have."

«Ho **appena** capito le regole!» dice Kevin. «Credo.» — “I've **just** understood the rules!” Kevin says. “I think.” (`s02e05-5-082`) — *appena* means it happened a few seconds ago.

«L'ultima volta è stata dieci anni **fa**,» dice Ornella. «E hai vinto perché io ho sbagliato di proposito.» — “Last time was ten years **ago**,” Ornella says. “And you won because I missed on purpose.” (`s02e05-5-010`) — *fa* comes after the time expression, never before it.

«Matteo,» dice Samira. «Tu hai **sempre** fame.» — “Matteo,” Samira says. “You're **always** hungry.” (`s02e05-5-048`) — same position, here in the present tense: the adverb doesn't move.

«Quindi il **picnic** è finito **appena** ha cominciato,» dice Leo. — “So the picnic ended **just** as it started,” Leo says. (`s02e05-7-015`) — *appena* with *essere* + participle, in a joke about time passing too fast.

«Non ha mai funzionato,» dice. — “It has never worked,” Chiara says. (`s02e05-3-038`) — *non* before the verb, *mai* after the auxiliary: "it has never worked."

## Common mistakes

- **Putting *già* at the end.** English says "I've eaten already"; Italian prefers *Ho **già** mangiato*, not *Ho mangiato già*.
- **Forgetting *non* with *mai*.** *Ho mai visto* is wrong in a statement. Say *Non ho mai visto*.
- **Confusing *ancora* and *già*.** *Ho ancora mangiato* means "I've eaten again," not "I've already eaten."
- ***fa* before the time.** Say *due anni **fa***, not *fa due anni*.
- ***scorso* before the noun.** Say *l'anno **scorso***, not *lo scorso anno* — this is the natural everyday order.
- **Splitting *non* and *ancora*.** They belong on either side of the auxiliary: *Non ho **ancora** capito*.

## Practice

Put the words in the right place, or choose the correct form.

1. Ho / già / mangiato. → Make a sentence.
2. Non / ancora / ho / firmato il modulo. → Make a sentence.
3. Ho appena (finire) i compiti.
4. L'anno (scorso) siamo andati al lago.
5. Sono arrivati due ore (fa / già).
6. Non ho (mai / sempre) visto una barca così piccola.
7. Io (sempre) bevo dalla borraccia. → Put the adverb correctly.
8. Translate: "We haven't eaten yet."

### Answer key

1. **Ho già mangiato.**
2. **Non ho ancora firmato il modulo.**
3. **Ho appena finito i compiti.**
4. **L'anno scorso siamo andati al lago.**
5. **Sono arrivati due ore fa.**
6. **Non ho mai visto una barca così piccola.**
7. **Io bevo sempre dalla borraccia.** (also correct: *Io ho sempre bevuto dalla borraccia*, if you mean "I've always drunk…")
8. **Non abbiamo ancora mangiato.**
