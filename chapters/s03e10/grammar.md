# The Formal Imperative (*Lei*)

## What it is

In Italian, when you talk to someone you don't know — a waiter, a shopkeeper, a hotel receptionist, an official — you use **Lei**, the formal "you". Like all *Lei* forms, the verb goes in the **third person singular**, not the second:

- Informal: *tu prendi* → «Prendi il menù.»
- Formal: *Lei prende* → «**Prenda** il menù.»

The **formal imperative** is the command or request form of this polite *Lei*. It's the language of restaurants, hotels and services: *Mi dica*, *Si accomodi*, *Prenda*, *Scusi*. In this chapter Kevin hears it all night long and translates it word by word for his parents.

## How it works

The formal imperative is completely different from the *tu* imperative, but very close to the ordinary *Lei* present tense. The endings are:

| Verb type | *tu* imperative | *Lei* imperative | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **-are** (parlare) | parla | **parli** | «**Parli** piano, per favore.» |
| **-ere** (prendere) | prendi | **prenda** | «**Prenda** il menù.» |
| **-ire** (aprire) | apri | **apra** | «**Apra** la finestra.» |
| **-ire with -isc-** (finire) | finisci | **finisca** | «**Finisca** il caffè.» |

So the pattern is the opposite of what English speakers expect: **-are** verbs end in **-i**, while **-ere** and **-ire** verbs end in **-a**.

Some very common verbs are irregular:

| Verb | *Lei* imperative |
|---|---|
| essere | **sia** |
| avere | **abbia** |
| sapere | **sappia** |
| andare | **vada** |
| venire | **venga** |
| fare | **faccia** |
| dire | **dica** |
| dare | **dia** |
| stare | **stia** |

### Pronouns go *before* the formal imperative

This is the rule that surprises learners most. With *tu*, pronouns attach to the end of the verb (*accomodati*, *dammi*, *scusami*). With *Lei*, they move **in front**:

| Informal (*tu*) | Formal (*Lei*) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| accomodati | **si accomodi** | have a seat |
| scusami | **mi scusi** | excuse me |
| dimmi | **mi dica** | tell me / how can I help you |
| prendilo | **lo prenda** | take it |
| portami il conto | **mi porti il conto** | bring me the bill |

### Negative

The negative formal imperative is simply **non** + the same form. (The negative *tu* imperative, by contrast, uses the infinitive: *non prendere*.)

- «**Non prenda** quello, signore.»
- «**Non si accomodi** lì, c'è corrente.»

## From the story

- «Buonasera,» dice il cameriere. «**Mi dica**.» — “Good evening,” the waiter says. “**How can I help you**.” (`s03e10-3-011`) — The waiter's opening: the classic formal invitation to state what you need, with the pronoun *mi* in front.
- «Buongiorno,» dice Lucia, con voce gentile e formale. «**Si accomodi**, signora.» — “Good morning,” Lucia says, with a kind and formal voice. “**Please come in**, ma'am.” (`s03e10-5-008`) — Lucia invites Linda in. The reflexive pronoun *si* comes before the verb, not attached to it.
- «**Prenda**!» dice Kevin, tutto contento. «**Scelga**! Le parole di ieri!» — “**Take**!” Kevin says, delighted. “**Choose**! Yesterday's words!” (`s03e10-3-027`) — Kevin repeating the waiter's verb, happy to have recognized it.
- «Qui no,» dice Matteo. «Ma **mi dica** lei, signore.» — “Not here,” Matteo says. “But **how can I help you**, sir.” (`s03e10-6-027`) — Matteo, at the bar, copies the formal register; a stressed *lei* can be added for emphasis.
- «Vuole uno **scontrino**?» chiede il cameriere. — “Would you like a **receipt**?” the waiter asks. (`s03e10-4-110`) — Not an imperative, but the same polite system: the *Lei* verb in a question.
- «Ottimo italiano,» dice il cameriere. «Bravo. Allora, che cosa **ordina**?» — “Excellent Italian,” the waiter says. “Well done. So, what are you **ordering**?” (`s03e10-3-103`) — The waiter uses the third person singular as "you": *ordina*, never *ordini tu*.
- «Sì. Lei ha lasciato troppo,» dice il cameriere. «Il **servizio** è **compreso**.» — “Yes. You left too much,” the waiter says. “The **service** is **included**.” (`s03e10-4-106`) — The polite service register in one line; formal language isn't only imperatives.
- «**Mi scusi**, signore,» dice a Doug. «Forse c'è un errore.» — “**Excuse me**, sir,” he says to Doug. “Perhaps there's a mistake.” (`s03e10-4-104`) — A very Italian way of correcting a customer politely: impersonal, soft, never *hai sbagliato*.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Using the *tu* imperative by accident.** «Prendi il menù» to a waiter sounds like you're talking to a child or a friend. Use **prenda**.
2. **Putting the pronoun after the verb.** *Dica mi* and *accomodisi* don't exist. Say **mi dica**, **si accomodi**.
3. **Mixing *Lei* with second-person verbs.** *Lei prendi* is wrong. *Lei* always takes the third person singular: **Lei prende**, **Lei prende?**, **Prenda!**
4. **Thinking the *-are* form ends in *-a*.** In the formal imperative, **-are** verbs end in **-i**: *scusare* → **scusi**, *aspettare* → **aspetti**.
5. **Forgetting the negative form.** For *Lei* it's **non prenda**, not *non prendere* — that's the informal negative.
6. **Over-using *Lei* with family and friends.** With Doug and Franco, Kevin hears *prendi*: they are friends. With the waiter, everything is *Lei*. The choice of *Lei* or *tu* is itself a piece of information about the relationship.

## Practice

Rewrite each sentence in the formal imperative (*Lei*).

1. *(tu)* Prendi il menù. → *(Lei)* ____
2. *(tu)* Scusami, dov'è il bagno? → *(Lei)* ____
3. *(tu)* Accomodati vicino alla finestra. → *(Lei)* ____
4. *(tu)* Dimmi che cosa vuoi. → *(Lei)* ____
5. *(tu)* Aspetta un momento, per favore. → *(Lei)* ____
6. *(tu)* Non prendere il pesce. → *(Lei)* ____
7. *(tu)* Portami il conto. → *(Lei)* ____
8. Choose the right form: «Buonasera, signora. *(Prendi / Prenda)* questo posto?»

### Answer key

1. **Prenda** il menù.
2. **Mi scusi**, dov'è il bagno?
3. **Si accomodi** vicino alla finestra.
4. **Mi dica** che cosa vuole. (Note: *vuole*, not *vuoi*.)
5. **Aspetti** un momento, per favore.
6. **Non prenda** il pesce.
7. **Mi porti** il conto.
8. **Prenda** — with a *signora* you don't know, the formal form is the polite choice.
