# The Imperfetto for Habits and Repeated Actions

## What it is

The **imperfetto** is the Italian past tense for things you *used to* do. Not one single event, but something that happened again and again: every summer, every morning, always, usually.

In English you say *when I was little I used to learn poems by heart* or *every summer we went to the sea*. In Italian, both halves of that idea take the imperfetto:

- **Ogni estate andavamo al mare.** — Every summer we went to the sea.
- **Da bambino imparavo le tabelline a memoria.** — As a child I used to learn my times tables by heart.

This chapter of *Via dei Tigli* is full of it, because Chiara and Matteo are arguing about what their childhood summers were like — and everything they describe happened *every year*, not once.

## How it works

Take the **io** form of the present, drop the ending, and add the imperfetto endings.

| | parlare | credere | dormire | finire |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| io | parlavo | credevo | dormivo | finivo |
| tu | parlavi | credevi | dormivi | finivi |
| lui/lei | parlava | credeva | dormiva | finiva |
| noi | parlavamo | credevamo | dormivamo | finivamo |
| voi | parlavate | credevate | dormivate | finivate |
| loro | parlavano | credevano | dormivano | finivano |

Notice the pattern: **-avo, -evo, -ivo** for *io*, and the same endings for all three groups (*-avo / -evo / -ivo*, then *-avi, -ava, -avamo, -avate, -avano*).

**Essere** is the one to memorize: *ero, eri, era, eravamo, eravate, erano*.
**Fare** is irregular: *facevo, facevi, faceva, facevamo, facevate, facevano*.
**Avere** is regular: *avevo, avevi, aveva, avevamo, avevate, avevano*.

### The words that go with it

These adverbs and expressions almost always bring the imperfetto with them:

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| **ogni estate / ogni giorno / ogni anno** | every summer / every day / every year |
| **di solito** | usually |
| **sempre** | always |
| **spesso** | often |
| **a volte** | sometimes |
| **da bambino / da piccola** | as a child |

### Habits then, habits now

The imperfetto contrasts with what you do *now*. To talk about a present habit, use the present tense:

- **Prima facevo il bagno in un lago. Adesso faccio il bagno al lago.** — Before, I used to swim in a lake. Now I swim in the lake.
- **Mio nonno imparava a memoria le tabelline. Io non imparo niente a memoria.** — My grandfather used to learn his times tables by heart. I don't learn anything by heart.

## From the story

«Ogni estate, in macchina, verso la Liguria, io e Matteo dovevamo imparare a memoria una poesia.» — “Every summer, in the car, toward Liguria, Matteo and I had to learn a poem by heart.” (`s03e03-1-034`) — *ogni estate* + imperfetto: a habit repeated for years.
«Quasi tutti» dice Lucia. «Qualcuno, **ogni estate**, tornava a settembre e dimenticava tutto.» — “Almost all of them,” says Lucia. “Someone, **every summer**, came back in September and forgot everything.” (`s03e03-1-025`) — the same pattern, with a third-person subject.
«Da bambino ci **andavo** **ogni estate** con mia madre» dice Franco, all'improvviso. — “As a boy I **used to go** there **every summer** with my mother,” says Franco, suddenly. (`s03e03-1-066`) — *da bambino* + *ogni estate* + *andavo*: the classic "used to" sentence.
«Ogni estate portavamo un po' di sabbia a casa» dice Chiara. — “Every summer we brought a little sand home,” says Chiara. (`s03e03-4-066`) — *portavamo*: we did this every single year.
«**Facevamo il bagno** alle sei del mattino» dice. «Prima di colazione.» — “**We went in the water** at six in the morning,” she says. “Before breakfast.” (`s03e03-2-049`) — Nadia's Tunisian summers: a repeated habit, not one swim.
«Io **ogni estate**, da bambino, mettevo le conchiglie nello **zaino**» dice Kevin. — “I, **every summer**, as a boy, put the shells in my **backpack**,” says Kevin. (`s03e03-2-077`) — Kevin, in his Italian, uses the imperfetto correctly here.
«**Ogni estate**, l'ultimo giorno, vostra madre piangeva» dice Franco. — “**Every summer**, on the last day, your mother cried,” says Franco. (`s03e03-5-064`) — Anna's yearly tears: habit and emotion together.
«Sempre in **agosto**, però» dice Matteo. — “Always in **August**, though,” says Matteo. (`s03e03-5-071`) — *sempre* is a signal word: expect the imperfetto nearby.
«**Di solito** è la parola più onesta della lingua» dice Franco. — “**Usually** is the most honest word in the language,” says Franco. (`s03e03-7-026`) — *di solito* on its own; Franco's answer means "usually I did."
«Le ho **imparate a memoria** a scuola» dice Franco. «E le ho ancora in testa.» — “I **learned them by heart** at school,” says Franco. “And I still have them in my head.” (`s03e03-7-018`) — careful: this is one completed action, so it takes the passato prossimo, not the imperfetto.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Using the imperfetto for one single event.** *Ieri imparavo la poesia* is wrong: yesterday you learned it once, so use the passato prossimo — *Ieri ho imparato la poesia*.
2. **Forgetting the accent... and the double ending.** *Parlavo* is *io*; *parlavo* and *parlava* differ by one letter. Watch *io parlavo* vs *lui parlava*.
3. **Saying *ero andato*.** That's the trapassato prossimo. For habits you just need *ero*: *Da bambino ero piccolo e magro.*
4. **Mixing up *di solito* and *solitamente*.** Both mean "usually" and both take the imperfetto for past habits.
5. **Translating "used to" with *usavo a*.** There is no Italian equivalent. Use the imperfetto alone: *Andavo al mare* = "I used to go to the sea."

## Practice

1. Complete with the imperfetto: *Ogni estate noi ______ (andare) in **pensione** a Levanto.*
2. Complete: *Da bambino mio padre ______ (imparare) le **tabelline** a memoria.*
3. Complete: *Tu, quando eri piccolo, ______ (obbedire) sempre?*
4. Complete: *Noi ______ (fare) il bagno due volte al giorno.*
5. Transform into a present habit: *Ogni estate facevo il bagno in un lago.*
6. Choose: imperfetto or passato prossimo? *Ieri ______ (io / perdere) la chiave in **cortile**.*
7. Add the right adverb: *______ estate portavamo la **sabbia** a casa.* (every)
8. Translate: "As a child I used to learn a poem by heart every month."

1. andavamo
2. imparava
3. obbedivi
4. facevamo
5. Ogni estate faccio il bagno in un lago. (also: *al lago*)
