# The conditional for politeness and wishes

## What it is

The conditional is the "would" form of a verb. In Italian it does more work than in English, because it is the normal way to be polite. When you say *vorrei* instead of *voglio*, you change "I want" into "I'd like". When you say *potresti* instead of *puoi*, you change "can you" into "could you". Italians use these forms all day long: in shops, in bars, at the table, and inside families, where asking nicely is simply good manners.

It also expresses a wish or a dream: *Mi piacerebbe imparare le bocce* — "I'd like to learn bocce."

At this level you only need a small set of forms by heart. But the endings are regular, and once you know them you can build many more.

## How it works

Take the infinitive, drop the final **-e**, and add the conditional endings:

| | **-are** (parlare) | **-ere** (prendere) | **-ire** (dormire) |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | parl**erei** | prend**erei** | dorm**irei** |
| tu | parl**eresti** | prend**eresti** | dorm**iresti** |
| lui / lei | parl**erebbe** | prend**erebbe** | dorm**irebbe** |
| noi | parl**eremmo** | prend**eremmo** | dorm**iremmo** |
| voi | parl**ereste** | prend**ereste** | dorm**ireste** |
| loro | parl**erebbero** | prend**erebbero** | dorm**irebbero** |

Notice that **-are** and **-ere** verbs share the same endings (the ones with an *e*): *parlerei*, *prenderei*.

Then there are the forms you will use every single day. Learn them as whole words, like vocabulary:

| verb | form | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| volere | **vorrei** | I would like |
| potere | **potrei / potresti** | I could / could you |
| dovere | **dovrei / dovresti** | I should / you should |
| essere | **sarebbe** | it would be |
| piacere | **mi piacerebbe** | I would like (it would please me) |

## Politeness and wishes

**Polite requests.** Use *potresti* with people you call *tu*, and *potrebbe* with people you call *Lei*:

- *Potresti aiutarmi?* — Could you help me?
- *Potrei aiutarvi?* — May I help you?
- *Vorrei un caffè, per favore.* — I'd like a coffee, please.

**Gentle advice.** *Dovresti* does not push; it suggests. It leaves the other person free:

- *Dovresti provare questo.* — You should try this one.
- *Dovresti parlare con papà.* — You should talk to Dad.

**Suggestions.** *Sarebbe* introduces an idea softly, like an opinion instead of an order:

- *Sarebbe la scelta giusta.* — That would be the right choice.
- *Sarebbe meglio aspettare.* — It would be better to wait.

**Wishes.** *Vorrei* and *mi piacerebbe* say what you want without demanding it:

- *Vorrei solo sedermi un momento.* — I'd just like to sit down for a moment.
- *Mi piacerebbe capire la mira.* — I'd like to understand the aim.

*Leo, who uses **Lei** with Ornella, still uses **mi piacerebbe**.*

## Common mistakes

- **Using the present tense when you want to be polite.** *Voglio un caffè* is not wrong, but in a shop it sounds blunt. Say *Vorrei un caffè*.
- **Mixing up *potrei* and *potresti*.** *Potrei* is about me (*Potrei aiutarvi?*), *potresti* is about you (*Potresti aiutarmi?*).
- **Thinking *mi piacerebbe* agrees with the person.** It agrees with the thing: *Mi piacerebbe una culla* (one thing), *Mi piacerebbero due culle* (two things).
- **Using the conditional to give orders.** *Dovresti* is advice. If you want to tell someone what to do, use the imperative you studied earlier: *Ascolta Nadia!*
- **Forgetting the *tu* / *Lei* difference.** With a shopkeeper or a stranger, use *potrebbe*, not *potresti*.

## Practice

Put the verb in the conditional.

1. (volere) ______ un bicchiere d'acqua, per favore.
2. (potere) ______ aiutarmi con questa scatola? — asking a friend.
3. (dovere) ______ parlare con tuo padre.
4. (essere) ______ meglio andare adesso.
5. (potere) ______ aiutarvi? — the shop assistant, politely.
6. (piacere) Mi ______ imparare le bocce.
7. (volere) ______ solo sedermi un momento.
8. (dovere) Franco, ______ venire anche tu.

**Answers**

1. **Vorrei** un bicchiere d'acqua, per favore.
2. **Potresti** aiutarmi con questa scatola?
3. **Dovresti** parlare con tuo padre.
4. **Sarebbe** meglio andare adesso.
5. **Potrei** aiutarvi?
6. Mi **piacerebbe** imparare le bocce.
7. **Vorrei** solo sedermi un momento.
8. Franco, **dovresti** venire anche tu.
