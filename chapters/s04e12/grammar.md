# Infinitive constructions: *prima di*, *dopo aver*, *senza*, *invece di*

## What it is

In Italian, a lot of everyday sentences link two actions together. Instead of saying *I taste the sauce. Then I add salt.*, you can say *Before adding salt, I taste the sauce.* The little words that do this job — **prima di**, **dopo**, **senza**, **invece di** — are almost always followed by a **verb in the infinitive** (the *-are / -ere / -ire* form), never by a conjugated verb.

This matters because English uses *-ing* or a full clause here: *before going*, *after eating*, *without saying*, *instead of cutting*. Italian uses the infinitive: *prima di andare*, *dopo aver mangiato*, *senza dire*, *invece di tagliare*.

The four patterns:

| Italian | English | Verb form after it |
|---|---|---|
| **prima di** + infinitive | before doing something | *prima di andare* |
| **dopo** + past infinitive | after having done something | *dopo aver mangiato* |
| **senza** + infinitive | without doing something | *senza dire* |
| **invece di** + infinitive | instead of doing something | *invece di tagliare* |

## How it works

### 1. *Prima di* + infinitive

The subject of both actions is normally the same person.

- *Prima di **cominciare**, leggo le regole.* — Before starting, I read the rules.
- *Prima di **mettere** il sale, assaggiate.* — Before putting in the salt, taste it.

If the subject is different, use *prima che* + subjunctive (a later lesson) or a full sentence with *prima*: *Prima assaggio io, poi assaggi tu.*

Note: **prima di** + infinitive always has **di**. Beginners often drop it: *~~prima mangiare~~* is wrong.

### 2. *Dopo* + past infinitive (*infinito passato*)

*Dopo* is followed by **aver** or **essere** + past participle. This is how Italian says "after having done X".

- *Dopo **aver** mangiato, andiamo a casa.* — After eating, we go home.
- *Dopo **aver** messo l'acqua, si comincia dal centro.* — After adding the water, you start from the center.
- *Dopo **averlo** usato, lo lavo.* — After using it, I wash it. (The pronoun attaches to *avere*: *averlo, averla, averli*.)
- *Dopo **essere** arrivati, ci si lava le mani.* — After arriving, we wash our hands.

Two rules to remember:

1. **aver** with most verbs (*dopo aver cucinato*); **essere** with movement and reflexive verbs (*dopo essere arrivato / arrivati*).
2. The past participle with *essere* agrees with the subject: *dopo essere arrivat**a*** (a woman), *dopo essere arrivat**i*** (mixed or all male).

The short form **dopo mangiato**, **dopo cena** exists in speech but *dopo aver mangiato* is the standard you should write.

### 3. *Senza* + infinitive

- *Esce **senza** dire altro.* — He goes out without saying anything else.
- ***Senza** perdere tempo, la lezione è lunga.* — Without wasting time, the lesson is long.
- *Gli ho lasciato un grembiule sulla sedia, **senza** dirgli niente.* — I left him an apron on the chair, without telling him anything.

Pronouns attach the same way: *senza dir**gli*** (without telling him), *senza veder**lo*** (without seeing it).

Watch out: when *senza* is followed by a **noun**, no infinitive is involved: *senza sale*, *senza grembiule*, *senza di te*.

### 4. *Invece di* + infinitive

- ***Invece di** tagliare la cipolla grande, tagliatela fine.* — Instead of cutting the onion big, cut it fine.
- ***Invece di** tagliarla fine, mia nonna la tagliava grande.* — Instead of cutting it fine, my grandmother cut it big.

*Invece di* = "instead of". *Invece* alone (no *di*) means "on the other hand / whereas": *Io bevo caffè; tu, invece, bevi tè.*

## From the story

- «Sì. **Prima di** cominciare, leggo le regole agli allievi.» — “Yes. **Before** we start, I read the rules to the students.” (`s04e12-1-029`) — *prima di* + infinitive, with no subject change: the same person starts and reads.
- «Prima di mettere il **sale**, assaggiate. Sempre,» dice. «Non fidatevi del quaderno.» — “**Before** adding the **salt**, taste it. Always,” Kevin says. “Don't trust the notebook.” (`s04e12-3-060`) — an instruction: taste first, salt afterwards.
- «Guardate: **dopo aver** messo l'acqua, si comincia dal centro,» dice. — “Watch: **after** adding the water, you start from the center,” Kevin says. (`s04e12-4-011`) — *dopo* + *aver* + participle. Notice *aver*, not *avere*, before the participle.
- «Sentite? Questo è il suono giusto. **Dopo aver** soffritto la cipolla, si sente subito.» — “Do you hear it? That's the right sound. **After** sautéing the onion, you hear it at once.” (`s04e12-2-092`) — the same pattern with a different verb.
- «Allora, continuiamo?» dice ad alta voce. «**Senza** perdere tempo: la lezione è lunga.» — “So, shall we go on?” Kevin says out loud. “**Without** wasting time: the class is long.” (`s04e12-2-107`) — *senza* + infinitive after a colon; Italian never says *senza perdendo*.
- «Non lo so,» dice Kevin. «Gli ho lasciato un grembiule sulla sedia, **senza** dirgli niente.» — “I don't know,” Kevin says. “I left him an apron on the chair, **without** telling him anything.” (`s04e12-6-032`) — *senza* + infinitive with an attached pronoun (*dirgli*).
- «**Invece di** tagliare la **cipolla** grande, tagliatela fine,» dice Kevin. — “**Instead of** cutting the **onion** big, cut it fine,” Kevin says. (`s04e12-3-052`) — *invece di* + infinitive, followed by an imperative.
- «**Invece di** tagliarla fine, mia nonna la tagliava grande,» dice Matteo. — “**Instead of** cutting it fine, my grandmother cut it big,” Matteo says. (`s04e12-1-052`) — with the pronoun *la* attached to *tagliare*.
- «Sì, Franco,» dice Kevin. «Dopo averlo usato, lo lavo.» — “Yes, Franco,” Kevin says. “**After** using it, I wash it.” (`s04e12-5-115`) — *averlo*: pronoun inside the past infinitive.
- «Le ricette vere cambiano sempre,» dice Kevin. «**Prima di** capirlo, ci ho messo dieci anni.» — “Real recipes always change,” Kevin says. “**Before** I understood that, it took me ten years.” (`s04e12-6-023`) — *prima di* + infinitive with an attached pronoun (*capirlo*).

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Using a conjugated verb after these words.** English *before I go* tempts you into *~~prima di vado~~*. Italian needs *prima di andare*.
2. **Forgetting *di* after *prima*.** *~~Prima mangiare~~* → *prima **di** mangiare*.
3. **Using *dopo* + present infinitive.** *~~dopo mangiare~~* is not standard; say *dopo aver mangiato*.
4. **Wrong auxiliary in the past infinitive.** Movement and reflexive verbs take *essere*: *dopo **essere** arrivati*, not *~~dopo aver arrivati~~*.
5. **No agreement with *essere*.** A woman says *dopo essere arrivat**a***; a group of friends says *dopo essere arrivat**i***.
6. **Putting the pronoun before the infinitive.** It attaches to the end: *senza dirlo*, *dopo averlo fatto*, *prima di capirlo*.
7. **Confusing *invece* and *invece di*.** *Invece di* needs an infinitive; *invece* alone is a contrast word with a comma.

## Practice

Complete each sentence with the correct form. Use the verb in brackets.

1. Prima di ______ (uscire), prendo le chiavi.
2. Dopo ______ (mangiare), laviamo i piatti.
3. Dopo essere ______ (arrivare) a Milano, ha chiamato sua madre.
4. Esco senza ______ (dire) niente a nessuno.
5. Invece di ______ (comprare) il pane, l'ha fatto a casa.
6. Dopo aver ______ (soffriggere) la cipolla, aggiungi il pomodoro.
7. Prima di ______ (capire) questa regola, ci ho messo un anno.
8. Non si impara una lingua senza ______ (sbagliare).

### Answer key

1. **uscire** — *prima di* + present infinitive.
2. **aver mangiato** — *dopo* + *aver* + participle (same subject, non-movement verb).
3. **arrivata** (or **arrivati**, depending on the subject) — *essere* + participle, with agreement.
4. **dire** — *senza* + infinitive.
5. **comprare** — *invece di* + infinitive.
6. **soffritto** — the participle after *aver*.
7. **capirlo** — the pronoun attaches to the infinitive.
8. **sbagliare** — *senza* + infinitive.
