# Indirect object pronouns: *mi, ti, gli, le…*

## What they are

An **indirect object** is the person something is *given*, *said*, *sent* or *written* **to**. In English you use "to me", "to you", "to him":

- I sent **a message to Franco**. → *Ho mandato un messaggio **a Franco**.*
- Julia answered **me**. → *Giulia **mi** ha risposto.*

Italian doesn't like repeating *a Franco*, *a me*, *a te*. It replaces those phrases with short pronouns that sit **in front of the verb**: *mi, ti, gli, le, Le, ci, vi, gli*.

These are different from direct object pronouns (*mi, ti, lo, la, ci, vi, li, le*), which are the person or thing **directly** receiving the action. Compare:

- *Franco **mi** telefona.* — direct? No: he phones **to me**. → indirect: **mi**
- *Franco **mi** vede.* — direct: he sees **me**. → direct: **mi** (same form, different job)

The forms *mi, ti, ci, vi* look the same, so the trick is to ask: **is there a hidden "to"?** If you can put "to" in English, it's indirect.

## How it works

| Person | Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| io | **mi** (to me) | *Kevin **mi** manda un vocale.* |
| tu | **ti** (to you) | *Io **ti** telefono stasera.* |
| lui | **gli** (to him) | *Chiara **gli** scrive ogni giorno.* |
| lei | **le** (to her) | *Leo **le** manda un saluto.* |
| Lei (formal) | **Le** (to you, polite) | *Signora Galli, **Le** telefono domani.* |
| noi | **ci** (to us) | *Il nonno **ci** manda quaranta vocali.* |
| voi | **vi** (to you all) | *Io **vi** scrivo nel gruppo.* |
| loro | **gli** (to them) | *Kevin **gli** telefona lunedì.* |

Note that **gli** means both "to him" and "to them".

The pronoun usually goes **directly before the conjugated verb**:

- *Gli telefono subito.* — I'll call him right away.
- *Ti do un consiglio.* — I'm giving you a piece of advice.

With the *passato prossimo*, it sits before the whole verb (before *ho / hai / ha…*), never between the auxiliary and the participle:

- *Gli ho telefonato.* — I called him. (**not** *Ho gli telefonato.*)
- *Mi hai mandato un messaggio.* — You sent me a message.

The verbs that most often take an indirect object are exactly the ones this chapter's characters use all day: **dire, dare, scrivere, mandare, telefonare, rispondere, chiedere, regalare, scusarsi (con qualcuno)**.

Careful: *telefonare a qualcuno* → *telefonargli* / *gli telefono* — "the phone call goes **to** the person". But *chiamare qualcuno* takes a direct object in Italian: *Chiamo Franco*, *Lo chiamo*.

## From the story

[[Gli telefono subito.]]
*The pronoun goes right before the verb; no need to repeat "a Franco".*

[[Tommaso, ti ho dato un consiglio.]]
*With passato prossimo, the pronoun comes before **ho**, not after it.*

[[Nadia mi ha dato un consiglio.]]
***mi** = to me: the advice is given **to** Kevin.*

[[Mi hai mandato un messaggio.]]
*Same pronoun, now with "you" as the subject: the message was sent **to me**.*

[[Le ho risposto.]]
***le** = to her, feminine singular: Emma answers **her**. Don't confuse it with **gli**.*

[[Le mando un saluto dal cane di Dario.]]
*Leo uses **Le** with a capital letter because he speaks to Ornella with **Lei**.*

[[Non ti dico più niente.]]
*A very common phrase for "I'm not telling you anything else."*

[[Perché non me l'hai mandato tu?]]
***me** is the stressed-looking form used before another pronoun; the meaning is still "to me".*

## Common mistakes

1. **Putting the pronoun after the verb.** *Telefono gli* is wrong; it is *gli telefono*.
2. **Splitting the passato prossimo.** *Ho gli detto* is wrong; it is *gli ho detto*.
3. **Using *lo / la* instead of *gli / le*.** *Lo telefono* means you're phoning **him** directly, like a switchboard. If you mean "I call him", use *gli telefono*.
4. **Using *gli* for a woman.** In careful Italian, *a Lucia* → ***le*** *telefono*. Many speakers say *gli* casually, but in this story keep *le* for a woman.
5. **Forgetting the formal *Le*.** With Ornella or Roberto, Kevin would say *Le telefono*, capital **L**, not *le*.
6. **Adding both pronoun and phrase.** *Gli telefono a Franco* is a duplicate; choose one.
