# Andare/venire, portare/prendere: going, coming, taking and bringing

## What it is

Italian has two pairs of verbs that English learners mix up constantly.

- **andare** = to go (away from where I am now)
- **venire** = to come (toward where I am now, or toward the person I'm talking to)
- **portare** = to take/bring (a person or a thing — the person *arrives with you*)
- **prendere** = to take (you *grab* something: a train, a bus, a ticket, a shortcut, or a lift)

So the real question is not "which verb means take?", but **where is the speaker standing?**

## How it works

### andare vs venire

| Verb | Movement | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **andare** | away from me | *Vado a Milano.* (I'm going to Milan — I'm speaking from Borgoverde.) |
| **venire** | toward me / with me | *Vieni con noi?* (Are you coming with us?) |

If someone is at home and you're at home, they **vengono** to the table. If you're at the bar and they're at the bar, they **vanno** home (away from the bar). The same trip is *andare* for the person leaving and *venire* for the person waiting.

*Venire* also means "to come along": «**Vieni** con noi.» You join the group.

### portare vs prendere

**Portare** = you *carry* someone or something from A to B.

- *Ti porto a Milano.* (I drive/ take you to Milan.)
- *Porta il quaderno.* (Bring the notebook.)

**Prendere** = you *grab* something and use it.

- *Prendo la macchina.* (I take the car — I use it.)
- *Prendo il treno.* (I take the train.)
- *Ho preso la strada sbagliata.* (I took the wrong road.)

Note: you **prendere** a *passaggio* (you accept a lift) and you **portare** a *passeggero* (you carry the passenger).

### tornare, passare, accompagnare

- **tornare** = to go back, to return to where you were before. *Torno a casa.*
- **passare** = to go by, to drop in. *Passo alle otto.*
- **passare a prendere** = to swing by and pick someone up. *Ti passo a prendere alle dieci.*
- **accompagnare** = to take someone somewhere and leave them there. *Accompagno Emma a Monza.*

### volerci vs metterci — how long something "takes"

This is the part that makes learners panic, so look at the table calmly.

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **ci vuole / ci vogliono** | "it takes" — the time the *thing* needs | *Con la coda ci vuole un'ora.* |
| **ci metto, ci metti, ci mette…** | "I take / you take…" — the time a *person* spends | *Ci metto un'ora.* |

So: **ci vuole** describes the journey. **Ci metto** tells us how long *you* spend doing it. The subject of *metterci* is always a person (or a group including one).

## From the story

[[«Con la coda in autostrada, ci vuole un'ora,»]]
*ci vuole* — impersonal: the tailback needs an hour.

[[«Ci mettiamo un'ora,»]]
*ci mettiamo* — same idea, but now with *us* as the subject: we take an hour.

[[«Dopo torno a Monza a prenderti alle cinque,»]]
*tornare* = come back; *prenderti* = pick you up (attached pronoun).

[[«Papà, perché torni indietro?»]]
*torni indietro* — going back to where you came from.

[[«Signor Carter, io prendo un passaggio?»]]
*prendere un passaggio* — accepting a ride.

[[«Il passaggio è tuo,»]]
The ride belongs to Leo: *passaggio* is the noun.

[[«Il passeggero è arrivato,»]]
The *passageiro* is the person being carried — a different word from *passaggio*.

[[«Perché ci metti tre ore?»]]
*ci metti* — second person of *metterci*, used with *tu*.

[[«Ci mettiamo sette minuti,»]]
Contrast with the hour-long trip: same verb, different time.

*venire* — joining a group, moving toward the speaker.

## Common mistakes

1. **Using *andare* when you mean *venire*.** From home, you don't say *Vado con te* if you're asking the other person to include you. Say *Vengo con te*, because you join them where they are.
2. **Saying *prendo il passaggio*** when you mean *do il passaggio*. The driver *dà* or *offre* a lift; the passenger *prende* a passaggio.
3. **Mixing *portare* and *prendere*.** *Porto la macchina* makes no sense — you *prendi* the car. *Prendo Emma a Monza* sounds odd too: you *porti* Emma to Monza.
4. **Using *ci vuole* with a person.** *Io ci vuole un'ora* is wrong. Say *Ci metto un'ora*.
5. **Forgetting the pronoun in *passare a prendere*.** *Passo prendere te* is ungrammatical. Write *Ti passo a prendere*.
