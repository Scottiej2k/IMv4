# Passato prossimo with *essere*: "I went", "we came", "she stayed"

## What it is

In Italian, the past tense you use for finished actions is the **passato prossimo**: *ho mangiato* ("I ate / I have eaten"), *sono andato* ("I went / I have gone").

Most verbs build it with **avere** (*ho, hai, ha…* + participle). But a large and very common group of verbs builds it with **essere** (*sono, sei, è…* + participle). These are mostly verbs of **going, coming, arriving, leaving, staying** — exactly the verbs you need to tell someone about your day.

The big difference: with *avere* the participle never changes (*ho visto, abbiamo visto*). With *essere* the participle behaves like an adjective: it agrees with the person who is the subject.

## How it works

| | *andare* (to go) | *partire* (to leave) | *arrivare* (to arrive) |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | sono andato / andata | sono partito / partita | sono arrivato / arrivata |
| tu | sei andato / andata | sei partito / partita | sei arrivato / arrivata |
| lui / lei | è andato / andata | è partito / partita | è arrivato / arrivata |
| noi | siamo andati / andate | siamo partiti / partite | siamo arrivati / arrivate |
| voi | siete andati / andate | siete partiti / partite | siete arrivati / arrivate |
| loro | sono andati / andate | sono partiti / partite | sono arrivati / arrivate |

**The rule for the ending:** a man or a boy takes **-o** (singular) and **-i** (plural); a woman or a girl takes **-a** (singular) and **-e** (plural). A mixed group takes the masculine plural, **-i**.

Emma says *sono restata a casa* (feminine), Kevin says *siamo tornati* (masculine plural, the whole family).

## Which verbs take *essere*

The usual ones: **andare, venire, arrivare, partire, uscire, entrare, tornare, restare**, plus *nascere* (to be born), *morire* (to die), *diventare* (to become), *stare, essere, piacere*. A handy memory: motion, staying, arriving, leaving, and changes of state.

**Note:** *entrare* takes **essere**: *sono entrato in casa*. But the fixed phrase for trains and buses is *sale sul treno* ("gets on the train"), which is why Chiara corrects Kevin's «Entriamo nel treno!».

Watch out for the small trap: *sono* also means "I am" and *sono* also means "they are". *Sono arrivato* = "I arrived / I have arrived"; *sono a Monza* = "I am in Monza". The participle tells you which one it is.

## From the story

[[Siamo andati a Monza]]
*Essere* + participle of *andare*, masculine plural: the whole family went.

[[Siamo venuti in treno]]
Chiara corrects Kevin: they *came* by train, so *venire* (with *essere*), not *andare*.

[[È venuto in bicicletta.]]
Ornella describes Tommaso: third person singular, masculine participle *venuto*.

[[Sono caduta con stile]]
Emma: feminine singular of *cadere*, so the participle ends in **-a**, not **-o**.

[[Sono restata a casa a studiare. Tutto il pomeriggio.]]
Emma again: *restare* takes *essere*, and the participle agrees with her.

[[Alle quattro sei uscita con lo skateboard.]]
Ornella to Emma: *tu* form of *uscire*, feminine **-a** agreement.

[[Il treno è partito alle dieci e dodici, in perfetto orario.]]

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Using *avere* with motion verbs.** Not *ho andato*, never. It is *sono andato*.
2. **Forgetting agreement.** A woman does not say *sono andato*; she says *sono andata*. A group of two women says *siamo andate*.
3. **Adding agreement to *avere* verbs.** *Ho vista Emma* is wrong in standard Italian: *ho visto Emma*. Agreement belongs to the *essere* group only.
4. **Confusing *sono* + adjective with *sono* + participle.** *Sono stanco* = "I'm tired"; *sono arrivato* = "I arrived."
5. **Mixing up *andare* and *venire*.** *Andare* moves away from where you are; *venire* moves toward the person you're talking to. Chiara *è venuta* to Monza with the family, but they *sono andati* to Monza from Borgoverde.
6. **Wrong participle forms.** Learn them with the verb: *andare → andato*, *venire → venuto*, *uscire → uscito*, *entrare → entrato*, *restare → restato*, *arrivare → arrivato*, *partire → partito*, *tornare → tornato*.
