# Ci and ne with verbs: "about it", "there", "of it"

## What it is

Italian has two little particles, **ci** and **ne**, that attach to a verb and point back to something you already said, so you don't have to repeat it. They have no meaning alone.

- **Ci** usually means "about it / on it / to it" — and sometimes "there".
- **Ne** usually means "of it / about it / some of it".

*Ci penso io.* = "I think **about it**" → "I'll take care of it."
*Che ne pensi?* = "What do you think **of it**?"

## How it works

### Ci replaces *a questa cosa* (about it)

| Without ci | With ci |
|---|---|
| Penso **al seggiolino**. | **Ci** penso. (I'll take care of it.) |
| Credo **a questa storia**. | **Ci** credo. (I believe it.) |
| Tengo **a questa famiglia**. | **Ci** tengo. (I care about it.) |
| Riesco **a finire**. | **Ci** riesco. (I manage it.) |
| Conto **su di te**. | **Ci** conto. (I'm counting on it.) |

Some verbs change meaning once **ci** is glued on:

- *tengo* = I keep, I hold → **ci tengo** = I care about it
- *credo* = I believe → **ci credo** = I believe it, I buy it
- *riesco* = I succeed → **ci riesco** = I manage to do it
- *penso* = I think → **ci penso** = I'll deal with it

### Ne replaces *di questa cosa* (of it) and quantities

| Without ne | With ne |
|---|---|
| Penso **della facciata**. | **Ne** penso bene. (I think well of it.) |
| Parliamo **del problema**? | **Ne** parliamo? (Shall we talk about it?) |
| Vuoi **del tè**? — Sì, **ne** voglio. | (some of it) |
| Quanti biscotti? — **Ne** prendo due. | (two of them) |

### Where does it go?

- Present tense: **before** the verb. *Ci penso. Ne parlo.*
- With an infinitive: attach it to the end. *riuscirci*, *contarci*, *prendersene cura*.
- Passato prossimo: **ci / ne** stay in front of the auxiliary. *Ci ho pensato. Ne abbiamo parlato.*

### Two fixed expressions from this episode

**non ce la faccio** = "I can't manage it." Here *ce* is just *ci* in front of another pronoun, and *la* is fixed. Positive: *ce la faccio*.

**andarsene** = "to leave, to go away." Preview for later: *me ne vado*, *se ne va* — *ne* here means "from here".

## From the story

E nell'aria resta una frase di Chiara: «**Ci penso io**». — And in the air one sentence of Chiara's stays: “**I’ll take care of it**.” (`s03e17-1-094`)
*Ci* stands for the thing being handled. Chiara says it again and again, and never says what the thing is — everybody already knows.

«**Che ne pensi** di Tommaso?» chiede. — "**What do you think of** Tommaso?" she asks. (`s03e17-4-011`)
*Ne* = "of him / about it": Emma asks for an opinion without repeating Tommaso's name.

«**Ne penso** bene» dice. — "**I think well of it**," she says. (`s03e17-2-015`)
The answer uses *ne* alone, with no object after it.

«**Ci tengo** a lui.» — "**I care about** him." (`s03e17-4-017`)
*Tenerci* = to care about someone. Without *ci*, *tengo* means "I hold".

«**Non ce la faccio**, papà» dice. — "**I can't manage it**, Dad," she says. (`s03e17-5-060`)
The fixed idiom for "I can't manage it anymore" — Chiara's breaking point.

«**Non ci credo** nemmeno un po'.» — "**I don't believe it** at all." (`s03e17-7-035`)
*Crederci* = to believe it. Nadia's verdict on Matteo's promises.

«**Ci vai** tu con lui?» — "**Will you go** with him?" (`s03e17-6-048`) / «**Ci vado** io» dice Kevin. — "**I'll go**," Kevin says. (`s03e17-6-049`)
Here *ci* means "there": "Will you go there?" — "I'll go."

«**Puoi contarci**» dice Kevin. «Sempre.» — "**You can count on it**," Kevin says. "Always." (`s03e17-4-074`)
*Contarci* = to count on it. The *ci* stays even on the infinitive.

«**Ne parliamo** adesso» dice. — "**Let's talk about it** now," Chiara says. (`s03e17-5-096`)
*Parlarne* = to talk about it, with no noun after.

«Adesso **me ne vado**» dice. — "Now **I'm leaving**," she says. (`s03e17-5-111`)
Preview of *andarsene*: "Now I'm leaving." *Ne* = "from here".

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Leaving ci / ne out.** *Penso* alone means "I think". If you mean "I'll take care of it", you need *ci penso*. Same for *ci credo*, *ci tengo*, *ci riesco*.
2. **Swapping them.** Rough rule: **ci** = *a* (to, about, on), **ne** = *di* (of, from). *Ci penso* (about the problem), *ne parlo* (about the problem, with someone).
3. **Translating "it" as *lo* / *la*.** For ideas introduced by *a* or *di*, Italian wants *ci* / *ne*: *Ci penso*, not *lo penso*.
4. **Wrong position in the past.** *Ci ho pensato*, not *ho ci pensato*.
5. **Breaking up fixed expressions.** *Voglio riuscirci*, not *voglio riuscire ci*.
6. **Confusing *ci sono* ("there is / there are") with *ci penso*.** Both use *ci*, but the jobs are different; context makes it clear.

## Practice

Fill in **ci** or **ne**.

1. Non ___ credo! Hai comprato un seggiolino per bicicletta?
2. Che ___ pensi della nuova facciata?
3. ___ penso io al dottore, papà.
4. Quanti biscotti vuoi? — ___ prendo tre.
5. Non ___ riesco a finire tutto oggi.
6. Parliamo del passeggino? — Sì, ___ parliamo domani.
7. Puoi ___ contare: vengo io con tuo padre.
8. ___ tengo molto a questa famiglia.

### Answer key

1. **ci** (*crederci* = to believe it)
2. **ne** (*che ne pensi* = what do you think of it)
3. **ci** (*pensarci* = to take care of it)
4. **ne** (quantity: three of them)
5. **ci** — *non ci riesco*; the other idiom is *non ce la faccio*.
6. **ne** (*parlarne* = to talk about it)
7. **ci** (*contarci* = to count on it)
8. **ci** (*tenerci* = to care about something)
