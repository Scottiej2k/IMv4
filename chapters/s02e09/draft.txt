# Piacere in the past, and verbs like piacere

## What it is

In Italian, *piacere* ("to please") doesn't work like English "to like." You don't say *I like pizza*; you say *pizza is pleasing to me*: **Mi piace la pizza**. The thing you like is the **subject** of the sentence, and the person who likes it is an **indirect object pronoun** (*mi, ti, gli, le, ci, vi, gli*).

A small family of verbs works exactly the same way: **mancare** (to be missing, to miss), **servire** (to be needed), **sembrare** (to seem), **interessare** (to interest), **bastare** (to be enough), **restare** (to remain, to be left). The pattern is always: *pronoun + verb + thing*.

- **Mi manca Chicago** — Chicago is missing *to me* → I miss Chicago.
- **Ti serve un caffè?** — Is a coffee needed *to you*? → Do you need a coffee?
- **Le basta questo?** — Is this enough *for her*? → Is this enough for her?

## How it works

### Present

The verb agrees with the **thing**, not with the person.

| Thing | Example |
|---|---|
| singular | **Mi piace** il caffè. / **Mi manca** la mia casa. |
| plural | **Mi piacciono** i pancake. / **Mi mancano** i miei amici. |

The indirect pronouns: **mi** (to me), **ti** (to you, informal), **gli** (to him), **le** (to her / to you, formal), **ci** (to us), **vi** (to you all), **gli** (to them).

### Past: passato prossimo

*Piacere* takes **essere** in the past, and the participle agrees with the **thing** — like an adjective:

| Thing | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| singular m. | **Mi è piaciuto** il pomeriggio. | I liked the afternoon. |
| singular f. | **Mi è piaciuta** la cena. | I liked the dinner. |
| plural m. | **Mi sono piaciuti** i biscotti. | I liked the biscuits. |
| plural f. | **Mi sono piaciute** le foto. | I liked the photos. |

Note: the pronoun stays the same, and the auxiliary is always **essere** (mi è, ti è, gli è, le è, ci è, vi è, gli sono).

The other verbs follow the same logic. They also take **essere**, and their participles agree with the thing:

- **Mi è mancato** il lago. / **Mi sono mancati** i miei amici.
- **Mi è servito** un caffè. / **Mi sono serviti** due biscotti.
- **Mi è sembrata** una bella idea. / **Mi sono sembrate** strane.
- **Mi è bastato**, **mi è interessato**, **mi è restato**.

## From the story

- [[Ti è piaciuto?]] — Singular *piacere* in the past with **ti**; the participle agrees with the thing (here, the pancake).
- [[Mi è piaciuto tanto]] — The same verb with **mi**; the participle is masculine singular.
- [[Mi manca Chicago. E mi mancano tante cose]] — Singular and plural of *mancare* with the same pronoun: the verb follows the **thing**.
- [[E che cosa le manca di più?]] — *Le* is the formal *you* here: "What do you miss most?" with *Lei*.
- [[La nostalgia, signor Carter, non è una debolezza]] — Not the grammar focus, but the theme word that the whole verb family is built around.
- [[Il caffè e due biscotti mi bastano per una conversazione.]] — *Bastare* in the present: "Coffee and two biscuits are enough for me."
- [[Non gli interessa nulla, signor Carter]] — *Interessare* with **gli** (to him), said of Pavarotti the cat.
- [[Mi sembrano buoni, signor Carter]] — *Sembrare* with a plural thing: "They seem good to me."
- [[Mi è piaciuto molto. Mi è piaciuto essere utile, essere bravo]] — *Piacere* in the past, twice, with **mi** and agreement in the masculine singular.
- [[A me interessa solo la musica, la mattina]] — *Interessare* with the stressed *a me* added for contrast.
- [[Non mi basta restare qui]] — *Bastare* with a verb phrase as the subject: "Staying here is not enough for me."

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **"Io manco Chicago."** English "I miss Chicago" puts *I* first. Italian puts the **thing** first and the person in the pronoun: **Mi manca Chicago**. The literal meaning is "Chicago is missing to me."
2. **"Mi manco Chicago."** A very common slip: *mi manco* means "I miss myself." The pronoun must be the **person who feels**, and the verb must agree with the **thing missed**: *mi manca* (singular), *mi mancano* (plural).
3. **Wrong participle agreement.** *Mi è piaciuto i pancake* is wrong; it must be *Mi **sono piaciuti** i pancake*. Look at the thing: is it singular or plural, masculine or feminine?
4. **Using *avere*.** Say **Mi è piaciuto**, never *Mi ho piaciuto*. This verb always takes *essere*.
5. **Piacere with a person.** *Mi piace Marco* means you find Marco pleasant (you like him). To say you love someone, Italian uses *voler bene a* — a different structure. *Mi piace Marco* is friendly, not romantic.
6. **Forgetting that *piacere* is not *like*.** *Like* is a normal verb with a direct object; *piacere* is a "backwards" verb. Translate it with "pleases": *La pizza mi piace* = "Pizza pleases me."
