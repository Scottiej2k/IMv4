# Basic Connectors: perché, quindi, però, allora, siccome, infatti, invece, mentre, anche se, prima / poi / alla fine

## What they are

Connectors are the small words that hold a story together. They tell the reader *why* something happened, *what the result was*, *what was different*, *in what order*. Without them, Italian sounds like a list of separate facts: *Ho letto la lettera. Ho pianto.* With them, it becomes a story: *Ho letto la lettera, **quindi** ho pianto.*

In this chapter, Chiara reads her mother's letter and tells the whole story to Kevin and to her father. Every scene runs on connectors, because a letter — like a person telling something — has to link one idea to the next.

## How they work

| Connector | Meaning | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **perché** | because / why | gives a reason, or asks for one |
| **siccome** | since, as | gives the reason *first*, at the start of the sentence |
| **quindi** | so, therefore | gives the result |
| **allora** | so, then | gives the result, or moves the story on (*Allora, che facciamo?*) |
| **però** | but, however | contrasts, a little softer than *ma* |
| **invece** | instead, on the other hand | contrasts two different things or people |
| **infatti** | in fact, indeed | confirms what you just said |
| **mentre** | while | two things at the same time |
| **anche se** | even if, even though | a contrast you accept anyway |
| **prima … poi … alla fine** | first … then … in the end | puts events in order |

Two notes on word order:

- **perché** and **siccome** both give a reason, but they sit in different places. *Siccome* opens the sentence: *Siccome ti conosco, so che stai piangendo.* *Perché* normally comes after the main clause: *Ho pianto perché la lettera era triste.* Never start a sentence with *perché* to mean "since" — that sounds like a child answering a question (*Perché?* "Why?").
- **anche se** ("even though") is followed by the normal indicative, not by a subjunctive: *Anche se è piccola, un giorno capirà.*

## From the story

[[Siccome ti conosco, so che stai piangendo.]] — *Siccome* opens the sentence and gives the reason before the result.

[[Quindi non posso spedirla oggi?]] — *Quindi* introduces the result of the tabaccheria being closed.

[[Però va bene. Piangere fa bene.]] — *Però* softens the contrast: it's bad, but it's ok.

[[Infatti ho aspettato mesi prima di scrivere questa lettera.]] — *Infatti* confirms what Anna has just said.

[[Invece tuo padre non sa niente di questa lettera.]] — *Invece* contrasts Franco with Lucia.

[[Anche se gli voglio bene, non gli dico tutto.]] — *Anche se* accepts a contrast; the verb stays in the indicative.

[[Prima come amica, poi come qualcosa di più, se succedeva.]] — *prima … poi* puts the two stages in order.

[[Alla fine ti dico una cosa semplice.]] — *alla fine* closes the sequence and moves to the most important point.

[[Prima la scatola, poi la busta, alla fine la lettera.]] — the same three connectors compress a whole afternoon into one line.

[[Perché non me l'hai detto?]] / [[Perché ho promesso a tua moglie di non dirti niente.]] — *perché* asks for a reason and then gives it.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

- **Using *perché* at the start to mean "since".** English "Because I know you…" is a wrong model. Italian uses *Siccome ti conosco…* or *Dato che…*. *Perché* first is a question.
- **Confusing *però* and *ma*.** Both mean "but". *Ma* is short and neutral; *però* is a little more emphatic and often comes after the verb or at the end: *Va bene, però…*
- **Confusing *infatti* and *in effetti*.** *Infatti* confirms something already said ("in fact, exactly"). It does not mean "in effect" in a legal sense.
- **Confusing *invece* and *invece di*.** *Invece* alone means "instead / on the other hand" and stands with a comma: *Invece tuo padre non sa niente.* *Invece di* + infinitive means "instead of doing": *Invece di leggere, ha dormito.*
- **Putting *anche se* with a subjunctive.** No: *Anche se piove, esco.* *Anche se era stanca, ha letto la lettera.*
- **Forgetting that *quindi* and *allora* are not the same.** *Quindi* is logical ("therefore"). *Allora* can mean "so" but also "at that time" and "so, let's go": *Allora, questi pancake?*
- **Mixing up *mentre* and *per*.** *Mentre* means "while" (two actions at once). *Per* means "for" (duration or purpose).
