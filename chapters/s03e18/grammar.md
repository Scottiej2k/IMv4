# Basic Connectors: perché, quindi, però, allora, siccome, infatti, invece, mentre, anche se, prima / poi / alla fine

## What they are

Connectors are the small words that hold a story together. They tell the reader *why* something happened, *what the result was*, *what was different*, *in what order*. Without them, Italian sounds like a list of separate facts: *Ho letto la lettera. Ho pianto.* With them, it becomes a story: *Ho letto la lettera, **quindi** ho pianto.*

In this chapter, Chiara reads her mother's letter and tells the whole story to Kevin and to her father. Every scene runs on connectors, because a letter — like a person telling something — has to link one idea to the next.

## How they work

| Connector | Meaning | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **perché** | because / why | gives a reason, or asks for one |
| **siccome** | since, as | gives the reason *first*, at the start of the sentence |
| **quindi** | so, therefore | gives the result |
| **allora** | so, then | gives the result, or moves the story on (*Allora, che facciamo?*) |
| **però** | but, however | contrasts, a little softer than *ma* |
| **invece** | instead, on the other hand | contrasts two different things or people |
| **infatti** | in fact, indeed | confirms what you just said |
| **mentre** | while | two things at the same time |
| **anche se** | even if, even though | a contrast you accept anyway |
| **prima … poi … alla fine** | first … then … in the end | puts events in order |

Two notes on word order:

- **perché** and **siccome** both give a reason, but they sit in different places. *Siccome* opens the sentence: *Siccome ti conosco, so che stai piangendo.* *Perché* normally comes after the main clause: *Ho pianto perché la lettera era triste.* Never start a sentence with *perché* to mean "since" — that sounds like a child answering a question (*Perché?* "Why?").
- **anche se** ("even though") is followed by the normal indicative, not by a subjunctive: *Anche se è piccola, un giorno capirà.*

## From the story

«**Siccome** ti conosco, so che stai piangendo.» — “**Since** I know you, I know you're crying.” (`s03e18-2-019`) — *Siccome* opens the sentence and gives the reason before the result.

«**Quindi** non posso spedirla oggi?» chiede Leo. — “**So** I can't mail it today?” asks Leo. (`s03e18-1-027`) — *Quindi* introduces the result of the tabaccheria being closed.

«**Però** va bene. Piangere fa bene.» — “**But** it's okay. Crying is good.” (`s03e18-2-020`) — *Però* softens the contrast: it's bad, but it's ok.

«**Infatti** ho aspettato mesi prima di scrivere questa lettera.» — “**In fact** I waited months before writing this letter.” (`s03e18-2-025`) — *Infatti* confirms what Anna has just said.

«**Invece** tuo padre non sa niente di questa lettera.» — “**Instead** your father knows nothing about this letter.” (`s03e18-2-036`) — *Invece* contrasts Franco with Lucia.

«**Anche se** gli voglio bene, non gli dico tutto.» — “**Even though** I love him, I don't tell him everything.” (`s03e18-2-037`) — *Anche se* accepts a contrast; the verb stays in the indicative.

«**Prima** come amica, **poi** come qualcosa di più, se succedeva.» — “**First** as a friend, **then** as something more, if it happened.” (`s03e18-2-040`) — *prima … poi* puts the two stages in order.

«**Alla fine** ti dico una cosa semplice.» — “**In the end** I'll tell you one simple thing.” (`s03e18-2-050`) — *alla fine* closes the sequence and moves to the most important point.

«Sì. Ma dall'inizio» dice Chiara. «**Prima** la scatola, **poi** la busta, **alla fine** la lettera.» — “Yes. But from the beginning,” says Chiara. “**First** the box, **then** the envelope, **at the end** the letter.” (`s03e18-5-013`) — the same three connectors compress a whole afternoon into one line.

«Perché non me l'hai detto?» chiede Franco. — “Why didn't you tell me?” asks Franco. (`s03e18-4-050`) / «**Perché** ho promesso a tua moglie di non dirti niente» risponde Lucia. — “**Because** I promised your wife not to tell you anything,” answers Lucia. (`s03e18-4-051`) — *perché* asks for a reason and then gives it.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

- **Using *perché* at the start to mean "since".** English "Because I know you…" is a wrong model. Italian uses *Siccome ti conosco…* or *Dato che…*. *Perché* first is a question.
- **Confusing *però* and *ma*.** Both mean "but". *Ma* is short and neutral; *però* is a little more emphatic and often comes after the verb or at the end: *Va bene, però…*
- **Confusing *infatti* and *in effetti*.** *Infatti* confirms something already said ("in fact, exactly"). It does not mean "in effect" in a legal sense.
- **Confusing *invece* and *invece di*.** *Invece* alone means "instead / on the other hand" and stands with a comma: *Invece tuo padre non sa niente.* *Invece di* + infinitive means "instead of doing": *Invece di leggere, ha dormito.*
- **Putting *anche se* with a subjunctive.** No: *Anche se piove, esco.* *Anche se era stanca, ha letto la lettera.*
- **Forgetting that *quindi* and *allora* are not the same.** *Quindi* is logical ("therefore"). *Allora* can mean "so" but also "at that time" and "so, let's go": *Allora, questi pancake?*
- **Mixing up *mentre* and *per*.** *Mentre* means "while" (two actions at once). *Per* means "for" (duration or purpose).

Fill in the best connector: *perché, siccome, quindi, però, invece, infatti, anche se, mentre, alla fine*.

1. Non ho il francobollo, ______ non posso spedire la lettera.
2. ______ ti conosco, so che stai piangendo.
3. Sono andata al Centro civico ______ volevo vedere Lucia.
4. Leo è piccolo, ______ scrive già lettere vere.
5. ______ tua madre non diceva niente, tu parli sempre.
6. Ho aperto la busta, ______ ho trovato due fogli.
7. ______ è piccola, un giorno capirà.
8. ______ ho letto la lettera tre volte, e ______ ho capito tutto.
9. Anna preparava il tè ______ Lucia tagliava la cipolla.
10. «Perché non mi hai detto niente?» «______ ho promesso a tua moglie.»

### Answer key

1. quindi (result)
2. Siccome (reason, opening the sentence)
3. perché (reason, after the main clause)
4. però (soft contrast)
5. Invece (contrast between two people)
6. infatti (confirmation)
7. Anche se (accepted contrast + indicative)
8. Prima / alla fine (order of events)
9. mentre (two actions at the same time)
10. Perché (answer to a question with *perché*)
