# Stressed Pronouns: *per me*, *con te*, *anche lui*

## What they are

Italian has two families of object pronouns. The **unstressed** ones (*mi, ti, lo, la, ci, vi, li, le*) are short and cling to the verb: *ti aiuto*, *lo vedo*. The **stressed** ones — also called **tonic** or **disjunctive** — are the same words as the subject pronouns: *me, te, lui, lei, Lei, noi, voi, loro*.

They are "stressed" because you say them with more force, and they stand on their own. You use them in three main situations:

1. **After a preposition**: *per me*, *con te*, *a lui*, *da lei*, *senza di noi*, *secondo me*.
2. **For emphasis or contrast**: *Lo faccio io, non tu.* — *Anche lui lavora.* — *A me piace, a lei no.*
3. **When the object is "heavy"** or when two objects of the same kind appear: *Vedo lui, non lei.*

Their great advantage for a learner is this: unlike *mi/ti/lo*, they never change shape and never attach to the verb. *Per me* is always *per me*.

## How it works

**The forms**

| Person | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| io | **me** | questo è per **me** |
| tu | **te** | vengo con **te** |
| lui | **lui** | parlo di **lui** |
| lei | **lei** | un consiglio per **lei** |
| Lei (formal) | **Lei** | questo è per **Lei**, signora |
| noi | **noi** | senza **noi** non parte niente |
| voi | **voi** | questo lavoro è per **voi** |
| loro | **loro** | lo dico a **loro** |

**Preposition + stressed pronoun** is the everyday pattern. The commonest combinations in speech are *per me* ("in my opinion", "for me"), *secondo me* ("in my opinion"), *con te*, *a lui*, *da loro*.

- *Per me va bene.* — In my opinion it's fine.
- *Secondo me è tardi.* — I think it's late.

**Emphasis and contrast.** Put the stressed pronoun at the front or end of the sentence to single someone out:

- ***Io** aspetto come gli altri.* — I wait like the others.
- ***Anche lui** è stanco.* — He's tired too.
- *Il problema sono **io**.* — The problem is me.

**With *anche*, *neanche*, *pure*:** *anche lui*, *anche a me*, *neanche a loro*. Note that *anche a me* means "to me too": the *a* is needed because *piacere*-type verbs take *a*.

**A warning about *Lei*.** The stressed formal pronoun is written with a capital letter when it means "you": *Questo è per **Lei**, signora Galli.* Without the capital, *lei* means "she".

**Stress, at last.** Some verbs need the stressed pronoun because there is no unstressed one to use: *pensare a* → *penso a te* (never *ti penso* in modern standard Italian), *contare su* → *conto su di te*. When the preposition is *con*, *su* or *per* with *noi/voi*, you often add *di*: *con noi* → *con noi* or *con **di** noi*, *su di voi*.

## From the story

«Grazie. **Per me** è importante sentirlo da **te**.» — “Thank you. **For me** it's important to hear it from **you**.” (`s02e12-5-062`) — Preposition + stressed pronoun, twice: *per me*, *da te*.
_E i miei **colleghi**? Che cosa dicono **loro**?_ — _And my **colleagues**? What do **they** say?_ (`s02e12-4-014`) — *loro* after *dicono* singles out the colleagues.
«Perché **per me** la **fila** è la **fila**. E **io** aspetto come gli altri.» — “Because **for me** the **line** is the **line**. And **I** wait like the others.” (`s02e12-6-019`) — *Per me* = "in my opinion"; *io* is emphatic.
«Sì. **Loro**. Se un cliente è **stanco**, gli dai un bicchiere d'acqua.» — “Yes. **Them**. If a customer is **tired**, you give him a glass of water.” (`s02e12-6-038`) — Contrast with the unstressed *gli* in the same sentence.
«**Per me** un consiglio di Franco è come un premio. E lui ha dato un consiglio a **te**.» — “**For me** a piece of advice from Franco is like a prize. And he gave advice to **you**.” (`s02e12-6-089`) — *a te*, not *ti*, because the speaker is contrasting two people.
«**Per lui** chi?» — “**For him** who?” (`s02e12-5-049`) — Question form: preposition + stressed pronoun, no verb needed.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

- **Using *mi/ti* after a preposition.** *Per mi* and *con ti* do not exist. Say *per me*, *con te*.
- **Forgetting *a*** with *piacere*: *Anche io piace* is wrong; say *Anche **a me** piace*.
- **Dropping the stressed pronoun when you need emphasis.** *Parlo con te* is not the same as *Ti parlo*; the first means "you're the one I'm talking with".
- **Leaving *Lui/Lei* out of the subject slot too soon.** At this level, the stressed pronoun often replaces a plain subject: *Lui aspetta* is more emphatic than *Aspetta*, but both are correct. Just don't say *Lui è* before the verb when you mean the neutral *È*.
- **Confusing *lei* and *Lei*.** In writing, the capital tells your reader whether you mean "she" or formal "you".
- **Adding *di* everywhere.** *Su di te* and *con di noi* are common, but *per di me* is not Italian: it is simply *per me*.

## Practice

1. Complete with the right stressed pronoun: *Questo caffè è per ___ (io).*
2. Complete: *Vengo al mercato con ___ (tu).*
3. Complete with *a* and a pronoun: *Anche ___ (io) piace il caffè lungo.*
4. Make it emphatic: *Anche lui lavora* → change *lui* into the formal *Lei*: *Anche ___ lavora, signore.*
5. Answer with a stressed pronoun: *Per chi è l'acqua?* — *È per ___ (noi).*
6. Choose the right form: *Penso a (ti / te) ogni giorno.*
7. Choose the right form: *Dico a (loro / li) la verità.*
8. Turn into a contrast sentence: *Io aspetto qui. Tu vai dentro.* → *Io aspetto qui, ___ vai dentro.*

### Answer key

1. *per me*
2. *con te*
3. *Anche a me piace il caffè lungo.*
4. *Anche Lei lavora, signore.*
5. *È per noi.*
6. *te*
7. *loro*
8. *Io aspetto qui, **tu** vai dentro.*
