# Present tense: -are verbs

## What it is

Italian verbs come in three big families, called *coniugazioni*. The biggest family is the **-are** verbs: *parlare*, *abitare*, *lavorare*, *mangiare*, *comprare*, *preparare*, *decorare*, *giocare*, *bussare*, *suonare*, *spiegare*, *tornare*, *regalare*. These are the first verbs every learner meets, and the good news is that almost all of them behave in exactly the same way.

The present tense in Italian covers three English forms at once. *Parlo* means "I speak", "I am speaking" and "I do speak". You don't need a helper verb.

## How it works

Take off the ending **-are** and you get the *radice* (stem). Then add the ending that matches the subject.

| | parlare | abitare | lavorare |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | parl**o** | abit**o** | lavor**o** |
| tu | parl**i** | abit**i** | lavor**i** |
| lui / lei | parl**a** | abit**a** | lavor**a** |
| noi | parl**iamo** | abit**iamo** | lavor**iamo** |
| voi | parl**ate** | abit**ate** | lavor**ate** |
| loro | parl**ano** | abit**ano** | lavor**ano** |

Notice two things. First, the *io* and *lui/lei* forms have no *-i*: *parlo*, *parla*. Second, Italian usually drops the subject pronoun, because the ending already tells you who is doing the action: *parlo* is enough for "I speak".

**-care and -gare verbs.** Verbs ending in **-care** (*giocare*, *bussare* is regular, but *cercare*, *spiegare*, *giocare* are the classic ones) keep a hard *c* or *g* sound. To do that, they add an **h** in the *tu* and *noi* forms:

- giocare → io **gioco**, tu **giochi**, lui **gioca**, noi **giochiamo**, voi **giocate**, loro **giocano**
- spiegare → io **spiego**, tu **spieghi**, lui **spiega**, noi **spieghiamo**, voi **spiegate**, loro **spiegano**

**-iare verbs.** Verbs ending in **-iare**, like *mangiare*, keep the *i* in the *tu* and *noi* forms, and drop it in the others:

- mangiare → io **mangio**, tu **mangi**, lui **mangia**, noi **mangiamo**, voi **mangiate**, loro **mangiano**

## From the story

Kevin **decora** il giardino con attenzione. — Kevin **decorates** the garden carefully. (`s01e08-1-006`)
Third person singular: *lui* (Kevin) → *decor**a***.

Sul muro della casa numero quattordici ci sono venti **zucche** piccole. — On the wall of house number fourteen there are twenty little **pumpkins**. (`s01e08-2-003`)
A reminder that *ci sono* is "there are", used here with a plural noun.

«Venti **zucche**, venti **candele**!» dice Kevin. — “Twenty **pumpkins**, twenty **candles**!” Kevin says. (`s01e08-2-005`)
Plural nouns *zucche*, *candele* are the things Kevin is busy decorating.

«Matteo, ti **spiego** Halloween,» dice Kevin. — “Matteo, let me **explain** Halloween to you,” Kevin says. (`s01e08-3-012`)
First person singular: *io* → *spieg**o***, with the *h* gone in the *io* form, and the hard sound coming back in *spieghi*, *spieghiamo*.

«Ok, **spiega**,» dice Matteo, e prepara un caffè. — “OK, **explain**,” Matteo says, and makes a coffee. (`s01e08-3-013`)
Second person singular *tu* → *spieg**a***; third person singular *lui* → *prepar**a***.

«I bambini **bussano** alle porte e dicono **dolcetto o scherzetto**,» dice Kevin. — “The kids knock at the doors and say **trick or treat**,” Kevin says. (`s01e08-3-014`)
Third person plural: *loro* → *buss**ano***, "they knock".

«Sì. Ogni anno **compro** i **fiori** e un **vaso**,» dice Franco. — “Yes. Every year I **buy** the **flowers** and a **vase**,” Franco says. (`s01e08-3-033`)
First person singular: *io* → *compr**o***.

«A Ognissanti la **festa** è qui,» dice. — “On All Saints' Day the **holiday** is here,” he says. (`s01e08-6-052`)
A little *essere* mixed in: *la festa è qui*.

Kevin guarda la **tomba** e poi i **fiori**. — Kevin looks at the **grave** and then at the **flowers**. (`s01e08-6-045`)
Third person singular regular verb *guardare* → *guard**a***.

«Sì, **giochiamo**,» dice Kevin. — “Yes, we're **playing**,” Kevin says. (`s01e08-7-037`)
*Noi* form of *giocare*: the *h* keeps the *g* hard before *-iamo*.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding an English helper verb.** English speakers say "I am working", so they write *sono lavoro* or *sto lavoro*. In Italian, present tense alone is enough: *lavoro*.
- **Mixing *io* and *lui/lei*.** *Parlo* (I) and *parla* (he/she). The final vowel is the whole message.
- **Forgetting the *h* in *-care* / *-gare*.** *Tu giochi*, not *tu gioci*; *noi spieghiamo*, not *noi spiegiamo* (which would sound like *spiegiamo* with a soft *g*).
- **Dropping the *i* in *mangiare*.** *Mangi*, not *mang*; *mangiamo*, not *mangamo*.
- **Using the wrong family ending.** Learners sometimes say *lavora* for "I work". Remember: *io lavor**o***, *lui lavor**a***.
- **Talking about *io* where the Italian talks about *noi*.** *Giochiamo* means "we play" or "let's play", depending on the situation. Italian often uses the *noi* form where English uses "let's".

## Practice

Complete each sentence with the correct present-tense form.

1. Io ______ (abitare) in Via dei Tigli.
2. Tu ______ (mangiare) una caramella.
3. Franco ______ (comprare) i fiori.
4. Leo e Emma ______ (giocare) a Halloween.
5. Noi ______ (preparare) i sacchetti insieme.
6. Voi ______ (bussare) alla porta di Ornella.
7. Loro ______ (spiegare) la regola del Comitato.
8. Io ______ (decorare) la zucca con una candela.

### Answer key

1. **abito** — first person singular, *io*.
2. **mangi** — *-iare* verb, *tu* keeps the *i*.
3. **compra** — third person singular, *franco*.
4. **giocano** — third person plural; *-care* verb, *noi* form would be *giochiamo*.
5. **prepariamo** — first person plural.
6. **bussate** — second person plural, ends in *-ate*.
7. **spiegano** — third person plural; the *h* appears in *tu spieghi* and *noi spieghiamo*, not here.
8. **decoro** — first person singular, *io*.
