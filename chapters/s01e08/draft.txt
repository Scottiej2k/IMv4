# Present tense: -are verbs

## What it is

Italian verbs come in three big families, called *coniugazioni*. The biggest family is the **-are** verbs: *parlare*, *abitare*, *lavorare*, *mangiare*, *comprare*, *preparare*, *decorare*, *giocare*, *bussare*, *suonare*, *spiegare*, *tornare*, *regalare*. These are the first verbs every learner meets, and the good news is that almost all of them behave in exactly the same way.

The present tense in Italian covers three English forms at once. *Parlo* means "I speak", "I am speaking" and "I do speak". You don't need a helper verb.

## How it works

Take off the ending **-are** and you get the *radice* (stem). Then add the ending that matches the subject.

| | parlare | abitare | lavorare |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | parl**o** | abit**o** | lavor**o** |
| tu | parl**i** | abit**i** | lavor**i** |
| lui / lei | parl**a** | abit**a** | lavor**a** |
| noi | parl**iamo** | abit**iamo** | lavor**iamo** |
| voi | parl**ate** | abit**ate** | lavor**ate** |
| loro | parl**ano** | abit**ano** | lavor**ano** |

Notice two things. First, the *io* and *lui/lei* forms have no *-i*: *parlo*, *parla*. Second, Italian usually drops the subject pronoun, because the ending already tells you who is doing the action: *parlo* is enough for "I speak".

**-care and -gare verbs.** Verbs ending in **-care** (*giocare*, *bussare* is regular, but *cercare*, *spiegare*, *giocare* are the classic ones) keep a hard *c* or *g* sound. To do that, they add an **h** in the *tu* and *noi* forms:

- giocare → io **gioco**, tu **giochi**, lui **gioca**, noi **giochiamo**, voi **giocate**, loro **giocano**
- spiegare → io **spiego**, tu **spieghi**, lui **spiega**, noi **spieghiamo**, voi **spiegate**, loro **spiegano**

**-iare verbs.** Verbs ending in **-iare**, like *mangiare*, keep the *i* in the *tu* and *noi* forms, and drop it in the others:

- mangiare → io **mangio**, tu **mangi**, lui **mangia**, noi **mangiamo**, voi **mangiate**, loro **mangiano**

## From the story

[[«Ok, spiega,» dice Matteo, e prepara un caffè.]]

## Common mistakes

- **Adding an English helper verb.** English speakers say "I am working", so they write *sono lavoro* or *sto lavoro*. In Italian, present tense alone is enough: *lavoro*.
- **Mixing *io* and *lui/lei*.** *Parlo* (I) and *parla* (he/she). The final vowel is the whole message.
- **Forgetting the *h* in *-care* / *-gare*.** *Tu giochi*, not *tu gioci*; *noi spieghiamo*, not *noi spiegiamo* (which would sound like *spiegiamo* with a soft *g*).
- **Dropping the *i* in *mangiare*.** *Mangi*, not *mang*; *mangiamo*, not *mangamo*.
- **Using the wrong family ending.** Learners sometimes say *lavora* for "I work". Remember: *io lavor**o***, *lui lavor**a***.
- **Talking about *io* where the Italian talks about *noi*.** *Giochiamo* means "we play" or "let's play", depending on the situation. Italian often uses the *noi* form where English uses "let's".
