# Irregular Plurals: le ginocchia, le braccia, le dita

## What it is

Most Italian nouns are easy in the plural: *la casa → le case*, *il libro → i libri*. But a small group of very common nouns — and many of them are parts of the body — do not follow the normal rule. They are usually masculine in the singular and **feminine in the plural**, and they end in **-a**:

> *il ginocchio → le ginocchia* · *il braccio → le braccia*

There is also a second group: nouns that **never change at all**. *Il film* stays *il film*, *la città* stays *la città*, whether there is one or ten.

## How it works

### 1. Body parts that change gender

| Singular (m.) | Plural (f.) |
|---|---|
| il braccio | le **braccia** |
| il ginocchio | le **ginocchia** |
| il dito | le **dita** |
| il labbro | le **labbra** |
| l'osso | le **ossa** |

The plural is feminine, so it takes **le** and any adjective must be feminine too: *le braccia lunghe*, *le ginocchia stanche*.

### 2. Some everyday nouns do this too

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| l'uovo (m.) | le **uova** (f.) |
| il paio | le **paia** |
| il miglio | le **miglia** |

### 3. Nouns that never change

Nouns ending in a stressed vowel, in a consonant, or borrowed from other languages stay exactly the same:

- *la città → le città*
- *il film → i film*
- *il bar → i bar*
- *il re → i re*
- *il caffè → i caffè*

You know they are plural from the article and the verb: *Le città sono grandi*.

### 4. A warning about *dito*

*Il dito* has two plurals. **Le dita** means "the fingers" as a whole, on a hand. *I diti* is used only for separate fingers: *i diti mignoli*. In everyday Italian, use **le dita**.

## From the story

**Le ginocchia** fanno un rumore strano, come una porta vecchia. — His **knees** make a strange noise, like an old door. (`s03e19-1-008`) — *le ginocchia*: masculine singular, feminine plural, with the article *le*.

Si tocca **le ginocchia** con **le dita**. — He touches his **knees** with his **fingers**. (`s03e19-1-015`) — two irregular plurals in one line, both feminine with *le*.

«E che **le dita** non chiudono bene» dice Nadia. — “And that his **fingers** don't close well,” says Nadia. (`s03e19-4-016`) — *le dita* here means the fingers of one hand, as a group.

«Le dita no. Sono vecchie» dice Franco. — “My fingers, no. They're old,” says Franco. (`s03e19-2-035`) — the adjective *vecchie* is feminine plural, agreeing with *le dita*, not with the singular *il dito*.

Nel tegame ci sono due uova. — In the pan there are two eggs. (`s03e19-1-004`) — *l'uovo* becomes *le uova*: a change of gender and of ending.

«Le **braccia** sì. Le ginocchia no» dice Chiara. — “His **arms**, yes. His knees, no,” says Chiara. (`s03e19-3-068`) — the two most frequent body irregulars, side by side, both with *le*.

«Nella **vecchiaia** le mani cambiano» dice Nadia. — “In **old age** the **hands** change,” says Nadia. (`s03e19-6-054`) — *la mano* looks feminine in the singular and stays feminine in the plural: *le mani*.

«In **città** tutti hanno un dottore» dice Franco. «Io no». — “In the **city** everyone has a doctor,” says Franco. “Me, no.” (`s03e19-2-051`) — *la città* never changes; the plural would still be *le città*.

«I dottori parlano sempre di un film che non ho visto» dice Franco. — “Doctors always talk about a movie I haven't seen,” says Franco. (`s03e19-2-090`) — *il film* is invariable: *un film*, *due film*, *i film*.

Franco la guarda. **Le labbra** si muovono, ma non esce niente. — Franco looks at her. His **lips** move, but nothing comes out. (`s03e19-6-086`) — *le labbra*, feminine plural, with the verb *si muovono* in the plural.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Using -i for everything.** *\*le ginocchi*, *\*i bracci* (when you mean both arms) and *\*i diti* are wrong. Learn these nouns as pairs: *il ginocchio / le ginocchia*.
2. **Keeping the masculine article.** *\*i ginocchia* is wrong because the plural is feminine: **le** ginocchia.
3. **Making the adjective masculine.** *\*le braccia lunghi* → *le braccia lunghe*. The adjective follows the plural, not the singular.
4. **Pluralizing invariable nouns.** *\*le città**s***, *\*i films*, *\*due caffès* are all wrong. *Due film*, *tre città*, *due caffè*.
5. **Confusing *le dita* and *le ditte*.** *La ditta* means "the company" and its plural is *le ditte*. Fingers are *le dita*.

## Practice

Complete each sentence with the correct plural form.

1. Franco ha due problemi: **le ______** (il ginocchio).
2. Leo alza **le ______** (il braccio) come una bandiera.
3. Nadia guarda **le ______** (il dito) di Franco.
4. Per la frittata servono sei **______** (l'uovo).
5. **Le ______** (la mano) di Franco sono calde.
6. In **______** (la città) tutti hanno un dottore.
7. Ieri sera abbiamo visto due **______** (il film).
8. Le tue **______** (il labbro) si muovono, ma non parli.

### Answer key

1. le ginocchia
2. le braccia
3. le dita
4. sei uova
5. le mani
6. in città
7. due film
8. le labbra
