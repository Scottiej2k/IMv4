# Review: the passato prossimo and pronouns

## What it is

By now you have two big tools for telling a story in Italian: the **passato prossimo** ("I did", "I have done") and **pronouns** — the little words that replace nouns so you don't repeat them a hundred times. This chapter puts them together, because in real Italian they almost always work as a team: *Il permesso? **L'ho preso** io.* ("The permit? **I got** it.")

In this episode the whole street tells the story of the renovation, and everybody tells it differently. That's what the passato prossimo is for: recounting what happened.

## How it works

**1. Passato prossimo = avere or essere + past participle.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| lavorare | **ho lavorato** | I **worked** |
| aprire | **hanno aperto** | they **opened** |
| arrivare | **siamo arrivati** | we **arrived** |
| uscire | **è uscita** | she **went out** |

Verbs with **essere** agree with the subject: *Emma **è uscita***, *i muratori **sono arrivati***.

**2. Irregular participles** you already know: *aperto, rotto, preso, messo, perso, visto, fatto, detto, scritto, stato, dipinto*.

**3. Reflexives** take essere: *mi sono svegliato, ci siamo conosciuti*.

**4. Direct object pronouns** (lo, la, li, le) go **before** the verb, and the participle agrees with them.

- *Il permesso? **L'ho preso** io.*
- *La mensola? **L'ho dipinta** io.*
- *I muratori? **Li ho chiamati** io.*
- *Le mele? **Le ho portate** a Ornella.*

**5. Indirect object pronouns** (mi, ti, gli, le, ci, vi) also go before the verb, with no agreement.

- ***Ti ho sentito** russare.*
- ***Gli abbiamo parlato** ieri.*
- ***Ci hanno detto** tutto.*

**6. Ne** replaces "of it / of them", usually a quantity: ***Ne ho parlato** con il comune.* ***Ne** hai prese due.*

**7. Ci** means "there": ***Ci sono voluti** otto mesi.* ("It took eight months.") You sometimes see **ce ne** together: *Ce ne vuole ancora.*

## From the story

«Kevin, **com'è andata** quest'anno?» chiede. — “Kevin, **how did it go** this year?” Chiara asks. (`s02e25-1-043`) — the fixed question you use to ask how something went.
«L'ho **dipinta** io, da sola, dopo cena.» — “I **painted** it myself, alone, after dinner.” (`s02e25-1-019`) — *la* replaces *la mensola*, and the participle agrees: *dipinta*.
«Sì, l'ho **dipinta** io. **Ne** sono contenta» risponde. — “Yes, I **painted** it. I'm happy **about it**,” Chiara answers. (`s02e25-1-021`) — *lo/la* for the object, *ne* for "about it".
«Il tubo ha perso acqua per una settimana» dice. — “The pipe leaked water for a week,” Kevin says. (`s02e25-1-029`) — an irregular participle (*perso*) with *avere*.
«Io ho visto tutto dalla mia finestra» dice. — “I saw everything from my window,” she says. (`s02e25-4-040`) — with *avere* the participle never changes, even with *io*.
«Ti ho guardata tutto l'anno» dice. — “I watched you all year,” Tommaso says. (`s02e25-6-016`) — *ti* is the direct object, so the participle agrees with the person: *guardata*.
«E **l'abbiamo asciugato** insieme, il **pavimento**» dice. — “And **we dried** it together, the **floor**,” Chiara says. (`s02e25-5-035`) — *lo* for *il pavimento*, and the participle stays masculine.
«**Ce ne** vuole ancora, di acqua nel **secchio**» dice. — “**It takes** more water in the **bucket**,” Kevin says. (`s02e25-1-026`) — *ce* ("there") + *ne* ("of it").
«**Sono venuto** lo stesso» dice. — “**I came** anyway,” Franco says. (`s02e25-5-054`) — a movement verb with *essere*, so the participle agrees with *io*.

## Common mistakes

- **Wrong auxiliary.** Movement verbs like *andare, arrivare, uscire, partire, tornare, venire* take **essere**: *sono venuto*, not *ho venuto*.
- **Agreement with avere.** With *avere* the participle never agrees with the *subject*: *Chiara ha chiamato*, not *Chiara ha chiamata* — but it **does** agree with a direct object pronoun that comes first: *L'ho **dipinta***.
- **Forgetting the agreement with lo/la/li/le.** *La bottiglia? **L'ho presa** io.* With *la*, the participle ends in *-a*.
- **Putting the pronoun after the verb.** In normal sentences it goes before: *Ti ho visto*, not *Ho visto ti*.
- **Mixing up ne and ci.** *Ne* = "of it / them" (a quantity). *Ci* = "there". *Ce ne vuole ancora* uses both, because both meanings are present.
- **Using the imperfetto too early.** In this lesson everything is a completed action: *ho aperto il muro*, not *aprivo*.

## Practice

1. Complete with *avere* or *essere*: *I muratori ___ aperto il muro sbagliato.*
2. Complete with *avere* or *essere*: *Emma ___ uscita in giardino.*
3. Replace the object with a pronoun (*lo, la, li, le*): *Hai preso il permesso?* → *___*
4. Replace the object with a pronoun: *Avete montato le finestre?* → *___*
5. Answer with *ne*: *Quante mele hai preso?* → *___*
6. Complete with *ci*: *___ sono voluti otto mesi.*
7. Complete with *ti* or *mi*: *Kevin, ___ ho sentito russare.*
8. Put the participle in the right form: *La cucina? L'abbiamo ___ (disegnare) io.*

### Answer key

1. *hanno* — *avere*, no agreement.
2. *è* — *uscire* takes *essere*.
3. *L'hai preso?*
4. *Le avete montate?* — agreement with *le*.
5. *Ne ho prese due.*
6. *Ci* sono voluti otto mesi.
7. *Kevin, **ti** ho sentito russare.*
8. *disegnata* — agreement with *la* (la cucina).
