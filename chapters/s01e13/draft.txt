# Simple prepositions: *di, a, da, in, con, su, per, tra/fra*

## What they are

Prepositions are the little words that connect things in a sentence: *the presents go **under** the tree*, *a coffee **for** you*, *I come **with** Leo*. Italian has short, one-word prepositions — that's why they're called **simple** prepositions. The eight you need most are:

**di · a · da · in · con · su · per · tra/fra**

They are small, but they do a lot of work. Most of them can talk about **place**, **origin**, **company** or **purpose**, and the meaning changes with the words around them.

## How they work

| Preposition | Main use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **di** | of, belonging to; origin | un amico **di** Franco | a friend of Franco's |
| **a** | to, at (a place, a time) | vado **a** casa | I'm going home |
| **da** | from; at someone's place | vengo **da** Milano / **da** Franco | I come from Milan / to Franco's |
| **in** | in, into (rooms, countries, streets) | **in** cucina, **in** Italia | in the kitchen, in Italy |
| **con** | with (company, tools) | un caffè **con** Leo | a coffee with Leo |
| **su** | on, on top of | la stella è **su** l'albero | the star is on the tree |
| **per** | for (purpose, recipient) | un regalo **per** te | a present for you |
| **tra / fra** | between, among; in (of time) | **tra** me e te | between you and me |
|  |  | **tra** dieci minuti | in ten minutes |

Two notes:

- **tra** and **fra** mean exactly the same thing. *Tra* is more common before words starting with *tr-*, and *fra* before words starting with *fr-*, but either one is correct.
- **da** is the busiest of all. It means *from* (*il treno da Milano*), *at someone's place* (*vado da Franco* = I go to Franco's house), and it also introduces a period of time (*studio italiano da settembre*).

## From the story

[[I regali vanno sotto l'albero.]]
*Sotto* = under. Here it shows **place**: where the presents end up.

[[Tra l'albero e il presepe, facciamo tutti e due.]]
*Tra … e …* = between … and. Two things, one in the middle.

[[Da noi il presepe è la tradizione vera.]]
*Da noi* = at our house, in our family. This *da* is not "from" — it means "where we live / how we do things".

[[Le candele sono per la sera.]]
*Per* = for. It shows **purpose**: the candles exist for the evening.

[[La stella è su un ramo, in alto.]]
*Su* = on top of, with contact. And *in alto* = up high.

[[Il gatto è sotto la finestra.]]
Another **place** preposition. Notice the pattern: *sotto* and *su* are the two opposites.

[[La lezione di ballo è tra dieci minuti.]]
*Tra* + a time = in (how long from now). Very useful and very common.

[[Un pacchetto per ogni regalo?]]
*Per* again, this time per recipient: one package for each present.

[[Leo corre in giardino con il pane in mano.]]
*In giardino* = into the garden (movement into a space), and *con il pane* = with the bread (what he's carrying).

[[La bugia resta tra noi.]]
*Tra noi* = between us. A small, confidential phrase you'll hear a lot.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Leaving the preposition out.** English says *I'm going home*; Italian says *vado **a** casa*. English says *a friend of mine*; Italian says *un amico **di** me* more rarely — usually *un mio amico*, but with names it's *un amico **di** Franco*. Small words, but they must be there.
2. **Using *a* where Italian uses *in*.** Cities take **a** (*a Milano*), but countries, regions, rooms and streets take **in** (*in Italia*, *in cucina*, *in Via dei Tigli*). This is a common slip.
3. **Forgetting the article after the preposition.** *Con pane* is wrong; you say *con **il** pane*. Same for *per il regalo*, *sotto la finestra*.
4. **Translating "for" always as *per*.** *Per* is for purpose and recipient (*un regalo per te*). But "for three years" is *da tre anni*, not *per tre anni*.
5. **Mixing up *da* and *di* for origin.** *Da* answers *where from* for travel and people: *vengo da Milano*. *Di* answers *whose / of what*: *la porta di casa*. When in doubt, ask yourself: movement and place, or belonging?
6. **Putting *su* where English says "up".** Italian uses *in alto* for "up high" and *su* for "on": *la stella è su un ramo, in alto*.

### Answer key

1. **su** — *su l'albero* (on top of it). Up high would be *in alto*.
2. **per** — recipient.
3. **da** — origin, where you come from.
4. **tra** (or **fra**) — between two things.
5. **da** — *da* + person's name = at their house.
6. **con** — company. Matteo and the panettone, together.
7. **tra** (or **fra**) — *tra* + time = in, from now.
8. **per** — purpose and recipient.
