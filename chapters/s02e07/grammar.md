# Object Pronouns with the Passato Prossimo

## What it is

In Italian, when you talk about something you've already mentioned, you don't repeat its name. You replace it with a small word called a **direct object pronoun**: *lo, la, li, le*. These mean "him / it", "her / it", "them" (masculine), "them" (feminine).

So instead of saying:

*Ho cercato la fede.* → *Ho cercato la fede dappertutto.* → *L'ho cercata dappertutto.*

you say *L'ho cercata dappertutto* — "I looked for it everywhere". The little word *la* (the ring) is now in front of the verb, and — this is the key thing — it changes the past participle. *Cercato* becomes *cercata*, to match the feminine *la*.

This is the grammar of this chapter, and it's exactly what the characters use when they hunt for the ring, the keys and the books.

## How it works

### The four pronouns

| Pronoun | Replaces | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **lo** | a masculine singular noun (*il libro*) | him / it |
| **la** | a feminine singular noun (*la fede*) | her / it |
| **li** | a masculine plural noun (*i libri*) | them |
| **le** | a feminine plural noun (*le chiavi*) | them |

### Where they go

The pronoun comes **before** the verb:

*Non trovo **le chiavi**.* → *Non **le** trovo.*
*Ho perso **le chiavi**.* → ***Le** ho perse.*

*Lo* and *la* shorten to **l'** before a vowel:

*Ho visto **il film**.* → ***L'**ho visto.*
*Ho trovato **la fede**.* → ***L'**ho trovata.*

### The participle must agree

With the passato prossimo and *avere*, the participle normally ends in **-o**. But when *lo, la, li, le* come **before** it, the participle becomes an adjective and matches the pronoun:

| Pronoun | Participle ending | Example |
|---|---|---|
| lo | -o | *Il libro? **L'ho letto**.* |
| la | -a | *La fede? **L'ho trovata**.* |
| li | -i | *I libri? **Li ho comprati**.* |
| le | -e | *Le chiavi? **Le ho perse**.* |

Only these four pronouns cause the change. *Ho mangiato la pizza* stays *Ho mangiato la pizza* if nothing is replaced.

### Negative and questions

Nothing special happens: the pronoun still sits before the verb.

*Non **l'ho vista**.* — I haven't seen it.
***L'hai vista**?* — Have you seen it?
***Le hai cercate**?* — Did you look for them?

### Nouns that change the pronoun

The pronoun matches the **noun**, not the person speaking:

*Ho nascosto **l'anello*** (m. sing.) → ***L'ho nascosto**.*
*Ho nascosto **la fede*** (f. sing.) → ***L'ho nascosta**.*
*Ho nascosto **i manuali*** (m. plur.) → ***Li ho nascosti**.*
*Ho nascosto **le chiavi*** (f. plur.) → ***Le ho nascoste**.*

## From the story

«**L'hai vista**?» chiede Franco. — “**Have you seen it**?” asks Franco. (`s02e07-1-022`) — *la fede* is feminine singular, so *la* becomes *l'* and the participle is *vista*, not *visto*.

«**L'ho cercata** dappertutto» dice Franco. — “**I've looked for it** everywhere,” says Franco. (`s02e07-1-034`) — the same agreement, from Franco: he looked for it (the ring) everywhere.

«E **non l'abbiamo trovata**» dice Kevin. — “And **we didn't find it**,” says Kevin. (`s02e07-2-006`) — negative sentence, pronoun still before the verb, participle still feminine.

«Non lo so» dice Emma. «**Le ho perse** ieri.» — “I don't know,” says Emma. “**I lost them** yesterday.” (`s02e07-4-008`) — *le chiavi* is feminine plural, so *le* and the ending *-e*.

«**Le ho cercate** dappertutto» dice Emma. «**Le ho cercate** in camera, **Le ho cercate** in cucina.» — “**I looked for them** everywhere,” says Emma. “**I looked for them** in my room, **I looked for them** in the kitchen.” (`s02e07-4-010`) — the same pronoun with a different verb; the agreement stays.

«Tre **libri**» dice Matteo. «**Li ho comprati** online lunedì.» — “Three **books**,” says Matteo. “**I bought them** online Monday.” (`s02e07-3-025`) — *i libri* is masculine plural: *li* + participle in *-i*.

«**L'ho nascosta** io» dice Franco. «**L'ho nascosta** due settimane fa.» — “**I hid it** myself,” says Franco. “**I hid it** two weeks ago.” (`s02e07-5-057`) — "I hid it": the pronoun tells you the hidden thing is feminine (the ring, or the books in Nadia's case).

«Sì» dice Nadia. «**L'ho ordinata** ieri al negozio in piazza.» — “Yes,” says Nadia. “**I ordered it** yesterday at the shop in the piazza.” (`s02e07-7-040`) — *la culla* is feminine, so *ordinata*.

«**L'abbiamo trovato** ieri» dice. «Nel **cemento**.» — “**We found him** yesterday,” he says. “In the **cement**.” (`s02e07-7-058`) — here the thing found is *il gatto*, masculine: *trovato*, with no change.

«Pavarotti» dice. «**Ti sei nascosto** nel **cemento**.» — “Pavarotti,” he says. “**You hid** in the **cement**.” (`s02e07-5-033`) — this one is different: with *essere* (reflexive verbs), the participle already agrees with the subject, not with an object pronoun.

## Common mistakes

**1. Forgetting the agreement.** English speakers say *L'ho visto* for everything. If the thing is feminine or plural, you must change the ending: *L'ho **vista***, *Li ho **presi***, *Le ho **prese***.

**2. Putting the pronoun after the verb.** *Ho lo visto* and *Ho le perse* are wrong. The pronoun goes first: ***L'ho visto***, ***Le ho perse***.

**3. Writing *lo ho* and *la ho*.** These always contract: ***l'ho***, not *lo ho*.

**4. Agreeing with the speaker instead of the object.** A woman saying *Ho perso le chiavi* still says ***Le** ho perse*, because *le* refers to the keys — not to her.

**5. Using the pronoun when there's no noun to replace.** *Ho visto lui* is a different structure (a stressed pronoun), taught later. For now: replace a noun you've already mentioned, or one your listener knows.

**6. Mixing up *li* and *le*.** *Li* is masculine plural, *le* is feminine plural. Ask yourself: *il libro* or *la chiave*?
