# Narrating in the past: review

## What it is

When Italians tell a story — a fairy tale, a family anecdote, how two people met — they mix **two past tenses** in the same breath. The **imperfetto** paints the background: what things were like, what used to happen, how someone felt. The **passato prossimo** moves the story forward: what happened, once, at a specific moment.

Think of it as a stage. The imperfetto builds the set and the mood; the passato prossimo is the action that happens on that stage.

## How it works

**Imperfetto** — background, description, habits, feelings, things in progress:

- *Era novembre. Faceva freddo.* (It was November. It was cold.)
- *Da ragazzo venivo qui ogni giorno.* (As a boy I used to come here every day.)
- *All'inizio non volevo niente.* (At the beginning I didn't want anything.)

**Passato prossimo** — single finished events, one after another:

- *E poi ho conosciuto Anna.* (And then I met Anna.)
- *A un certo punto lui è arrivato alla siepe.* (At a certain point he came to the hedge.)

**Time sequencing.** Stories need signposts, and Italian ones are easy:

| Italian | English | Use |
|---|---|---|
| c'era una volta | once upon a time | opens a story or a fairy tale |
| all'inizio | at the beginning | the first situation |
| poi / e poi | then / and then | the next event |
| dopo | after | the next event |
| a un certo punto | at a certain point | the turning point |
| alla fine | in the end, finally | the last event |

Notice that *all'inizio* and *alla fine* often go with the imperfetto (the situation before and after), while *poi* and *a un certo punto* usually bring a passato prossimo event.

**Two little details.** *C'era* is the imperfetto of *c'è* and means "there was." And verbs of movement, arrival and change (*arrivare, andare, uscire, nascere, piacere*) take **essere** in the passato prossimo, so the participle agrees: *è arrivato / è arrivata*.

## From the story

«Da ragazzo **venivo** qui ogni giorno con mio padre.» — “As a boy I **used to come** here every day with my father.” (`s03e24-1-087`) — Imperfetto for a repeated habit in the past: every day, again and again.

«E poi **ho conosciuto** Anna.» — “And then I **met** Anna.” (`s03e24-1-089`) — A single event in the passato prossimo, moved forward by *poi*.

«**All'inizio** non volevo niente.» — “**At the beginning** I didn't want anything.” (`s03e24-1-078`) — Imperfetto for a feeling or state at the beginning of the story.

«**All'inizio** ci salutavamo. Ogni mattina, sul treno.» — “**At the beginning** we said hi. Every morning, on the train.” (`s03e24-2-013`) — Imperfetto for something the two of them did repeatedly.

«Era novembre. Faceva freddo.» — “It was November. It was cold.” (`s03e24-2-047`) — Pure description: the imperfetto builds the background, with no event at all.

«**C'era una volta** una ragazza americana che arrivava a Borgoverde.» — “**Once upon a time** there was an American girl who arrived in Borgoverde.” (`s03e24-5-018`) — The classic story opener: *c'era* plus imperfetto.

«**A un certo punto**, in novembre, lui è arrivato alla siepe.» — “**At a certain point**, in November, he came to the hedge.” (`s03e24-5-047`) — The turning point, with an *-are* verb that takes *essere*.

«E poi mi hai parlato.» — “And then you talked to me.” (`s03e24-5-066`) — A single moment, passato prossimo with *avere*.

«Questa settimana mi è uscita una **lacrima**.» — “This week a **tear** came out of me.” (`s03e24-1-053`) — *Uscire* takes *essere*; the subject follows the verb.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the passato prossimo for description.** *Era novembre* is right; *È stato novembre* is not. If nothing "happens," use the imperfetto.
- **Using the imperfetto for a single event.** *Alla fine lui è arrivato* (once), not *alla fine lui arrivava*.
- **Wrong auxiliary with movement verbs.** Say *è arrivata*, not *ha arrivato*; *sono andato*, not *ho andato*.
- **Forgetting agreement with *essere*.** A woman says *sono arrivata*; two people say *siamo arrivati*.
- **Translating "then" as *allora*.** *Allora* means "so / at that time." For the next event in a story, use *poi* or *dopo*.
- **Saying *c'era* for everything.** *C'erano due persone* — *c'era* becomes *c'erano* with a plural.

## Practice

Complete each sentence with the imperfetto or the passato prossimo.

1. Da piccolo mio nonno ______ (andare) al lavoro in tram.
2. Ieri sera Lucia ______ (arrivare) alle otto.
3. ______ (essere) gennaio e ______ (fare) freddo.
4. **All'inizio** Emma non ______ (volere) stare in Italia.
5. **A un certo punto** Tommaso ______ (parlare) con lei.
6. Noi ______ (vedersi) ogni mattina sul treno.
7. **Alla fine** Franco ______ (dire) la frase giusta.
8. ______ una volta un **cavaliere** con le ginocchia stanche.

**Answer key**

1. andava — habit in the past.
2. è arrivata — one finished event, with *essere*.
3. Era … faceva — description, no event.
4. voleva — a feeling at the beginning.
5. ha parlato — a single turning point.
6. ci vedevamo — a repeated action.
7. ha detto — the final event.
8. C'era — the classic story opener.
