# Adverbs of Frequency and Time Expressions

## What it is

Adverbs of frequency are the little words that say *how often* something happens: **sempre** (always), **spesso** (often), **qualche volta** (sometimes), **di solito** (usually), **raramente** (rarely), **mai** (never). Italian uses them the same way English does, but their position in the sentence is stricter — and one of them, *mai*, has a rule of its own. In this chapter you also meet **time expressions**: **ogni giorno** (every day), **prima** (first / before) and **poi** (then / after), plus the weather phrases that describe what the sky is doing right now.

## How it works

### 1. Where the adverb goes

Most frequency adverbs go **after the verb**:

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Io **lavoro** **spesso** a Milano. | I **often** work in Milan. |
| Tu **pulisci** **raramente** la cucina. | You **rarely** clean the kitchen. |
| Il gatto **scivola** **qualche volta**. | The cat **sometimes** slips. |

*Di solito* and *sempre* are more flexible. *Di solito* very often comes at the **beginning**:

- **Di solito** il treno è in ritardo. (Usually the train is late.)
- Il treno è **di solito** in ritardo. (also correct)

### 2. Mai needs non

**Mai** is negative, and Italian is not afraid of two negatives in one sentence. Before the verb you need **non**:

- Leo **non** capisce **mai**. (Leo never understands.)
- Io **non** bevo **mai** il caffè freddo. (I never drink iced coffee.)

*Mai* can also stand alone as an answer: *«Bevi il caffè freddo?» «Mai!»*

### 3. Ogni giorno, prima, poi

**Ogni giorno** works like an adverb and usually goes at the start or at the end:

- **Ogni giorno** Kevin guarda il tempo sul telefono.
- Kevin guarda il tempo sul telefono **ogni giorno**.

**Prima** and **poi** put two actions in order. They often appear together, as a little formula:

- **Prima** il caffè, **poi** la neve. (First the coffee, then the snow.)

### 4. The weather: fare, c'è, è

Weather does not use "it is" the way English does. Italian chooses:

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| **fa** + cold/hot | **Fa freddo.** **Fa caldo.** |
| a verb by itself | **Piove.** **Nevica.** |
| **c'è** + noun | **C'è** la nebbia. **C'è** il ghiaccio. **C'è** il sole. |

Never say *È freddo* about the weather: say **Fa freddo**. For snow and rain there is no "it": just **nevica**, **piove**.

## From the story

[[In Via dei Tigli la neve è una cosa rara.]] — A plain statement with *essere*, the frame for the frequency adverbs that follow. (Note: here *è* is fine, because it describes *la neve*, not the weather in general.)

[[Ogni giorno Kevin guarda il tempo sul telefono.]] — *Ogni giorno* at the start of the sentence, then the present tense.

[[Emma, quando nevica il treno arriva spesso in ritardo.]] — *Spesso* sits neatly after the verb *arriva*.

[[Il treno è in ritardo. Di solito è in ritardo.]] — *Di solito* used at the start of the second sentence, echoing the first *(in ritardo)*. Repetition is how Italian sounds natural here.

[[Fa freddo. Non "è freddo". Fa freddo, l'americano.]] — The weather rule said out loud: *fa freddo*, never *è freddo*.

[[Raramente sono a casa di giorno.]] — *Raramente* in front of the verb, for emphasis. A private thought, so it sounds like a confession.

[[In primavera piove spesso.]] — *Piove* alone, no subject, plus *spesso* after it.

[[Qualche volta due parole.]] — *Qualche volta* used as a short, complete answer, with no verb at all.

[[Prima il caffè, poi la neve.]] — The *prima… poi* formula that closes the first scene.

[[E non capisci mai.]] — *Non… mai* around the verb: the classic double negative that is perfectly correct in Italian.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

- **"È freddo" for the weather.** English says "it's cold"; Italian says **fa freddo**. Keep *è freddo* for things (this coffee is cold → *questo caffè è freddo*).
- **Putting *spesso* before the verb.** *Spesso lavoro a Milano* is possible but marked; the neutral order is *lavoro spesso*.
- **Forgetting *non* with *mai*.** *Capisco mai* is wrong. It is **non capisco mai** — and *mai* stays after the verb.
- **"Sempre" at the end of the sentence.** *Vado sempre al bar*, not *vado al bar sempre*, is the normal order.
- **Adding "it" to *piove* and *nevica*.** There is no *lui* or *esso*: **Piove**, **Nevica**, full stop.
- **Confusing *prima* and *poi*.** *Prima* is "before / first"; *poi* is "then / afterwards". *Prima il pane, poi il caffè.*

## Practice

1. Put the adverb in the right place: *(spesso)* Io \_\_\_ bevo il caffè al bar.
2. Make it negative with *mai*: Leo capisce. → Leo \_\_\_ capisce \_\_\_.
3. Choose *fa* or *è*: Oggi \_\_\_ freddo. / Questo pane \_\_\_ caldo.
4. Complete with *prima* or *poi*: \_\_\_ la doccia, \_\_\_ la colazione.
5. Translate: "It's raining and there's fog."
6. Translate: "Usually I work at home, but sometimes I go to Milan."
7. Answer with a full sentence: «Pulisci spesso la cucina?» (answer: rarely)
8. Fix the sentence: *Nevica ogni giorno in inverno, e è freddo.*

### Answer key

1. Io **bevo spesso** il caffè al bar.
2. Leo **non** capisce **mai**.
3. Oggi **fa** freddo. / Questo pane **è** caldo.
4. **Prima** la doccia, **poi** la colazione.
5. **Piove e c'è la nebbia.**
6. **Di solito lavoro a casa, ma qualche volta vado a Milano.**
7. **Pulisco raramente la cucina.**
8. **Nevica ogni giorno in inverno, e fa freddo.** (Change *è freddo* to *fa freddo*.)
