# Possessive adjectives: my, your, his, her, our, their

## What it is

Possessive adjectives are the little words that say **whose** something is: *my* family, *your* brother, *our* house. In Italian they are:

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| I | **mio / mia / miei / mie** |
| you (tu) | **tuo / tua / tuoi / tue** |
| he/she (lui/lei) | **suo / sua / suoi / sue** |
| we | **nostro / nostra / nostri / nostre** |
| you plural (voi) | **vostro / vostra / vostri / vostre** |
| they | **loro** (never changes) |

## How it works

**They agree with the thing, not the owner.** In Italian you say *mia madre* — literally "my mother" — but also *sua madre* for a man ("his mother") *and* for a woman ("her mother"). The form is the same, because it agrees with *madre*, not with the person who owns her. So *suo* means both "his" and "her"; context tells you which.

**They behave like normal adjectives:** *o* → masculine singular, *a* → feminine singular, *i* → masculine plural, *e* → feminine plural.

- *mio padre, mia madre, i miei fratelli, le mie sorelle*

**No article with one family member in the singular.** This is the big one. With a singular, unmodified family noun, English and Italian differ:

- **mia** madre, **tuo** zio, **sua** figlia, **nostra** nonna

But you **do** use the article:

- with **loro**: *il loro figlio*, *la loro figlia*
- with **plurals**: *i miei fratelli*, *le mie cugine*
- with a modified noun: *la mia cara madre*
- with *mamma* and *papà* in many regions: *la mia mamma*

**Relationships you should learn as chunks:** *mio padre, mia madre, mio figlio, mia figlia, mio fratello, mia sorella, mio nonno, mia nonna, mio zio, mia zia, mio cugino, mia moglie, mio marito, mio nipote*.

## From the story

«**Mia** madre si chiama Linda. **Mio** padre si chiama Doug.» — “**My** mother's name is Linda. **My** father's name is Doug.” (`s01e12-1-035`)
No article: *madre* and *padre* are singular family nouns, so the article disappears.

«Voi due siete i **miei** figli.» — “You two are **my** children.” (`s01e12-1-080`)
With a plural (*figli*), the article comes back: *i miei figli*.

«I **loro** figli sono i **miei** cugini.» — “**Their** children are **my** cousins.” (`s01e12-1-051`)
*Loro* never changes, but it always takes the article (*i loro figli*).

«**Mia** **figlia** è Chiara. **Tua** **madre**.» — “**My** **daughter** is Chiara. **Your** **mother**.” (`s01e12-2-022`)
*Mia* agrees with *figlia*, *tua* agrees with *madre* — two different owners, same rule.

*Genero* ("son-in-law") is a masculine singular family noun: no article, masculine *mio*.

«Sì. Non ha nipoti. E io sono il **suo** nipote.» — “Yes. She has no grandchildren. And I'm **her** grandson.” (`s01e12-5-036`)
*Suo* here means "her" because the owner is Ornella; the form agrees with *nipote*.

«Il **mio** **cuore** è grande. E adesso ha un nipote **in più**.» — “**My** **heart** is big. And now it has one more grandson **extra**.” (`s01e12-6-027`)
The article is optional with body parts used affectionately, and *mio* still agrees with *cuore*.

«Gino è **tuo** nonno. Ma **solo** per oggi.» — “Gino is **your** grandfather. But **only** for today.” (`s01e12-6-023`)
Another singular family noun with no article: *tuo nonno*, not *il tuo nonno*.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding an article where it isn't needed.** *~~Il mio padre è americano.~~* → *Mio padre è americano.* Remember: one family member, singular, no article.
- **Making *loro* agree.** *~~Loro figli~~*, *~~lora madre~~*. *Loro* is invariable: *i loro figli*, *la loro madre*.
- **Choosing the wrong gender.** *~~Mio madre~~* is wrong because *madre* is feminine: *mia madre*. Look at the noun, not the owner.
- **Forgetting the article with plurals.** *~~Mia sorelle~~* → *le mie sorelle*.
- **Confusing *suo* with "his only".** *Suo* also means "her", "its" and the polite "your": *Suo padre, signora?* ("Your father, ma'am?").

## Practice

Fill in the blank with the correct possessive (add the article where needed).

1. ______ padre si chiama Doug. *(I)*
2. ______ sorella è in Ohio. *(you, singular)*
3. ______ nonno è di Milano. *(she)*
4. ______ figli sono a scuola. *(we)*
5. ______ cugini sono americani. *(they)*
6. ______ nipote è ancora piccolo. *(I)*
7. Leo è ______ fratello. *(she)*
8. ______ nonna si chiama Anna. *(we)*

### Answer key

1. Mio padre 2. Tua sorella 3. Suo nonno 4. I nostri figli 5. I loro cugini 6. Mio nipote 7. suo fratello 8. Nostra nonna
