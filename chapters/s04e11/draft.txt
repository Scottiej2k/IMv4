# Uses of the gerund: *sbagliando s'impara*

## What it is

The **gerundio** is the Italian *-ing* form: *parlando* (speaking), *essendo* (being), *leggendo* (reading). In English, *-ing* words do many jobs — they can be the main verb (*I am reading*), a noun (*reading is fun*), or an adverbial (*by reading, while reading, because of reading*). In Italian, the gerund does **only the last job**: it is never the main verb of a sentence and never a noun on its own.

That's why the gerund is so useful for explaining things: it tells your listener **how**, **why**, **when** or **by what means** something happens. It's the grammar of instructions, recipes and proverbs — which is exactly why this chapter is full of it.

## How it works: forms

| Verb type | Gerund | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *-are* verbs | **-ando** | parlare → **parlando** |
| *-ere* verbs | **-endo** | leggere → **leggendo** |
| *-ire* verbs | **-endo** | capire → **capendo** |

Common irregulars: *essere* → **essendo**, *avere* → **avendo**, *fare* → **facendo**, *dire* → **dicendo**, *bere* → **bevendo**, *stare* → **stando**.

Note: the gerund has no subject of its own. It has one form for everybody: *io parlando, tu parlando, noi parlando* — the form never changes.

## The four main uses

**1. Means / manner — *how?* by doing what?**
*Sbagliando s'impara.* — You learn **by making mistakes**.
*Leggendo ad alta voce, impari la pronuncia.* — **By reading** aloud, you learn pronunciation.

**2. Cause — *why?* because of what?**
*Essendo straniero anch'io, capisco le loro difficoltà.* — **Being** a foreigner too, I understand their difficulties.
*Essendo tardi, siamo andati via.* — **Since it was** late, we left.

**3. Time — *when?* while doing what?**
*Mescolando piano, si aspetta dieci minuti.* — **While stirring** gently, you wait ten minutes.

**4. Topic / framing — *speaking of…***
*Parlando di macchine, il primo passaggio è l'acqua.* — **Speaking of** machines, the first step is the water.

## Pronouns attach to the gerund

Object pronouns glue onto the end of the gerund: *parlandone* (talking about it), *vedendolo* (seeing him/it), *dandoglielo* (giving it to him).
*Parlandone si capisce sempre.* — **By talking about it**, you always understand.

## The subject rule

The gerund's subject must normally be **the same as the main clause's**:
*Parlando con loro, imparo anch'io.* — **I** talk, and **I** learn. One subject, fine.
If the subjects are different, you cannot use the gerund; use a real clause: *Mentre io parlavo, lui ascoltava.*

## From the story

- [[Sbagliando s'impara.]] — The proverb: means ("by making mistakes") with a general subject. It's the chapter in one line.
- [[Essendo straniero anche tu, li capisci meglio di me.]] — Cause: *because you are* a foreigner.
- [[Parlando con loro, imparo anch'io.]] — Means/manner, and a single shared subject.
- [[Parlando di macchine, il primo passaggio è l'acqua.]] — Framing a topic: *speaking of* machines.
- [[Sì. Perché leggendo ad alta voce impari la pronuncia che ti serve di più.]] — Means: *by reading* aloud.
- [[Poi si aggiunge il pomodoro e, mescolando piano, si aspetta dieci minuti.]] — Time: *while stirring* slowly.
- [[Ecco. Spegnendo il fuoco prima, il basilico non perde il profumo.]] — Cause: *by turning off* the fire first, the result follows.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Using the gerund as the main verb.** *Sto cucinando* is correct — that's *stare* + gerund (present progressive). But *Cucinando la pasta* alone is not a sentence. The gerund never carries the tense.
2. **Using the gerund where Italian wants the infinitive.** After prepositions and many verbs, use the infinitive: *invece di parlare* (not *invece di parlando*), *prima di mangiare*, *senza capire*.
3. **Mixing subjects.** *Parlando lui, io ascoltavo* is wrong. If the subjects differ, use *mentre* + a finite verb.
4. **Negative gerunds.** "Not knowing" is *non sapendo* — the *non* goes before the gerund.
5. **Piling them up.** English loves chains (*having been reading, being asked to explain*). Italian prefers one gerund per sentence, and the compound gerund (*avendo fatto*) belongs to a later level.
