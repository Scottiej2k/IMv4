# Fare, andare, venire: three verbs you need every day

## What they are

*Fare*, *andare* and *venire* are three of the most common verbs in Italian, and all three are **irregular** in the present tense. That means you can't build them by simply swapping the ending of the dictionary form; the stems change too. The good news: they're so frequent that you'll meet them in almost every conversation, so they're worth memorizing early.

- **fare** = to do, to make (and many everyday actions)
- **andare** = to go
- **venire** = to come

## How they work: the present tense

Here are the full forms. Learn them as little songs; the rhythm is regular even if the endings aren't the standard ones.

| | **fare** (to do/make) | **andare** (to go) | **venire** (to come) |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | **faccio** | **vado** | **vengo** |
| tu | **fai** | **vai** | **vieni** |
| lui/lei | **fa** | **va** | **viene** |
| noi | **facciamo** | **andiamo** | **veniamo** |
| voi | **fate** | **andate** | **venite** |
| loro | **fanno** | **vanno** | **vengono** |

Notice a couple of things. *Fare* has a doubled **c** in *faccio* and *facciamo*. *Andare* and *venire* both have short forms in the singular (*vado, vai, va; vengo, vieni, viene*) but switch back to the long stem in the plural (*andiamo, andate, vanno; veniamo, venite, vengono*).

## Andare a / in + place

*Andare* almost always needs a preposition before the place you're going to.

- **a** + a city, a village, or a small place: *Vado a Milano. Andiamo a casa. Va a scuola.*
- **in** + a country, a region, or many bigger public places: *Vado in Italia. Vanno in centro. Va in piazza.*
- **in** is also normal with **centro**, **metropolitana**, **banca**, **stazione** in everyday speech: *Andiamo in centro.* *Andiamo in metropolitana.*
- **da** + a person means "to someone's place": *Vado da Franco.* *Andiamo da nonna.*

The same prepositions work with *venire*: *Vieni a Milano?* *Vengo da te.* *Viene da Chicago.*

## Fare + common expressions

*Fare* is a workhorse. Many everyday actions in Italian use *fare* where English uses a different verb.

- **fare colazione** = to have breakfast
- **fare la spesa** = to do the shopping
- **fare una foto** = to take a photo
- **fare un giro** = to take a walk/ride
- **fare un discorso** = to make a speech
- **fare una domanda** = to ask a question
- **fare la doccia** = to take a shower

So if you want to say "I take a photo" in Italian, don't reach for a verb like *prendere*; use *fare*: **Faccio** una foto.

## From the story

[[Oggi andiamo a Milano!]]
*Andare* to a city, with **a**: this is the basic pattern you'll use all the time.

[[Io non vengo. Oggi faccio la spesa e faccio il pranzo.]]
*Venire* says "I'm coming along"; *fare* handles the two chores in one line.

[[Ecco il treno. Presto, saliamo!]]
"Here's the train, let's get on!" — the 1st-person plural *saliamo* works like *andiamo* as an invitation.

[[Il controllore arriva dal fondo del vagone.]]
*Arrivare* and *venire* both describe movement toward the speaker; here you see the ordinary present tense used to narrate.

[[Papà, perché i pomodori? È una scusa per la cena?]]
*Fare* also appears in fixed phrases like *fare una scusa*, "to make an excuse."

[[Franco, hai detto a Emma che fai un corso di storia.]]
*Che fai* = "that you're taking" — the present tense is enough for a current activity.

[[Siamo sotto terra, Leo. Sotto la città.]]
*Siamo* is *essere*, another irregular you already know; *sotto* + *terra* = "underground."

[[Emma, guarda fuori! Ci sono le case, gli alberi…]]
*Guardare* and *essere* working together in the present tense; the same pattern as *guardare davanti a te*.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Using *prendere* or *avere* for *fare*.** *"Prendo una foto"* and *"Ho la colazione"* are wrong. Use *fare*: **Faccio una foto. Faccio colazione.**
2. **Forgetting the preposition after *andare*.** *"Vado Milano"* is not Italian. Say **Vado a Milano** or **Vado in centro**.
3. **Mixing up *a* and *in*.** Cities take *a*: *a Roma, a Borgoverde, a casa*. Countries, regions and many larger places take *in*: *in Italia, in Lombardia, in centro, in piazza*.
4. **Treating *venire* like *to come* with the same word order.** In Italian the subject pronoun is usually left out: *Vengo* (not *Io vengo*), unless you want to stress who exactly is coming: *Vengo anch'io!*
5. **Regularizing the plural stems.** *Andiamo*, *veniamo* and *facciamo* are correct; *andamo*, *venamo* and *famo* are not.
