# Reflexive Verbs: *mi sveglio*, *ti alzi*, *si lava*

## What it is

Some verbs describe something you do **to yourself**. In English we often say *I wash myself* or just *I wash*, *I get up*, *I get dressed*. In Italian these verbs usually carry a small extra word, a **reflexive pronoun**, in front of the verb:

- *svegliarsi* → **mi sveglio** (I wake up)
- *alzarsi* → **ti alzi** (you get up)
- *lavarsi* → **si lava** (he/she washes)

The little word (*mi, ti, si, ci, vi, si*) means *myself, yourself, himself…* It changes with the person. The verb ending also changes, exactly as with any present-tense verb.

Verbs like these are called **reflexive verbs**, and the dictionary form ends in **-si**: *svegliarsi*, *alzarsi*, *lavarsi*, *vestirsi*, *prepararsi*, *addormentarsi*, *chiamarsi*, *sentirsi*, *divertirsi*. They are everywhere in everyday Italian, and the whole morning routine — waking up, washing, getting dressed, going to bed — runs on them.

## How it works

Put the pronoun **before** the verb. Then change the verb ending normally.

| Person | *svegliarsi* | *alzarsi* | *lavarsi* |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | mi sveglio | mi alzo | mi lavo |
| tu | ti svegli | ti alzi | ti lavi |
| lui / lei / Lei | si sveglia | si alza | si lava |
| noi | ci svegliamo | ci alziamo | ci laviamo |
| voi | vi svegliano | vi alzate | vi lavate |
| loro | si svegliano | si alzano | si lavano |

A few points to hold on to:

- **io → mi**, **tu → ti**, **lui/lei → si**, **noi → ci**, **voi → vi**, **loro → si**. *Si* appears twice (he/she *and* they); the verb ending tells you which one.
- The pronouns **mi, ti, si, ci, vi** go **before** the verb: *mi lavo*, not *lavo mi*.
- The subject pronoun (*io, tu…*) is usually left out, because the verb already says who: *mi sveglio alle sei.*
- Add an object when it matters: *mi lavo i denti* (I brush my teeth), *mi lavo le mani* (I wash my hands). Italian says “I wash myself the teeth / the hands”.
- The same verbs without the pronoun mean something else: *lavo la macchina* = I wash the car, *alzo la mano* = I raise my hand. With the pronoun, you are doing it to yourself.
- *chiamarsi* works the same way: *mi chiamo Leo*, *come ti chiami?*

## From the story

Kevin **si sveglia** sempre prima della **sveglia**. — Kevin always **wakes up** before the **alarm clock**. (`s01e18-1-006`) — third person *si*, and the nice repetition of the verb and its noun: the verb *svegliarsi* and the object *la sveglia*.

Chiara **si alza** e va alla finestra. — Chiara **gets up** and goes to the window. (`s01e18-1-015`) — *si alza*: she gets herself up. Two verbs, one person, no subject pronoun needed.

«Terzo: **mi lavo** i **denti**.» — “Third: I **brush** my **teeth**.” (`s01e18-1-029`) — the pronoun *mi* with *io* left out, and the object *i denti*.

«Adesso io **mi lavo** e **mi vesto**.» — “Now I **wash** and **get dressed**.” (`s01e18-1-056`) — the pronoun is repeated with each verb: *mi lavo... mi vesto*.

«Io **mi preparo** bene, tu **ti prepari** bene, tutti **si preparano** bene.» — “I **get ready** well, you **get ready** well, everyone **gets ready** well.” (`s01e18-1-034`) — the same verb in three persons: *mi*, *ti*, *si*.

Franco **si lava** le mani con calma. — Franco **washes** his hands calmly. (`s01e18-5-095`) — *si lava le mani*: he washes his own hands.

Poi **si veste** con il **pigiama**. — Then she **gets dressed** in her **pajamas**. (`s01e18-6-036`) — *vestirsi*, to get dressed; the pronoun is *si* for he or she.

Emma va in bagno e **si lava**. — Emma goes into the bathroom and **washes**. (`s01e18-6-035`) — *lavarsi* with no object: she washes herself.

Pavarotti **si addormenta** anche lui. — Pavarotti **falls asleep** too. (`s01e18-5-110`) — the cat falls asleep too.

Leo **si addormenta** sul divano. — Leo **falls asleep** on the sofa. (`s01e18-6-024`) — *addormentarsi*: to fall asleep, third person singular.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

- **Leaving the pronoun out.** *Sveglio alle sei* is wrong for “I wake up at six”; you need *mi sveglio alle sei*. Without it, you're waking someone else.
- **Putting the pronoun after the verb.** It goes before: *ti vesti*, not *vesti ti*.
- **Using the wrong pronoun for the person.** *Tu si svegli* is wrong; *tu ti svegli*. Match the pronoun with the ending.
- **Adding *io* / *tu* when it isn't needed.** *Io mi sveglio* is fine for emphasis, but *mi sveglio* is the normal way.
- **Saying *mi lavo i denti* as *lavo i denti*.** If it's your own teeth, the pronoun belongs there.

## Practice

Make each sentence reflexive with the right pronoun, or correct it.

1. (io) ______ sveglio alle sei e mezza.
2. Luca ______ alza sempre tardi la domenica.
3. Voi ______ preparate la colazione?
4. Correggi: Emma si lava i denti. (Change it to *io*.)
5. Correggi: Tu si veste in tre minuti.
6. Completa: Noi ______ (alzarsi) alle sette.
7. Completa: I bambini ______ (addormentarsi) alle otto.
8. Traduci: “I wash my hands.”

### Answer key

1. **mi** sveglio alle sei e mezza.
2. Luca **si** alza sempre tardi la domenica.
3. Voi **vi** preparate la colazione?
4. **Io mi lavo i denti.** (pronoun *mi* with *io*)
5. **Tu ti vesti in tre minuti.** (*ti*, not *si*)
6. Noi **ci alziamo** alle sette.
7. I bambini **si addormentano** alle otto.
8. **Mi lavo le mani.**
