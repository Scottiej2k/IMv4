# Sapere and conoscere in the past: *sapevo* vs *ho saputo*

## What it is

Italian has two verbs where English often uses just one. **Sapere** means *to know a fact*, and **conoscere** means *to know a person, a place, or a thing — to be acquainted with it*.

Both verbs change meaning depending on the tense you put them in.

- **Imperfetto** (*sapevo*, *conoscevo*) = a state that was already true. "I knew it (all along)."
- **Passato prossimo** (*ho saputo*, *ho conosciuto*) = a moment when something started. "I found out." / "I met."

This is the same contrast you already know from *potevo* vs *ho potuto* and *volevo* vs *ho voluto*: the imperfetto describes a situation, the passato prossimo describes an event.

## How it works

**Sapere** (to know a fact)

| Person | Imperfetto | Passato prossimo |
|---|---|---|
| io | sapevo | ho saputo |
| tu | sapevi | hai saputo |
| lui/lei | sapeva | ha saputo |
| noi | sapevamo | abbiamo saputo |
| voi | sapevate | avete saputo |
| loro | sapevano | hanno saputo |

- *Lo **sapevo** da giugno.* — I **knew** it since June. (the knowledge was already there)
- *Due minuti fa **ho saputo** la notizia.* — Two minutes ago I **found out** the news. (the moment I learned it)
- *Perché non **me l'hai** detto?* Note the pronouns with the passato prossimo: *l'ho saputo*, *me l'ha detto*.

The *passato prossimo* of *sapere* is often best translated as *found out*, *heard*, or *learned*.

**Conoscere** (to know a person, to be familiar with a place)

| Person | Imperfetto | Passato prossimo |
|---|---|---|
| io | conoscevo | ho conosciuto |
| tu | conoscevi | hai conosciuto |
| lui/lei | conosceva | ha conosciuto |
| noi | conoscevamo | abbiamo conosciuto |
| voi | conoscevate | avete conosciuto |
| loro | conoscevano | hanno conosciuto |

- *La **conoscevi**?* — **Did you know** her? (Were you acquainted with her?)
- *L'**ho conosciuta** nel 2022.* — I **met** her in 2022. (the first meeting — a single event)
- *Non **conoscevo** nessuna L.* — I **didn't know** any L. (never had any contact with such a person)

**Careful:** *ho conosciuto* is not "I knew for a long time". It means *I met*, the beginning of the acquaintance. If the acquaintance lasted, use the imperfetto.

**Potevo / volevo, ho potuto / ho voluto**

Exactly the same logic:

- *Non **potevo** parlare.* — I **couldn't** speak. (I was not able to, it wasn't possible)
- *Non **ho potuto** parlare.* — I **couldn't** speak. (I tried, and the attempt failed)
- *Non **volevo** litigare.* — I **didn't want** to argue. (a state of mind)
- *Non **ho voluto** litigare.* — I **refused** to argue. (a decision, a refusal)

## From the story

[[Io lo sapevo prima]] — *Sapevo* in the imperfetto: Leo's knowledge was already there, before everyone else's.

[[Lo sapevo da ieri sera]] — *Da* + time with the imperfetto: the knowledge started yesterday evening and was still true.

[[Lei lo sapeva?]] — The same state, asked politely with *Lei*: it means "were you already aware of this?", not "did you find out?".

[[Sì. Lo sapevo.]] — Kevin repeats the imperfetto. He is describing a state, and he even says he knew it since June.

[[Io ho scoperto un segreto]] — *Scoprire* in the passato prossimo marks the exact moment of discovery.

[[Tu conoscevi questa persona?]] — *Conoscevi* in the imperfetto: were you acquainted with her at that time?

[[Io ho conosciuto Franco due anni fa.]] — *Ho conosciuto*: the precise moment Lucia met Franco for the first time.

[[Anna non l'ho conosciuta]] — The direct object pronoun *l'* with the passato prossimo: "I never met Anna", a single event that never happened.

[[No. Non conoscevo nessuna L.]] — Back to the imperfetto: she had no acquaintance at all with anyone called L.

[[Io ho conosciuto Leo quando aveva cinque anni]] — Two tenses in one line: *ho conosciuto* (the meeting, an event) and *aveva* (his age, a description).

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Translating "I knew" as *ho saputo*.** If you already knew something, it is *lo sapevo*. *Ho saputo* means "I found out".
2. **Using *ho conosciuto* for a long acquaintance.** *Ho conosciuto Marco a Roma* = I met Marco in Rome. If you're saying you were friends for years, use *conoscevo*.
3. **Forgetting the pronoun.** *Non l'ho conosciuta* — the *l'* / *lo* / *la* is obligatory, and the participle agrees: *l'ho conosciut**a***.
4. **Mixing up *sapere* and *conoscere* themselves.** *Sapevo* a fact; *conoscevo* a person or a city. *Conosco Milano* = I know Milan (I've been there, I know my way around). *So che Milano è grande* = I know that Milan is a big city.
5. ***Ho saputo che…* + wrong mood.** At this level, keep it simple: *Ho saputo che Emma e Tommaso stanno insieme* — present indicative after *ho saputo*. Subjunctive comes much later.

### Answer key

1. **sapevo** — the knowledge was already there, since June.
2. **ho saputo** — a single moment: I found out.
3. **conoscevi** — acquaintance in the past, not a first meeting.
4. **Ho conosciuto** — the first meeting, a single event.
5. **potevo** — it wasn't possible; a state, not a failed attempt.
6. **ho potuto** — I tried to call and couldn't get through: the attempt failed.
7. **volevo** — a state of mind; the arguing still happened.
8. **ho voluto** — a decision, a refusal to argue.
