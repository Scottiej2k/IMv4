# Pronominal verbs: *farcela*, *andarsene*, *cavarsela*

## What they are

A **pronominal verb** is a verb that always travels with small pronouns — *ce*, *ne*, *la*, *mi*, *ti*, *si* — and whose meaning is different from the plain verb alone. Compare:

- *fare* = to do, to make → *farcela* = to manage, to cope
- *andare* = to go → *andarsene* = to leave, to go away
- *cavare* = to pull out → *cavarsela* = to get by, to manage
- *sentire* = to hear, to feel → *sentirsela* = to feel up to it
- *smettere* = to stop (something) → *smetterla* = to stop doing it

These pronouns are not decoration. *Non faccio più* means "I don't do it any more"; *non ce la faccio più* means "I can't take it any more". The *ce* and the *la* are part of the verb.

## The most useful ones

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **farcela** | to manage, to cope | *Ce la faccio.* — I can do it. |
| **andarsene** | to leave, to go away | *Me ne vado.* — I'm leaving. |
| **cavarsela** | to get by, to manage | *Me la cavo.* — I get by. |
| **prendersela** | to take it personally | *Non prendertela!* — Don't take it personally! |
| **sentirsela** | to feel up to it | *Non me la sento.* — I don't feel up to it. |
| **smetterla** | to stop (doing something) | *Smettila!* — Stop it! |
| **metterci** | to take (time) | *Ci metto un'ora.* — It takes me an hour. |

## How the pronouns change

The pronouns match the subject. The verb itself is regular.

|  | farcela | andarsene | cavarsela | metterci |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| io | ce la faccio | me ne vado | me la cavo | ci metto |
| tu | ce la fai | te ne vai | te la cavi | ci metti |
| lui / lei | ce la fa | se ne va | se la cava | ci mette |
| noi | ce la facciamo | ce ne andiamo | ce la caviamo | ci mettiamo |
| voi | ce la fate | ve ne andate | ve la cavate | ci mettete |
| loro | ce la fanno | se ne vanno | se la cavano | ci mettono |

## In the past

In the *passato prossimo* the little pronouns move **in front of** the auxiliary, and the past participle agrees with the subject:

- *Ce l'**ho fatta**!* — I made it!
- *Se n'**è andata**.* — She left.
- *Me la **sono cavata**.* — I got by.
- *Me la **sono presa**.* — I took it personally.
- *Ci **ho messo** due ore.* — It took me two hours.

*Andarsene* takes **essere** and agrees like any movement verb: *me ne sono andat**o*** (man), *me ne sono andat**a*** (woman), *se ne sono andat**i*** (group containing men).

## With commands and infinitives

With an infinitive, the pronouns attach to the end and are written as one word: *andarsene*, *cavarsela*, *sentirsela*, *smetterla*.

In the *tu* command they do the same: ***Smettila**!* (Stop it!), *Non **prendertela**!* (Don't take it personally!), *Non **ti scusare**!* (Don't apologize!).

## From the story

[[Non me la sento di rispondere oggi.]]
*Sentirsela* at the first person: “I don't feel up to it.”

[[Me la cavo.]]
*Cavarsela*: “I get by” — life is hard, but I manage.

[[Non prendertela, Kevin.]]
The negative *tu* command of *prendersela*, with the pronouns attached: “Don't take it personally.”

[[Non ce la faccio più.]]
*Farcela* plus *più*: the situation has gone past the limit.

[[Va bene. La smetto di punirmi.]]
*Smetterla* in the present, with *la* before the verb: “I'll stop punishing myself.”

[[Ho smesso di dormire. Ho smesso di mangiare. Ho smesso di parlare.]]
The past of *smettere*, without pronouns, because here no *la* is needed.

[[Se me ne vado adesso, nessuno lo saprà mai.]]
*Andarsene*: the pronoun *ne* is inside the verb and means “from here”.

[[Me la sono cavata, più o meno.]]
*Cavarsela* in the *passato prossimo*: pronouns before the auxiliary, participle agreed.

[[Non me la prendo con te. Me la prendo con la situazione.]]
The same verb positive and negative: “I'm not taking it out on you, I'm taking it out on the situation.”

[[Ci metto tre minuti in macchina.]]
*Metterci*: “it takes me” — the *ci* does the work of the English “it”.

[[Ce la faccio.]]
The shortest and most useful form of all.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

- **Dropping the pronouns.** *Non faccio più* is not “I can't take it any more”. You need *non **ce la** faccio più*.
- **Putting the pronouns in the wrong place in the past.** It's *ce l'ho fatta*, never *ho ce la fatta*. The pronouns always come before the auxiliary.
- **Using *avere* with *andarsene*.** It takes *essere*: *me ne sono andato*.
- **Forgetting agreement.** A woman says *me ne sono andata*, a mixed group *se ne sono andati*.
- **Confusing *sentirsela* and *sentire*.** *Non me la sento* means “I don't feel up to it”, not “I can't hear it”.
- **Translating literally.** *Cavarsela* is not “to pull oneself out”; it means “to manage, to get by”.
- **Using *ne* and *ci* as if they were optional.** With these verbs, they are the verb.

## Practice

Choose the correct form.

1. (io / farcela) — __________ a finire il lavoro prima di cena.
2. (lui / andarsene) — Perché __________ senza dire niente?
3. (tu / smetterla, command) — __________ di urlare!
4. (noi / cavarsela) — Con il nuovo orario __________ abbastanza bene.
5. (io / sentirsela) — Oggi non __________ di uscire.
6. (voi / metterci) — Quanto __________ per arrivare a Milano?
7. (lei / prendersela) — Non __________: non era una critica.
8. (loro / andarsene, past) — Ieri __________ alle due di notte.

### Answer key

1. **Ce la faccio** — presente di *farcela*, prima persona.
2. **se ne va** — *andarsene*, terza persona singolare.
3. **Smettila** — imperativo di *smetterla*, con il pronome attaccato alla fine.
4. **ce la caviamo** — *cavarsela*, prima persona plurale.
5. **me la sento** — *sentirsela*, prima persona.
6. **ci mettete** — *metterci*, seconda persona plurale.
7. **se la prende** — *prendersela*, terza persona singolare.
8. **se ne sono andati** — *andarsene* al passato, con *essere* e accordo al plurale.
