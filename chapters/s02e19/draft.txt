# Duration: *da*, *per*, *fa*

## What it is

Italian has three little words that English speakers mix up constantly: **da**, **per** and **fa**. They all translate something like "for" or "ago", but they do completely different jobs.

The good news: the choice depends on **which tense you use** and on **whether the action is still going on**. Once you see the pattern, it's mechanical.

- **da** = since / for (and it's still true now)
- **per** = for (a finished block of time)
- **fa** = ago (a point in the past, counted backwards from now)

## How it works

### 1. Present tense + **da** = ongoing

If you started something in the past **and you're still doing it now**, use the present tense plus **da**.

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Abito qui **da** cinquantadue anni. | I've lived here for fifty-two years. |
| Lavoro in un bar **da** due mesi. | I've been working in a bar for two months. |
| Cerco lavoro **da** marzo. | I've been looking for a job since March. |
| **Da quanto tempo** sei presidente? | How long have you been president? |

Note that English uses the present perfect ("I *have* lived"), but Italian uses the **plain present**: *abito*, *lavoro*, *cerco*. "Abito qui da cinquantadue anni" literally says "I live here since fifty-two years" — and that is correct Italian.

**da** works with a length of time (*da due mesi*) or a starting point (*da marzo*, *da lunedì*, *da quando sono arrivato*).

### 2. Passato prossimo + **per** = a finished block of time

If the action is **over**, use the past tense plus **per**.

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Ho lavorato **per** dodici anni in America. | I worked for twelve years in America. |
| Ho guidato il tram **per** trentacinque anni. | I drove the tram for thirty-five years. |
| Ho studiato italiano **per** due anni. | I studied Italian for two years. |

The rule of thumb: **da = still true, per = finished.** Compare:

- Lavoro qui **da** due mesi. (I work here now, and I started two months ago.)
- Ho lavorato lì **per** due anni. (That job is finished.)

### 3. **fa** = ago

**fa** goes **after** the time expression and puts the action in the past. The verb is in the passato prossimo.

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Sono arrivato otto mesi **fa**. | I arrived eight months ago. |
| Ho mangiato due ore **fa**. | I ate two hours ago. |

### 4. The question: **Da quanto tempo…?**

To ask "how long…?", the standard form is **Da quanto tempo + present**:

- **Da quanto tempo** lavori qui?
- **Da quanto tempo** vivi in Italia?

With a past action, use **Quanto tempo fa…?** ("How long ago…?"):

- **Quanto tempo fa** sei arrivato?

## Common mistakes

1. **Using *per* for something ongoing.** *Abito qui per cinque anni* is wrong if you still live there. Say **Abito qui da cinque anni**.
2. **Using *da* with a finished action.** *Ho lavorato da dodici anni in America* suggests you're still there. Say **Ho lavorato per dodici anni in America**.
3. **Using a past tense with *da*.** *Ho abitato qui da due anni* sounds like the story is over. If it's still true, use the present: **Abito qui da due anni**.
4. **Putting *fa* in the wrong place.** It comes *after* the time: *due mesi fa*, not *fa due mesi*.
5. **Translating "since" with *da* + a past tense.** *Da* is fine with *da marzo*, *da lunedì* — just keep the verb in the present if it's still going on.
