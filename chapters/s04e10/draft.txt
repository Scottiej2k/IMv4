# Fare + infinitive: "to have something done"

## What it is

Italian has a very useful pattern: **fare + infinitive**. Instead of doing something yourself, you *make* or *have* someone else do it. English often uses "have" or "get":

- *Mi faccio fare un vestito.* — I'm having a suit made.
- *Faccio riparare la macchina.* — I'm having the car fixed.

In this episode everyone is having things done by other people: Franco has a suit made by his old tailor, he has his hair cut in a salon, Lucia has flowers, hair and dress done by professionals — and only the cake she makes herself.

## How it works

**1. fare + infinitive = "to have something done"**

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| Faccio riparare il tetto. | I'm having the roof fixed. |
| Faccio cucire l'orlo. | I'm having the hem sewn. |
| Facciamo vedere il disegno. | We're showing the design. |

Here *fare* means "to arrange, to order", and the action is done by somebody else. If you want to say **who** does the work, use **da** + person: *Faccio cucire l'orlo dal sarto.* — I'm having the hem sewn by the tailor.

**2. farsi + infinitive = "to have something done for oneself"**

When the person who benefits is the same as the subject, Italian adds the reflexive pronoun **si** (or **mi, ti, ci, vi**):

- *Mi faccio fare un abito.* — I'm having a suit made (for me).
- *Si fa tagliare i capelli.* — He's having his hair cut.
- *Ti fai fare un logo?* — Are you having a logo made?

| Person | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| io | mi faccio | mi faccio tagliare i capelli |
| tu | ti fai | ti fai fare il vestito |
| lui/lei | si fa | si fa fare le scarpe |
| noi | ci facciamo | ci facciamo portare il caffè |
| voi | vi fate | vi fate cucire l'orlo |
| loro | si fanno | si fanno fare le foto |

**3. Far vedere, far sapere**

Two expressions from this chapter are causatives you'll hear every day: **far vedere** ("to show", literally "to make someone see") and **far sapere** ("to let someone know"). With pronouns they become one word: *Mi fa vedere il disegno. Ti faccio sapere domani. Ve lo faccio sapere sabato.*

**Note the pronoun order:** with *fare*, the indirect pronoun (*mi, ti, ci, vi, gli, le*) comes before the verb. Before another pronoun, *mi, ti, ci, vi* become **me, te, ce, ve**: *Ve lo faccio sapere* (I'll let you know it).

## From the story

[[Mi faccio fare un logo!]] — The whole pattern in one line: *fare* + infinitive, with *mi* = for himself.
[[Faccio fare tutto lì.]] — *Faccio fare* = I'm having it all done, no reflexive, no named doer.
[[Mi faccio fare un abito dal mio sarto.]] — Adds **da** + person: who actually makes the suit.
[[Venerdì mi faccio tagliare i capelli.]] — *farsi tagliare*: having something done to your own body.
[[E farsi fare un completo vuol dire pagare qualcuno per farlo.]] — Lucia explains the meaning: paying someone else to do it.
[[Ti faccio sapere domani]] — *far sapere* = to let someone know, with the pronoun in front.
[[Vi faccio sapere io, quando è pronto.]] — *Vi* + *faccio sapere*: I'll let you know, when it's ready.
[[Sì. Faccio fare una prova anche a te.]] — *fare* used for arranging a fitting for someone else.

## Common mistakes

- **"Faccio fare un vestito a me"** → say *Mi faccio fare un vestito*. The reflexive pronoun is what makes it "for myself".
- **"Mi faccio un vestito"** (without *fare*) means "I make myself a dress" — you are sewing it! If the tailor does it, you need two verbs: *mi faccio fare*.
- **"Faccio fare da sarto"** → the person is *dal sarto*, not *da sarto*.
- **"Ti faccio sapere a te"** → redundant. *Ti faccio sapere* is enough; *a te* is added only for emphasis.
- **Word order with two pronouns:** *Ti lo faccio sapere* is wrong. Say *Te lo faccio sapere*.
- Don't confuse *fare* with *lasciare* ("to let"). *Lascio cucire l'orlo* means "I let (someone) sew the hem", a different meaning.
