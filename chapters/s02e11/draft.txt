# Ne: the little word for "of it" and "of them"

## What it is

In English you say *I have three of them* or *I want two*. In Italian, the phrase "of them" / "of it" is usually a tiny word: **ne**. It comes before the verb, and it replaces a quantity phrase like *di cornetti*, *di latte*, *di uova*.

> Non ci sono cornetto → **Non ne** ci sono. (There aren't any **of them**.)

**Ne** never means a person or a single object on its own — it means "some / any / a number **of** something already mentioned."

## How it works

### 1. Ne replaces "di + thing" when a number or quantity is present

The number can be exact (due, tre, cento), vague (molto, poco, abbastanza), or absent altogether (some / any).

| Without ne | With ne |
|---|---|
| Vuoi **dei cornetti**? | Ne vuoi? |
| Ne voglio **due** di cornetti. | **Ne** voglio due. |
| Ci sono **dodici uova**. | Ce **ne** sono dodici. |
| Abbiamo **cento cornetti**. | **Ne** abbiamo cento. |
| Non c'è **caffè**. | Non **ne** c'è. |

Notice that the number or quantity (**due**, **dodici**, **cento**) stays in the sentence. **Ne** only replaces the *di + noun* part.

### 2. Position

Like other short pronouns, **ne** goes **directly before the verb** (or before the infinitive, or attached to it):

- **Ne** voglio due. — I want two of them.
- Ce **ne** sono dodici. — There are twelve of them. (*ci* + *ne* → **ce ne**)
- Vorrei comprar**ne** due. — I'd like to buy two of them.

Note **ce ne**: *ci* (there) plus *ne* become **ce ne**. This is the most common way to say "there are some / there are X of them."

### 3. Ne with the passato prossimo

With a past participle, **ne** still comes before the whole verb. And when **ne** refers to something already counted, the participle agrees with the thing counted — masculine **-i** or feminine **-e**:

- Ho comprato **tre cornetti**. → **Ne** ho comprat**i** tre.
- Ho ordinato **dieci litri** di latte. → **Ne** ho ordinat**i** dieci.
- Ho contato **trecento** cornetti. → **Ne** ho contat**i** trecento.
- Ho mangiato **due uova**. → **Ne** ho mangiat**e** due.

This agreement is the same rule you already know from *l'ho vista* / *li ho visti*: the participle follows the object when a short pronoun comes first.

→ Matteo replaces *di cornetti* with **ne**: "I'll sell three hundred **of them**."

[[Zero. Ce ne sono zero]]
→ **ce ne sono** = "there are (some) of them." Here it means there are none.

[[Mezzo chilo. Ne prendiamo mezzo chilo]]
→ **Ne** replaces *di farina*; the quantity *mezzo chilo* stays.

[[Quattro. Ne uso quattro]]
→ **Ne** replaces *di uova*: "I use four **of them**."

[[Allora: ne abbiamo contati trecento e ne teniamo cento]]
→ Two **ne** in one sentence; past participle **contati** agrees with *cornetti* (masculine plural).

[[Ce ne sono dodici!]]
→ Leo's counting formula: *ci* + *ne* + *sono* + number.

[[Sì. Ne ho ordinati dieci]]
→ **Ne ho ordinati dieci** = "I ordered ten **of them**." Hi, that's the past participle rule in action.

[[Nel msemen ne mettiamo due]]
→ **Ne** with a present-tense verb, replacing *di uova*.

→ Nadia answers her own question, reusing **ne** before **voglio**.

→ **ne** + quantity word *troppi*, agreeing in gender and number with the hidden noun.

→ **Ne** with an informal command ("put one **of them** in").

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Putting the noun in anyway.** *Ne voglio due cornetti* is wrong — if you use **ne**, you don't repeat the noun. Say *Ne voglio due.*
2. **Forgetting the number.** *Ne voglio* is fine only when the quantity is vague ("I want some"). If you mean two, you must say *Ne voglio due.*
3. **Word order.** **Ne** is not English "of them" at the end. *Voglio due ne* is wrong. It goes before the verb: *Ne voglio due.*
4. **Confusing ne with ci.** *Ci* = "there / in it / to it"; *ne* = "of it / of them." Together they become **ce ne**: *Ce ne sono due.*
5. **No agreement in the past.** If you've counted something, the participle ends in **-i** (m.) or **-e** (f.): *Ne ho comprati tre*, *Ne ho comprate tre*. Don't leave it as *comprato*.
6. **Using ne where you need a direct object pronoun.** *Lo voglio* (I want **it** — the whole thing) vs *Ne voglio* (I want some **of it**). Different jobs.
