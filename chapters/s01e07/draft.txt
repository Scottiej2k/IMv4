# Adjectives: Agreement and Position

## What it is

An adjective is a word that describes a noun: *alto* (tall), *timido* (shy), *bello* (good-looking), *gentile* (kind). In Italian, an adjective must **agree** with the noun it describes — in gender (masculine or feminine) and in number (singular or plural). If the noun is feminine plural, the adjective is feminine plural too. This is the single most important thing about Italian adjectives, and the thing English speakers forget most often, because English adjectives never change: *a tall boy*, *a tall girl*, *tall boys* — always *tall*.

## How it works

### 1. Four-form adjectives

Many adjectives have four endings: masculine singular **-o**, feminine singular **-a**, masculine plural **-i**, feminine plural **-e**.

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | alt**o** | alt**i** |
| feminine | alt**a** | alt**e** |

- Marco è **alto**. / Marco e Leo sono **alti**.
- Emma è **alta**. / Emma e Bianca sono **alte**.

*Alto*, *basso*, *timido*, *simpatico*, *antipatico*, *bello*, *vecchio*, *giovane* all work this way.

### 2. Two-form adjectives

Adjectives ending in **-e** have only two forms: **-e** for the singular (masculine *and* feminine) and **-i** for the plural (masculine *and* feminine).

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | gentil**e** | gentil**i** |
| feminine | gentil**e** | gentil**i** |

- Il signore è **gentile**. / La signora è **gentile**.
- Le persone sono **gentili**. / I ragazzi sono **gentili**.

*Gentile*, *intelligente* and *elegante* are two-form adjectives. With them you never have to choose between *-o* and *-a* — but you still have to add *-i* in the plural.

### 3. Position: after the noun

In Italian the adjective normally comes **after** the noun. This is the opposite of English.

- i capelli neri (black hair)
- gli occhi grandi (big eyes)
- un naso piccolo (a small nose)
- una faccia tranquilla (a calm face)

When an adjective follows the noun, it often just tells you a fact or makes a distinction, like *neri* against other colors.

### 4. Bello and buono before the noun

Some very common adjectives can come **before** the noun, and *bello* and *buono* then change shape like an article. This is a fixed pattern: learn it as a block.

| before a masculine singular noun | un **bel** ragazzo, un **bello** studente |
|---|---|
| before a feminine singular noun | una **bella** faccia |
| before a plural noun | **bei** capelli, **belle** persone |

So you say *un **bel** ragazzo* (a good-looking boy), but *una **bella** ragazza*. Compare: *È un ragazzo **bello*** works too, with *bello* after the noun — both are correct, but the position changes the rhythm of the sentence. After the noun, *bello* is regular: *bello, bella, belli, belle*.

### 5. Molto + adjective

*Molto* (very) does not agree with the noun when it stands before an adjective: it stays **molto**.

- Il finestrino è **molto** bello.
- Bianca è **molto** intelligente.

It only changes when it means "many" and stands before a noun: *molte persone*.

## From the story

[[Il ragazzo ha i capelli neri.]] — adjective after the noun, masculine plural *neri* with *capelli*.
[[Ha gli occhi grandi e un naso piccolo.]] — two adjectives, both after their nouns, both masculine plural or singular.
[[Lui è alto. Non è basso.]] — the four-form pattern in the masculine singular.
[[La signora Ornella è vecchia ma elegante.]] — *vecchia* is four-form (-a), *elegante* is two-form (-e), same subject.
[[È timido. Le persone timide sono intelligenti.]] — *timido* masculine singular; *timide* feminine plural; *intelligenti* two-form plural.
[[Secondo me il ragazzo è bello.]] — *bello* after a noun, regular form.
[[Le persone sono simpatiche o antipatiche.]] — feminine plural in *-e*, because *persone* is feminine.
[[La donna è vecchia. L'uomo è vecchio.]] — the same adjective, two different genders.

## Common mistakes

- **Putting the adjective before the noun.** English says *a tall boy*; Italian says *un ragazzo alto*. Keep the adjective after the noun unless it is one of the common ones (*bello*, *buono*, *grande*, *piccolo*) that can go first.
- **Forgetting agreement.** *Emma è alto* is wrong; *Emma è **alta***. *Le persone sono simpatico* is wrong; *Le persone sono **simpatiche***.
- **Using *-o* for a two-form adjective.** *Gentilo* and *intelligento* do not exist: it is *gentile*, *intelligente*, plural *gentili*, *intelligenti*.
- **Making *molto* agree with the adjective.** *È molta bella* is wrong; *È **molto** bella*.
- **Mixing up *alto* and *grande*.** *Alto* is about height (*un ragazzo alto*); *grande* is about size (*una casa grande*).
