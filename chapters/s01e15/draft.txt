# Time, days, months, and dates in Italian

## What it is

In this episode the Carters plan the whole month of January, the Befana arrives on the 6th, and Kevin's beautiful calendar falls apart because of times, days, and dates. Italian handles all of these with two very small words: **che ore sono?** to ask the time, and **a che ora?** to ask when something happens. Days of the week, months, and dates each have their own simple rules — and once you know them, you can plan anything.

## How it works

### Asking and telling the time

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| **Che ore sono?** / **Che ora è?** | What time is it? |
| **Sono le** otto. | It's eight o'clock. |
| **È l'una.** | It's one o'clock. |
| **A che ora** parte il treno? | **At what time** does the train leave? |
| **Alle** sette e mezza. | **At** seven thirty. |

Telling the time uses **sono le** + number: *Sono le due, sono le dieci.* The only exception is one o'clock, which is singular: **È l'una.** For "at" a time, use **alle** (*alle tre, alle otto*), and again **all'una** for one o'clock. You can add *e mezza* (half past), *e un quarto* (quarter past), *meno un quarto* (quarter to), and **di mattina**, **di pomeriggio**, **di sera**, **di notte**.

### Days of the week

lunedì, martedì, mercoledì, giovedì, venerdì, **sabato**, **domenica**

Days are masculine and take the definite article when you mean "on" a day, regularly: **il lunedì** ("on Mondays"), **il sabato**, **la domenica**. Note that *sabato* and *domenica* do not end in -ì. When the day is just a label, no article is needed: *Oggi è lunedì.*

### Months and dates

gennaio, febbraio, marzo, aprile, maggio, giugno, luglio, agosto, settembre, ottobre, novembre, dicembre

Months are all masculine and take **no article** in a date: **il 6 gennaio** ("on January 6th"), **il 5 gennaio**, **il 1° maggio**. For the first of the month it is always **il primo**: *il primo gennaio*.

### Seasons

la primavera, l'estate, l'autunno, l'inverno — *in primavera, in estate, in autunno, in inverno.*

## From the story

[[Lunedì, che ore sono?]]
Asking the time with a day attached — the question word **che** plus **ore sono**.

[[A che ora parte il treno di Emma?]]
**A che ora** asks for the specific time of an event; the answer needs **alle**.

[[Alle sette e mezza.]]
The answer to **a che ora**: **alle** + number, with *e mezza* for half past.

[[Sono le sette e un quarto.]]
Telling the time uses **sono le** and can add *e un quarto* for quarter past.

[[Oggi è il 6 gennaio. Quindi la data giusta è oggi.]]
The date format: **il** + number + month. No article before the month itself.

[[Il 6 gennaio, tesoro. Sempre il 6 gennaio.]]
The Befana's date, repeated: **il 6 gennaio**, always with **il**.

[[Martedì, giovedì, domenica.]]
Days of the week in a list, no article needed when they are simply names.

[[Il sabato la Befana dorme.]]
**Il sabato** with the article means "on Saturdays" — the repeated day.

[[Emma prende il treno alle sette e mezza la mattina.]]
**Alle** for the exact time, with **la mattina** for which part of the day.

## Common mistakes

- **Saying *in gennaio* for a date.** Use **il**: *il 6 gennaio*, not *in 6 gennaio*. **In** is only for seasons: *in inverno*.
- **Answering *a che ora* with *sono le*.** *A che ora?* needs **alle**: *Alle otto*, not *Sono le otto*.
- **Forgetting *È l'una*.** All other hours are plural with **le**, but one o'clock is singular.
- **Adding an article before months.** *Vado in Italia a **gennaio*** — not *a il gennaio*. Months stand alone.
- **Capitalizing days and months.** In Italian, *lunedì*, *gennaio*, *domenica* are all lowercase, unlike English.
- **Using *lunedì* to mean "every Monday".** For a habit, add the article: **il lunedì** means "on Mondays."
