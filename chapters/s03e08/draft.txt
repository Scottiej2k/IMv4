# The Negative Imperative and the *voi* Imperative

## What it is

The **imperative** is the form of the verb you use to tell someone to do something: *Vieni!* ("Come!"), *Aspetta!* ("Wait!"). In this chapter you meet two very useful sides of it:

1. The **negative imperative with *tu***: how you tell one person **not** to do something. *Non parlare!* ("Don't talk!")
2. The ***voi* imperative**: how you tell a group of people to do something. *Venite! Sbrigatevi!* ("Come! Hurry up!")

Both are extremely common in Italian daily life, and both are simpler than English learners expect. The negative *tu* form, in particular, is famous for being "the easy one".

## How it works

### 1. Positive *tu* imperative (review)

For regular verbs, the *tu* command is the same as the third-person singular of the present tense.

| Verb | *tu* command |
|---|---|
| parlare | **Parla!** (Speak!) |
| prendere | **Prendi!** (Take!) |
| dormire | **Dormi!** (Sleep!) |
| andare | **Va'! / Vai!** |
| fare | **Fai!** |
| dare | **Da' / Dai!** |
| essere | **Sii!** |
| avere | **Abbi!** |

### 2. Negative *tu* imperative: `non` + infinitive

This is the key rule of the chapter. To say "Don't (do something)" to one person, put **non** before the **infinitive** — not the command form.

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| Parla! | **Non parlare!** |
| Prendi! | **Non prendere!** |
| Dormi! | **Non dormire!** |
| Vai! | **Non andare!** |
| Fai! | **Non fare!** |
| Dai! | **Non dare!** |
| Sii gentile! | **Non essere** maleducato! |
| Abbi paura! | **Non avere** paura! |

So *Non parlare!* is correct, and *~~Non parli!~~* is not. Almost all verbs behave this way, including irregular ones — they all fall back to the plain infinitive.

### 3. The *voi* imperative: talking to a group

When you talk to two or more people, use the *voi* form. It is very easy: for regular verbs it is the same as the present-tense *voi* form.

| Verb | *voi* command |
|---|---|
| parlare | **Parlate!** |
| prendere | **Prendete!** |
| dormire | **Dormite!** |
| andare | **Andate!** |
| fare | **Fate!** |
| dare | **Date!** |
| venire | **Venite!** |
| essere | **Siate!** |
| avere | **Abbiate!** |
| sapere | **Sappiate!** |

The negative *voi* form is normal: **non** + the *voi* command. *Non parlate!* ("Don't talk!") *Non andate!* ("Don't go!")

### 4. The *noi* imperative: "let's"

To say "let's...", Italian uses the present-tense *noi* form, with no pronoun: **Andiamo!** ("Let's go!") **Aspettiamo!** ("Let's wait!") **Mangiamo!** ("Let's eat!")

## From the story

[[Venite! Andiamo! Sbrigatevi!]]
— Kevin calls the whole family with three *voi* commands in a row. *Sbrigatevi* is the reflexive verb *sbrigarsi*.

[[Non andate a destra! La strada è a sinistra]]
— Negative *voi*: *non* + *andate*, telling a group not to go somewhere.

[[Non preoccuparti, mamma.]]
— Classic negative *tu*: literally "don't worry yourself". *Preoccuparti* is the infinitive with the reflexive ending attached.

[[Non ridere, Lucia.]]
— Franco to Lucia: negative *tu*, infinitive *ridere*.

[[Ma presta attenzione: una parola alla volta.]]
— Positive *tu* command with *prestare attenzione* ("pay attention"). Contrast it with *non prestare attenzione*.

[[Non parlare troppo.]]
— Matteo warns Kevin: the negative *tu* is the most common way to give friendly advice.

[[Non mettere i tovaglioli così.]]
— Another negative *tu*, with *mettere*. Note that it sounds like a suggestion, not an order.

[[Ascoltate un momento.]]
— Positive *voi* from *ascoltare*: Franco asks the whole table to listen.

[[Dai una mano a Lucia.]]
— Positive *tu* from *dare*, then follow it with an indirect object: *a Lucia*.

[[Andiamo!]]
— The *noi* imperative: "let's go" — Linda tries it out with her new Italian.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

- **Using the present tense for a negative command.** English speakers say *~~Non parli!~~* because it "looks" like a command. Remember: the negative *tu* is always **non + infinitive**.
- **Forgetting the reflexive ending.** *Sbrigati!* / *Non sbrigarti!* make sense grammatically, but the natural sentence to a group is **Sbrigatevi!** (not *~~Sbrigati tutti!~~*). Match the form to the number of people.
- **Using *tu* commands with a group.** *Vieni!* is for one person. For a group, use **Venite!**
- **Using *noi* when you mean *voi*.** *Andiamo!* means "let's go" — the speaker comes too. *Andate!* means "you (all) go" — the speaker stays.
- **Mixing accents.** *Da'* (with an apostrophe) is the *tu* command of *dare*; *dai* also means "come on!" in conversation. When you're unsure, use *dare una mano* — a phrase the whole family understands.
- **Forgetting formal *Lei* exists.** This lesson is about *tu* and *voi*. Formal commands (*Parli!*, *Venga!*) use a different set of forms and come later.

## Practice

Turn each sentence into the form requested.

1. *Parlare* → tell one person: "Don't talk!" → ________
2. *Venire* → tell a group: "Come here!" → ________
3. *Andare* → tell a group: "Don't go!" → ________
4. *Aspettare* → suggest to your friends: "Let's wait!" → ________
5. *Preoccuparsi* → tell your mother (one person): "Don't worry!" → ________
6. *Sbrigarsi* → tell your kids (a group): "Hurry up!" → ________
7. *Prendere* → tell one person: "Don't take the bag!" → ________
8. *Ascoltare* → tell the whole class: "Listen!" → ________

### Answer key

1. **Non parlare!**
2. **Venite qui!**
3. **Non andate!**
4. **Aspettiamo!**
5. **Non preoccuparti!**
6. **Sbrigatevi!**
7. **Non prendere il bagaglio!**
8. **Ascoltate!**
