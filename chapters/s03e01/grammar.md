# The Imperfetto: Forms

## What it is

The **imperfetto** is the Italian past tense for "how things used to be". English has several ways to say it: *I played*, *I used to play*, *I was playing*. Italian has one tense for all of these when you are talking about the past in a general, open way — childhood, habits, memories, descriptions of how things were.

In this episode the family talks about childhood: what they played, what they had, what their mothers used to say. That is exactly the imperfetto's natural home. You will hear it constantly in Italian whenever someone says *quando ero piccolo…* ("when I was little…").

Note that this lesson is about the **forms**. Later chapters will teach the difference between the imperfetto and the passato prossimo.

## How it works

### Regular endings

Take the infinitive, drop **-re**, and add the endings. Every verb has the same endings except *-ere* verbs, which change the vowel to **-e-** in the *noi* and *voi* forms.

| Person | giocare (to play) | credere (to believe) | dormire (to sleep) |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | gioc**avo** | cred**evo** | dorm**ivo** |
| tu | gioc**avi** | cred**evi** | dorm**ivi** |
| lui / lei / Lei | gioc**ava** | cred**eva** | dorm**iva** |
| noi | gioc**avamo** | cred**evamo** | dorm**ivamo** |
| voi | gioc**avate** | cred**evate** | dorm**ivate** |
| loro | gioc**avano** | cred**evano** | dorm**ivano** |

Notice that the **-iamo** of the present (*noi giochiamo*) becomes **-avamo / -evamo / -ivamo**.

### The four irregulars

Only four verbs are irregular, and they are all very common. Learn them by heart.

| Person | essere | fare | dire | bere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| io | **ero** | facevo | dicevo | bevevo |
| tu | **eri** | facevi | dicevi | bevevi |
| lui/lei/Lei | **era** | faceva | diceva | beveva |
| noi | **eravamo** | facevamo | dicevamo | bevevamo |
| voi | **eravate** | facevate | dicevate | bevevate |
| loro | **erano** | facevano | dicevano | bevevano |

*Essere* keeps the **-r-** of the infinitive; *fare* and *dire* keep the **-c-**; *bere* keeps the **-v-**. Once you see the pattern, they are easy to remember.

### Spelling protection

Verbs in **-care** and **-gare** add an **h** before endings that start with **-i-** (*cercavo, cercavi…* but *cercherò* is future — not yet). In the imperfetto this matters less because the endings begin with **-a-** or **-e-** most of the time. Just remember *giocavo*, not *giocavo* with a *c* problem — *giocare* is regular: **giocavo, giocavi, giocava, giocavamo, giocavate, giocavano**.

### The key phrase: quando ero piccolo / piccola

*Quando ero piccolo* is the standard way to start a childhood story. The adjective agrees with the speaker: a man says **quando ero piccolo**, a woman says **quando ero piccola**. In the plural: **quando eravamo piccoli / piccole**.

## From the story

«**Quando ero piccolo**, la **ricreazione** era il momento migliore.» — “**When I was little**, **break** was the best moment.” (`s03e01-1-022`)
*First person singular of essere + a regular -are verb (era). The opening formula of every childhood story.*

«**Da bambino** il mio cane **nascondeva** le mie scarpe» aggiunge. — “**As a child**, my dog **hid** my shoes,” he adds. (`s03e01-1-039`)
*Third person singular of nascondere: the -ere vowel -e- appears (nascondeva).*

«Tua madre **aveva** una **bambola** sola» dice. — “Your mother **had** only one **doll**,” Franco says. (`s03e01-2-058`)
*Third person singular of avere in the imperfetto: aveva, not avéva with stress — just aveva.*

«**Era** povera, ma **diceva** sempre che una **bambola** basta.» — “She **was** poor, but she always **said** one **doll** is enough.” (`s03e01-2-059`)
*Irregular essere (era) and irregular dire (diceva) side by side in one sentence.*

*Irregular bere in the noi form: bevevamo. Notice the -v- keeps the memory of the infinitive.*

«Anch'io **ricordavo** questa strada così» dice. — “I **remembered** this street like this too,” she says. (`s03e01-2-054`)
*A regular -are verb, first person: ricordavo. This is what the whole chapter is about.*

«**Da bambina** giocavo sempre con quel **pallone**» dice. — “**As a child** I always played with that **ball**,” she says. (`s03e01-2-045`)
*Giocavo, a regular -are verb with the -c- kept (no h needed here).*

*Irregular fare (facevi) and essere (eravamo) together: description in the past.*

«E **dimenticavo** sempre la merenda fuori.» — “And I always **forgot** my snack outside.” (`s03e01-4-069`)
*A long -are verb — dimenticare — is still perfectly regular: dimenticavo.*

«**Io giocavo** a baseball» dice. «Con la "a".» — “**I played** baseball,” Emma says. “With the ‘a.’” (`s03e01-4-053`)
*The correction Emma gives twice: giocavo, not "sono giocato".*

## Common mistakes

**1. Using the wrong vowel.** *-are* verbs take **-avo**, *-ere* verbs take **-evo**, *-ire* verbs take **-ivo**. English speakers often mix them up. Say *giocavo* (are), *credevo* (ere), *dormivo* (ire).

**2. Stressing the wrong syllable.** *Aveva* has the stress on the first syllable (**a**-ve-va). *Diceva* is **di**-ce-va. Saying *a-VE-va* sounds foreign.

**3. Forgetting that essere is irregular.** *Essere* is **not** formed with *ess-* + endings. It is **ero, eri, era, eravamo, eravate, erano**. Do not say *essevo* — that word does not exist.

**4. Missing the double consonant in facevo / dicevo.** These two verbs keep the **-c-** of the stem before the **-e-** of the ending, otherwise *fa-evo* would be unpronounceable.

**5. Using the passato prossimo where the imperfetto is needed.** *Ho giocato* answers the question "what happened?" (a specific event). *Giocavo* answers "what was it like?" (a period, a habit). At this level, when you talk about childhood, you almost always want the imperfetto.

**6. Confusing the yo/noi forms in the plural.** *Noi giocavamo* (we played) is not the same as *noi abbiamo giocato* (we played — and finished). The imperfetto plural keeps its own endings: **-avamo, -evamo, -ivamo**.

## Practice

Complete each sentence with the correct imperfetto form.

1. Quando \_\_\_ (essere) piccolo, io giocavo a nascondino.
2. Mio padre \_\_\_ (avere) una trottola.
3. Noi \_\_\_ (dormire) otto ore ogni notte.
4. Tu \_\_\_ (fare) sempre gli stessi errori.
5. Le bambine \_\_\_ (credere) alle storie del nonno.
6. Mia madre \_\_\_ (dire): il gioco non costa niente.
7. Io \_\_\_ (dimenticare) sempre le chiavi.
8. Loro \_\_\_ (bere) il caffè senza zucchero.

### Answer key

1. ero
2. aveva
3. dormivamo
4. facevi
5. credevano
6. diceva
7. dimenticavo
8. bevevano
