# The future of probability: *Dove sarà?*

## What it is

In Italian, the **future tense** does two jobs. The first is the one you already know: it talks about the future. *Domani andrò a Milano.* *Giovedì finirà bene.*

The second job is the one this episode is built on. The future also expresses a **guess about the present**. You don't know where someone is or what is happening right now, so you use the future to say what is *probably* true:

- **Sarà** a casa. — He's probably at home. / He must be at home.
- **Avrà** fame. — She's probably hungry.
- **Saranno** le otto. — It must be about eight o'clock.

Nobody is talking about tomorrow. You are saying: *I don't know for sure, but this is my best guess, right now.*

## How it works

Take the normal **futuro semplice** and point it at the present. That is the whole trick.

| Italian | What it means | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Sarà** in ufficio. | a guess about now | He's probably at the office. |
| **Avrà** il telefono spento. | a guess about now | His phone is probably off. |
| **Starà** bene. | a guess about now | He's probably fine. |
| **Saranno** le dieci. | a guess about the time | It must be about ten. |
| **Sarà** un funzionario. | a guess about who someone is | He's probably an official. |
| **Avranno** fame. | a guess about now | They're probably hungry. |

It works with **any verb**, not only *essere* and *avere*:

- **Sarà** ancora in volo. — He's probably still in flight.
- **Verrà** dal comune. — He probably comes from the town hall.
- **Dormirà**. — He's probably asleep.

### The usual suspects: *essere* and *avere*

Most guesses in real conversation use **sarà / saranno** and **avrà / avranno**:

- **Sarà** un cliente. / **Saranno** clienti.
- **Avrà** paura. / **Avranno** paura.

### *Sarà* for time and dates

For clock time, the future of probability is the normal choice:

- **Saranno** le otto. — It must be about eight.
- **Sarà** mezzogiorno. — It must be around noon.

Compare it with the plain present, which states a fact: *Sono le otto.* — It is eight o'clock.

### The future of probability vs. *forse* and *probabilmente*

This is the contrast that matters in this episode, because Lucia wants certainty and Franco keeps giving her guesses.

| Expression | Strength | Example |
|---|---|---|
| future of probability | strong guess, almost certain | **Sarà** in volo. |
| **probabilmente** | strong guess, said as a guess | **Probabilmente** è in volo. |
| **forse** | weak, uncertain | **Forse** è in volo. |
| **chissà** | no idea at all | **Chissà** dov'è. |

So *Sarà in volo* and *Probabilmente è in volo* are close in meaning. *Forse è in volo* is weaker: "maybe, I really don't know." And *Chissà dov'è* means "I have no idea where he is."

You can also combine them, and the guess gets softer, not stronger: *Forse sarà in volo* — "maybe he's in flight."

### A small warning about the past

For a guess about the past, Italian uses the **futuro anteriore** (*Avrà perso il treno* — "he probably missed the train"). That is a later lesson. For now, stay in the present: *Sarà ancora a Londra*, or *Probabilmente è ancora a Londra*.

## From the story

«Non ti preoccupare. **Starà** bene.» — "Don't worry. He's **probably** fine." (`s04e03-2-009`)
Franco's classic reassurance: not a future fact, but a probability about right now.

«**Avrà** il telefono spento. Succede a tutti.» — "His phone is **probably** off. It happens to everyone." (`s04e03-2-019`)
*Avrà* + noun — the strongest kind of guess about the present.

«Allora **sarà** in **volo**.» — "Then he's **probably** in **flight**." (`s04e03-2-026`)
*Sarà* + prepositional phrase: where someone probably is at this moment.

«Sarà in ritardo. In Inghilterra c'è sempre un ritardo.» — "He's probably delayed. In England there's always a delay." (`s04e03-2-029`)
The guess about a delay that *probably* exists right now.

«Saranno le otto, Kevin. Otto teorie prima delle otto.» — "It must be about eight, Kevin. Eight theories before eight o'clock." (`s04e03-1-081`)
*Saranno* for clock time — the single most common use of the future of probability.

«**Chissà**. Magari è così.» — "**Who knows**. Maybe that's right." (`s04e03-5-050`)
The opposite pole: not a guess at all, but a total lack of information.

«**Probabilmente** a posto o sicuramente a posto?» — "**Probably** in order or definitely in order?" (`s04e03-7-009`)
The contrast in one line: *probabilmente* (a strong guess) against *sicuramente* (certainty).

«Io sono un **funzionario**, non un **ispettore**.» — "I'm a **town-hall official**, not an **inspector**." (`s04e03-5-097`)
Future of probability with a noun predicate: "he must be an official."

«Gennaro, è tardi qui. **Saranno** le undici e mezza.» — "Gennaro, it's late here. It must be **about** eleven thirty." (`s04e03-6-072`)
Another *Saranno* + time: the most natural way to guess at the clock.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the future of probability for a real future event.** *Domani sarà a casa* means "tomorrow he'll be at home," a genuine future. For "he's probably at home now," you say *Sarà a casa*, with no time word at all.
- **Treating *forse* as stronger.** *Forse sarà a casa* is weaker than *Sarà a casa*, not stronger.
- **Reaching for the conditional.** English speakers want *sarebbe* or *potrebbe essere*. At this level, use the future: *Sarà a casa*, not *sarebbe a casa*.
- **Forgetting that *essere* is irregular.** It's *sarò, sarai, sarà, saremo, sarete, saranno* — not *esserò*.
- **Using the future of probability with a past participle.** *Avrà perso* is the *futuro anteriore*, a later lesson. For now say *Probabilmente ha perso il volo*, or stay in the present: *Sarà ancora a Londra*.
- **Translating "must" literally.** Italian doesn't use *dovere* for these guesses. *Deve essere a casa* sounds like an obligation. Say ***Sarà** a casa*.
