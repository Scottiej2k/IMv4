# Combined pronouns: *me lo*, *te la*, *glielo*, *ce ne*

## What it is

In Italian you often need two pronouns in the same phrase: one for the *person* (to me, to you, to him) and one for the *thing* (it, them, some). Instead of saying them separately, Italian puts the person first and glues the two words together:

- *Mi dai il libro?* → *Me lo dai?* ("Do you give it to me?")
- *Gli porto i pacchi.* → *Glieli porto.* ("I'll bring them to him.")

The indirect pronoun (*mi, ti, gli/le, ci, vi*) changes shape before the direct one (*lo, la, li, le, ne*):
**mi → me**, **ti → te**, **ci → ce**, **vi → ve**, **gli/le → glie** (written as one word with the direct pronoun: *glielo, gliela, glieli, gliele, gliene*).

## How it works

### The table

| Person | + lo | + la | + li / le | + ne |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| to me | **me lo** | **me la** | **me li / me le** | **me ne** |
| to you (tu) | **te lo** | **te la** | **te li / te le** | **te ne** |
| to him / to her | **glielo** | **gliela** | **glieli / gliele** | **gliene** |
| to us | **ce lo** | **ce la** | **ce li / ce le** | **ce ne** |
| to you (voi) | **ve lo** | **ve la** | **ve li / ve le** | **ve ne** |
| to them | **glielo** | **gliela** | **glieli / gliele** | **gliene** |

Note that *gli* (to him) and *le* (to her) both become **glie-** before the direct pronoun. One form covers both: *glielo do* = "I give it to him" or "I give it to her" — context tells you which.

### With the passato prossimo

The past participle agrees with the **direct** pronoun (*lo → -o*, *la → -a*, *li → -i*, *le → -e*):

- *Me lo **hai** trovato tu.* ("You found it for me.")
- *Te le **ho** date ieri.* ("I gave them to you yesterday.")
- *Glieli **abbiamo** portati noi.* ("We brought them to him.")
- *Ce ne **sono** ventisette.* (With *ne* the participle doesn't change.)

### With infinitives and imperatives

You can attach the pair to the **end** of an infinitive or an informal/formal imperative:

- *Voglio regalartelo.* ("I want to give it to you.")
- *Posso prestartela.* ("Can I lend it to you?")
- *Legalo forte!* (one pronoun) → *Legamelo forte!* ("Tie it for me tightly!")
- *Dammelo!* ("Give it to me!") · *Diteglielo!* ("Tell him!")
- Formal (Lei): *Me lo dica, per favore.* ("Tell it to me, please.")

With *ne* you get *dammene* ("give me some"), *parlagliene* ("talk to him about it").

## From the story

«**Te lo** regalo», dice Franco. — "I'm giving **it to you**," says Franco. (`s03e11-2-032`)
*Franco gives the cap to Doug: gluing "to you" and "it" into one word.*

«Grazie. **Me lo** hai trovato tu», dice Kevin. — "Thanks. You found **it for me**," says Kevin. (`s03e11-1-053`)
*Me lo + passato prossimo; the participle doesn't change because lo stays lo.*

«**Ce ne** sono ventisette», dice Kevin. — "There are twenty-seven of **them**," says Kevin. (`s03e11-1-056`)
*Ci + ne for "there are twenty-seven of them."*

*To you (plural) + it: the family is addressed all together.*

«Bravo. Ma **glielo** dico io: la valigia è già piena», dice Matteo. — "Good. But I'm telling **you**: the suitcase is already full," says Matteo. (`s03e11-3-046`)
*Gli + lo → glielo: Matteo tells it to Kevin.*

«**Te la** presto», dice Ornella a Doug. — "I'm **lending it to you**," says Ornella to Doug. (`s03e11-4-064`)
*With an infinitive idea behind it: "I'm lending it to you."*

«**Te le** do con tutto il cuore», dice Linda. — "I give **them to you** with all my heart," says Linda. (`s03e11-3-065`)
*Le is feminine plural: the little socks.*

«Glieli porto io alla posta lunedì», dice Matteo. — "I'll take them to you to the post office Monday," says Matteo. (`s03e11-3-084`)
*Two packages: masculine plural direct pronoun, so -i on the participle if you use one.*

«Duecento euro? No. **Glieli** restituisco io», dice Matteo. — "Two hundred euros? No. I'll give **them back to him**," says Matteo. (`s03e11-7-011`)
*The same pair with a present-tense verb: "I'll give them back to him."*

«**Legalo** forte. È **fragile**», dice Linda. — "**Tie it** tight. It's **fragile**," says Linda. (`s03e11-5-018`)
*One pronoun only, on an imperative — the base the combined form builds on.*

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Keeping the separate words.** *Mi lo dai* and *Ti la presto* sound wrong. The indirect pronoun always changes before the direct one: *Me lo dai · Te la presto.*
2. **Forgetting the participle agreement.** *Me lo hai trovata* is wrong if the thing is masculine: *Me lo hai trovato.* Watch *la* and *le* in particular: *Te l'ho data* (the letter), *Te le ho date* (the letters).
3. **Writing *gli lo* or *le lo*.** They always merge: **glielo, gliela, glieli, gliele, gliene**.
4. **Two apostrophes.** *Te l'ho dato* is fine, but do not write *tel'ho*. The full forms are *te lo ho…* in careful speech and *te l'ho…* in writing; the glued *glielo* never takes an apostrophe.
5. **Putting the pair in the wrong place.** With a plain present or past verb they go **before** it (*Te lo dico, gliel'ho detto*); with an infinitive or imperative they go **after** and join the verb (*dirtelo, dammelo, diteglielo*).
6. **Confusing *ne* with *lo*.** *Ne* means "of it / of them" (some of them, about it): *Ce ne sono ventisette*, not *ce lo sono*.
