# Il condizionale passato (the past conditional)

## What it is

The **condizionale passato** is the "would have" form. You use it to talk about something that *didn't happen* but that you can imagine, want, or regret. In this chapter it carries the whole theme: regrets, apologies and missed chances.

- *Avrei dovuto chiedermelo* → "I should have asked you."
- *Sarebbe stato meglio* → "It would have been better."
- *Avrei voluto dirtelo* → "I would have liked to tell you."

If the *condizionale presente* is "I would do it" (*lo farei*), the *condizionale passato* is "I would have done it" (*l'avrei fatto*).

## How it works

You build it with two pieces: the **conditional of *avere* or *essere*** plus the **past participle**. The auxiliary (*avere* / *essere*) is always the same one the verb uses in the *passato prossimo*.

### Con *avere*

| Person | + participle |
|---|---|
| io | avrei |
| tu | avresti |
| lui / lei | avrebbe |
| noi | avremmo |
| voi | avreste |
| loro | avrebbero |

*avrei parlato, avresti parlato, avrebbe parlato, avremmo parlato, avreste parlato, avrebbero parlato*

### Con *essere*

| Person | + participle |
|---|---|
| io | sarei |
| tu | saresti |
| lui / lei | sarebbe |
| noi | saremmo |
| voi | sareste |
| loro | sarebbero |

With *essere* the participle agrees like an adjective: *sarei andato / andata*, *saremmo partiti / partite*. So a woman says *sarei partita*, and *sarebbe stato* becomes *sarebbe stata* if the thing is feminine (*la settimana* → *sarebbe stata lunga*).

### The three you need most

Regrets about the past are usually built with *dovere*, *volere* and *potere*:

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| avrei dovuto | I should have |
| avrei voluto | I would have liked to |
| avrei potuto | I could have |

Note the shape: **conditional of the auxiliary + the infinitive**, not a participle. *Avrei dovuto parlare* ("I should have spoken"), never *ho dovuto parlare* for a regret.

### The future in the past (a preview)

The same form also reports a future seen from the past: *Mi ha detto che sarebbe venuto* ("He told me he would come"), *Sapevo che avrebbero capito* ("I knew they would understand"). You'll meet this use more and more in B1 reading.

## From the story

«Quando hai accettato il progetto di Roma, **avresti dovuto** chiedermelo.» — "When you accepted the Rome project, **you should have** asked me." (`s04e06-5-028`)
→ Kevin's central accusation: *dovere* in the conditional past turns "you should ask me" into a regret about something already done.

«**Avrei dovuto** dirtelo due anni fa, in faccia, non per telefono.» — "**I should have** told you two years ago, face to face, not on the phone." (`s04e06-4-045`)
→ Franco admits his own missed chance; *avrei dovuto* + infinitive, first person.

«Perché **avrei voluto** dirti cose mie, non parole degli altri.» — "Because **I would have liked** to tell you things of mine, not other people's words." (`s04e06-5-061`)
→ *volere* in the conditional past expresses a wish that never became real.

«**Sarebbe stato** più semplice per tutti e due.» — "**It would have been** simpler for both of us." (`s04e06-5-076`)
→ *essere* verb, so *sarebbe stato*; the participle is masculine singular because it refers to something unnamed and general.

«Forse **avrei dovuto** parlarle domenica sera, prima del treno.» — "Maybe **I should have** talked to her Sunday night, before the train." (`s04e06-6-024`)
→ An admission softened by *forse*: the most common way Italians admit a regret without sounding dramatic.

«**Avrei dovuto** chiamarti martedì, non aspettare una settimana.» — "**I should have** called you Tuesday, not waited a week." (`s04e06-5-075`)
→ Same structure, but the two actions are contrasted with *non*: what he should have done against what he did.

«**Avresti dovuto** venire a trovarla più spesso.» — "**You should have** come to see her more often." (`s04e06-4-025`)
→ The second person again: Franco telling Gennaro what he owed his mother.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the *passato prossimo* instead of the conditional.** *Ho dovuto chiamarti* means "I had to call you" (and I did). *Avrei dovuto chiamarti* means "I should have called you" (and I didn't). The regret lives in the conditional.
- **Forgetting the auxiliary.** *Avrei parlato* is right; *parlerei avuto* or *avrei parlare* is not.
- **Wrong agreement with *essere*.** It is *sarebbe stata una bella idea* if the noun is feminine, *sarebbero stati contenti* if the people are plural.
- **Putting *dovere* in the participle.** English says "should have asked", so learners write *avrei dovuto chiesto*. Italian keeps the second verb in the infinitive: *avrei dovuto chiedere*.
- **Confusing *avrei* and *avrò*.** *Avrò parlato* is the *futuro anteriore* ("I will have spoken"). One letter, a different world.

## Practice

Translate into Italian.

1. I should have told you (to you, informal) earlier.
2. It would have been better to wait.
3. She would have liked to come with us.
4. We should have asked for help.
5. You (plural) would have understood immediately.
6. He said he would have arrived at eight. (Ha detto che…)
7. Would you (informal) have done it differently?
8. I couldn't have said it better.

### Answer key

1. *Avrei dovuto dirtelo prima.*
2. *Sarebbe stato meglio aspettare.*
3. *Lei avrebbe voluto venire con noi.* (or: *avrebbe voluto venire anche lei*)
4. *Avremmo dovuto chiedere aiuto.*
5. *Avreste capito subito.*
6. *Ha detto che sarebbe arrivato alle otto.*
7. *L'avresti fatto diversamente?*
8. *Non avrei potuto dirlo meglio.*
