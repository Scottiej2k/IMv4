# Question Words and Negation

## What it is

Two things go together in Italian conversation: asking, and saying no. **Question words** are the little words that start a question — *chi, che cosa, dove, quando, come, perché, quanto, quale*. **Negation** is how you say something is not true: you put **non** before the verb. In this episode Leo asks about sixty questions a day; Maestra Paola answers them, and Kevin writes them all down.

## How it works

### The question words

| Italian | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **chi** | who | **Chi** firma i documenti? |
| **che cosa** | what | **Che cosa** mangi? |
| **dove** | where | **Dove** sono le chiavi? |
| **quando** | when | **Quando** arriva il treno? |
| **come** | how, like | **Come** stai? |
| **perché** | why; because | **Perché** ridi? |
| **quanto / quanta / quanti / quante** | how much, how many | **Quante** domande hai? |
| **quale / quali** | which | **Quale** è il migliore? |

**Quanto** agrees with the thing you are counting: *quanto costa?* (m. singular), **quanta** acqua? (f. singular), **quanti** caffè? (m. plural), **quante** domande? (f. plural). **Quale** takes **-i** in the plural: *quale*, *quali*.

### Word order

Italian questions keep the normal word order: question word + verb + everything else. There is no *do* / *does*.

- **Dove** va il treno? — Where does the train go?
- **Che cosa** fai al lavoro? — What do you do at work?

### Two kinds of question

1. **With a question word:** **Chi** è il tuo professore preferito? **Dove** vivi?
2. **By intonation alone.** Take a statement and raise your voice at the end (in writing, just add the question mark): *Il treno arriva?* *Hai fame?* Answer with **sì** or **no**, or with a full sentence: *Sì, ho fame.* / *No, non ho fame.*

### Negation: non + verb

**Non** goes **directly before the verb**, never at the end of the sentence.

- **Non** capisco. — I don't understand.
- Leo **non** ride. — Leo doesn't laugh.
- Kevin **non** risponde subito. — Kevin doesn't answer right away.

If there is an object, **non** still stays glued to the verb: *Non capisco la domanda.* (I don't understand the question.) English *don't / doesn't* splits the sentence; Italian never does.

To make a **sì/no question negative**, answer with **no** and repeat the verb with **non**: *Non è una corsa?* — *No, non è una corsa.*

## From the story

[[Chi vuole fare una domanda?]] — **Chi** asks about a person; no *do* is needed.
[[Che cosa è una domanda?]] — **Che cosa** is the standard way to say *what*; note that it stays two words.
[[Dove è la risposta?]] — **Dove** asks for a place.
[[Quante domande hai?]] — **Quante** agrees with the feminine plural **domande**.
[[Quale è il pomodoro migliore?]] — **Quale** asks you to choose one from a group.
[[Come si dice lavagna in America?]] — **Come** asks for the way something is said or done.
[[Perché mi piace la parola]] — **Perché** opens a question here, and answers one when it means *because*.
[[Quanto costa il pane?]] — **Quanto** with a singular masculine noun is the classic shopping question.
[[Non capisco niente]] — **non** before the verb; *niente* after it.
[[Non è una corsa]] — a full negative sentence with **essere**.
[[Perché scrivi le mie domande?]] — **Perché** plus an -ere verb, still in the simple present.
[[Io non ho le risposte. Ho le domande.]] — the negation sits right before **ho**, and the second sentence contrasts with it.

## Common mistakes

- **Putting *non* at the end.** English *I don't understand*, Italian **Non** capisco — never *Capisco non*.
- **Using *do / does*.** *Do you have questions?* → **Hai domande?** or **Hai delle domande?**
- **Forgetting agreement on *quanto*.** *Quanto domande?* ✗ → **Quante** domande? ✓ (domande is feminine plural).
- **Confusing *perché*.** The same word means both *why* and *because*: **Perché** ridi? / **Perché** è una buona risposta.
- **Answering a question word with *sì*.** *Sì* only answers a yes/no question. To *Dove vai?* you must give a place: *Vado a Monza.*
