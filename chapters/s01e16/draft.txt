# Modal verbs: *volere*, *potere*, *dovere*

## What they are

Some verbs don't describe an action by themselves. They say how the action stands: *you want* to do it, *you can* do it, *you have to* do it. These are modal verbs. In Italian the three you need most are:

- **volere** — to want
- **potere** — can, to be able to
- **dovere** — must, to have to

A modal verb is almost always followed by another verb in the infinitive. Nothing goes between them: no *a*, no *di*, no *of*.

- *Voglio aiutare.* — I want to help.
- *Posso entrare?* — Can I come in?
- *Devo parlare con Lei.* — I have to talk to you.

## How they work

All three are irregular in the present tense, and they share the same endings. Learn them as one block:

| | volere | potere | dovere |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | **voglio** | **posso** | **devo** |
| tu | **vuoi** | **puoi** | **devi** |
| lui / lei | **vuole** | **può** | **deve** |
| noi | **vogliamo** | **possiamo** | **dobbiamo** |
| voi | **volete** | **potete** | **dovete** |
| loro | **vogliono** | **possono** | **devono** |

Notice the pattern in the singular: *-oglio*, *-osso*, *-evo*. The **noi** and **voi** forms are regular-looking (*-iamo*, *-ete*) and are the easiest to remember.

Subject pronouns are optional. *Voglio* already means *I want*, so *io voglio* is only used for emphasis or contrast:

- *Tu vuoi aiutare. Io voglio mangiare.* — You want to help. I want to eat.

### Asking permission, expressing obligation, expressing wishes

**potere** asks and gives permission:
- *Posso entrare?* — Can I come in?
- *Può scrivere Lei.* — You can write it. (permission, formal)

**dovere** expresses obligation. It is stronger than the English *should*:
- *Lei deve leggere questo.* — You have to read this.
- *Dobbiamo parlare.* — We have to talk.

**volere** expresses a wish or an intention:
- *Voglio una copia per me.* — I want a copy for myself.
- *Domenica voglio pensare.* — On Sunday I want to think.

### One important negative

*Non devo* does **not** mean *I must not*. It means *I don't have to*.

- *Non devo lavorare oggi.* — I don't have to work today.
- *Non devo parlare.* — I shouldn't talk / I don't have to talk.

To say *you must not*, Italian uses *non potere*: *Non puoi entrare.*

### The polite fixed phrase

*Vorrei* is a special form of *volere*. It means *I would like* and it is used as a polite fixed phrase for ordering and requesting:

- *Vorrei fare una festa.* — I'd like to have a party.

You will meet the full conditional tense much later. For now, just treat *vorrei* as one useful chunk.

## From the story

[[Posso entrare? Devo parlare con Lei.]] — *potere* asks permission and *dovere* states an obligation, in the same breath.
[[Lei deve leggere questo.]] — Obligation with a formal *Lei*: the verb is *deve*, not *devi*.
[[Voglio una copia per me.]] — A wish, with *volere* + object, no second verb.
[[Lei non può mettere l'auto sul marciapiede.]] — *potere* in the negative: it is not permitted.
[[A che ora devo mettere fuori la spazzatura?]] — A question built on *dovere* + infinitive.
[[Può scrivere Lei.]] — *potere* giving permission, formal, with the subject after the verb.
[[Tu vuoi aiutare. Io voglio mangiare.]] — *volere* contrasted between two people, which is why the pronouns appear.
[[Dobbiamo parlare.]] — *dovere* + infinitive, the sentence the whole episode turns on.
[[Possiamo mangiare insieme.]] — *potere* as a small, warm invitation.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Adding a preposition.** English says *I want to go*, so learners write *voglio di andare* or *posso a parlare*. Italian never does this: *voglio andare*, *posso parlare*.
2. **Using *dovere* for everything.** *Devo* is strong. For *I should*, Italian often softens the sentence another way. Do not use *devo* when you only mean a suggestion.
3. **Confusing *non devo* and *non posso*.** *Non devo* = I don't have to. *Non posso* = I can't / I must not.
4. **Wrong person.** *Lei deve*, not *Lei devi*. The formal *Lei* takes the *lui/lei* form of the verb.
5. **Mixing up *potere* and *sapere*.** At this level, *potere* covers permission and possibility. *Sapere* (to know how to) comes later, so use *posso* for now.
6. **Forgetting the infinitive.** *Voglio un caffè* is fine (wanting a thing), but *voglio andare* needs the second verb to stay in the infinitive. Never conjugate it: not *voglio vado*.
