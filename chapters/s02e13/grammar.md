# Reciprocal verbs: "each other"

## What it is

Some verbs describe something two people do **to each other**. In English we say *they see each other*, *we write to each other*, *you two greet each other*. Italian has a simple trick for this: take the verb and add a small word in front — **ci**, **vi** or **si**.

- **ci vediamo** — we see each other
- **vi scrivete** — you (two) write to each other
- **si conoscono** — they know each other

The little word changes with the subject, exactly like a reflexive verb. Everything else about the verb stays normal.

| Subject | Word | Example |
|---|---|---|
| io + tu (we) | **ci** | **Ci** vediamo domani. |
| tu + tu (you two) | **vi** | **Vi** scrivete ogni sera. |
| lui/lei/loro | **si** | **Si** salutano sulla strada. |

## How it works

**Present tense.** The verb is the same one you already know; only the little word is new.

- Io e Tommaso **ci vediamo** ogni mattina. — Tommaso and I see each other every morning.
- Voi due **vi conoscete**? — Do you two know each other?
- Franco e Lucia **si salutano**. — Franco and Lucia greet each other.

**Passato prossimo.** These verbs take **essere**, and the past participle agrees with the subject, like all reflexive verbs.

- Noi **ci siamo conosciuti** al centro civico. — We met (each other) at the community center.
- Loro **si sono abbracciati**. — They hugged each other.
- Voi **vi siete scritti**? — Did you two write to each other?

**Which verbs do this?** Very often verbs of meeting, seeing, talking, loving:

| Verb | Reciprocal |
|---|---|
| vedere | **vedersi** — to see each other / to date |
| conoscere | **conoscersi** — to meet, to know each other |
| incontrare | **incontrarsi** — to meet up |
| salutare | **salutarsi** — to greet each other |
| abbracciare | **abbracciarsi** — to hug each other |
| scrivere | **scriversi** — to write to each other |
| sentire | **sentirsi** — to be in touch |
| telefonare | **telefonarsi** — to call each other |

**Not the same as reflexive.** A reflexive verb has one person doing something to themselves (*mi lavo* — I wash myself). A reciprocal verb has two (or more) people doing something to each other (*ci vediamo* — we see each other). Some verbs can be both: *si guardano* can mean "they look at each other" or "they look at themselves."

**Careful with "ci vediamo."** Italians also use it to say goodbye: *Ci vediamo!* means "See you!" — literally "we'll see each other."

## From the story

«Ma **vi conoscete**?» chiede Bianca. — “Wait, do you two **know each other**?” asks Bianca. (`s02e13-1-017`)
Bianca asks the question that opens the whole episode: do these two know each other?

«Sì... no... **ci conosciamo** di vista.» — “Yes... no... we **know each other** by sight.” (`s02e13-1-019`)
Emma answers with the reciprocal form — and a lie.

«Ah. E allora perché vi **salutate** così?» — “Ah. So then why do you **greet each other** like that?” (`s02e13-1-022`)
*vi salutate* — you two greet each other, in that strange way.

«Emma, io e te **ci conosciamo** bene, no?» — “Emma, you and I **know each other** well, right?” (`s02e13-4-045`)
Chiara uses the reciprocal form to say something warm and true.

«Io e te **ci vediamo** ogni giorno. Qui.» — “You and I **see each other** every day. Here.” (`s02e13-3-021`)
Franco's version: we see each other every day, but only here.

«**Ci incontriamo** qui, sulla strada. Come due persone normali.» — “We **meet each other** here, on the street. Like two normal people.” (`s02e13-6-025`)
Lucia's hope for the day: *ci incontriamo* — we meet each other.

«Quante volte **ci abbracciamo** davanti a qualcuno? Mai.» — “How many times do we **hug each other** in front of someone? Never.” (`s02e13-3-030`)
*ci abbracciamo* — a question that is really a complaint.

«Sì, quello. E **vi siete abbracciati**, anche.» — “Yes, that. And you **hugged each other**, too.” (`s02e13-5-038`)
Reciprocal verb in the passato prossimo, with *essere* and the participle in -i.

Le due donne **si abbracciano** forte, come due vecchie amiche. — The two women **hug each other** tightly, like two old friends. (`s02e13-7-024`)
Third person plural, in narration: two people hug each other.

«E **vi sentite** tutti i giorni per **amicizia**?» — “And you **talk to each other** every day for **friendship**?” (`s02e13-5-026`)
*vi sentite* — you stay in touch with each other.

## Common mistakes

**Forgetting the little word.** *Vediamo ogni giorno* means "we see every day" (the world, the news). *Ci vediamo ogni giorno* means "we see each other every day." This is the mistake learners make most.

**Using avere in the past.** These verbs need **essere**: *ci siamo conosciuti*, not ~~ci abbiamo conosciuto~~.

**Wrong agreement.** With *essere*, the participle behaves like an adjective: *si sono abbracciat**i*** (two men, or a man and a woman), *si sono abbracciat**e*** (two women).

**Mixing up the person.** *Ci* is for *noi*, *vi* is for *voi*, *si* is for *loro*. *Noi vi vediamo* means "we see you"; *noi ci vediamo* means "we see each other."

**Body parts take the article, not the possessive.** *Si stringono la mano* means "they shake hands (each other's)." Say *la mano*, not ~~la loro mano~~.

**Adding "each other" twice.** Don't write ~~ci vediamo l'un l'altro~~ in everyday speech. The little word does the whole job.
