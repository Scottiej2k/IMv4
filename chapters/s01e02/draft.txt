# Nouns: gender and number

## What it is

Every Italian noun has a **gender** – masculine or feminine – and a **number** – singular or plural. Gender is part of the word itself, like its meaning: *tazza* is feminine, *bicchiere* is masculine, and there is no neutral option. You cannot guess gender from what the object is, only from the word's ending, which is why it is best learned together with the article from the very first day: *la tazza*, *il bicchiere*, *lo scontrino*.

The good news is that the ending usually tells you both gender and plural. Most Italian nouns follow three simple families, and only a few very frequent words behave differently. This chapter's story, set in a bar and at a Sunday lunch table, is full of all of them.

## How it works

### The three main families

| Singular | Plural | Gender | Examples from the story |
|---|---|---|---|
| **-o** | **-i** | masculine | *il piatto → i piatti*, *il bicchiere → i bicchieri*, *lo scontrino → gli scontrini* |
| **-a** | **-e** | feminine | *la tazza → le tazze*, *la parola → le parole*, *la brioche → le brioche* |
| **-e** | **-i** | masculine *or* feminine | *il pane → i pani*, *la carne → le carni*, *il dolce → i dolci* |

Nouns in **-e** are the only ones where the ending alone doesn't reveal the gender. You learn them with the article: *il pane* (m), *la carne* (f), *il latte* (m), *la frutta* (f), *il vino* (m), *il pranzo* (m). Notice that **frutta** looks feminine because of the *-a*, but its plural is irregular in everyday use: Italians normally say *la frutta* even when talking about many pieces of fruit.

### Words that don't change, and the famous exceptions

- **Accented words don't change:** *il caffè → i caffè*, *la città → le città*. Nothing moves except the article.
- **Shortened words don't change:** *il bar → i bar*, *la foto → le foto* (a photo), *l'auto → le auto*.
- **Masculine nouns in -a:** *il problema → i problemi*, *il programma → i programmi*. They are masculine even though they end in *-a*. This is why the chapter's *problema* is always *il problema*, never *la problema*.
- **Foreign words usually don't change:** *la brioche → le brioche*, *il computer → i computer*.

### The article moves with the noun

You can often hear a mistake before you see it, because the article changes too:

- *il / lo / l'* and **i / gli** for masculine: *il caffè, i caffè; lo scontrino, gli scontrini; l'americano, gli americani*
- *la / l'* and **le** for feminine: *la tazza, le tazze; la parola, le parole; l'acqua, le acque*

### One fixed phrase: *vorrei*

*Vorrei* means "I would like". Learn it as a single block, exactly as it is, because it is the polite way to order anything: **Vorrei un caffè, per favore.** You can put any noun after it, singular or plural: *Vorrei un cornetto. Vorrei due cornetti. Vorrei dell'acqua.*

## From the story

[[In America il caffè è caffè.]] — *caffè* is accented: singular and plural are identical, only the article changes.
[[Questa tazza è per la minestra.]] — a feminine noun in *-a*; the plural would be *le tazze*.
[[Brioche è la stessa cosa.]] — a foreign feminine noun in *-e* that stays the same in the plural.
[[Il latte è latte.]] — a masculine noun in *-e*: you must learn it with its article.
[[La pasta è la pasta.]] — *-a* feminine, plural *le paste* (which Italians rarely need).
[[Le parole sono gentili.]] — the regular feminine plural *-a → -e*.
[[Pasta, carne, frutta, dolce.]] — four nouns of different genders and endings, listed exactly as a menu.
[[Il bancone è per il caffè.]] — singular masculine *-e* noun (*il bancone*), plural *i banconi*.
[[Il conto sono i soldi.]] — singular *il conto*, plural *i conti*: the classic *-o → -i* family.
[[Vorrei un cappuccino, per favore.]] — the whole polite formula, with *un* + masculine singular noun.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Using "the" as one word.** English has one article; Italian has *il, lo, la, l', i, gli, le*. If you learn *tazza* without *la*, you will say *il tazza*.
2. **Making *il problema* feminine.** It ends in *-a*, but it is masculine: *il problema, i problemi*.
3. **Pluralising *il caffè*.** You never say *i caffi*. Accented nouns are invariable: *due caffè*.
4. **Using the wrong plural ending.** *-o* does not become *-e* (*i piatti*, not *i piatte*) and *-a* does not become *-i* (*le tazze*, not *le tazzi*).
5. **Forgetting that *-e* nouns can be masculine or feminine.** *Il latte* and *la carne* share an ending but not a gender.
6. **Changing the article and not the noun, or the noun and not the article.** They always move together: *lo scontrino → gli scontrini*.
7. **Treating *vorrei* as a normal verb to conjugate in this chapter.** For now, use the fixed phrase *vorrei* + noun. It is the polite formula Italians hear dozens of times a day.
