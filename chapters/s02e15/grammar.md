# Formal and Informal Address: *tu*, *Lei*, and *darsi del tu*

## What it is

Italian has two ways of saying "you" to one person. **Tu** is informal: family, friends, kids, classmates, people your own age you know well. **Lei** (always with a capital letter when it means "you") is formal: strangers, shopkeepers, officials, teachers, doctors, and older people you don't know well.

Italian has no word like English "you" that works everywhere. Choosing *tu* or *Lei* is a small social decision you make every time you open your mouth — and, as Kevin discovers in this episode, getting it wrong is not a grammar error, it is a manners error.

The verb forms are the easy part: **Lei uses the same verb forms as *lui* / *lei* (he/she)**.

| | tu (informal) | Lei (formal) |
|---|---|---|
| essere | (tu) **sei** | (Lei) **è** |
| avere | (tu) **hai** | (Lei) **ha** |
| stare | (tu) **stai** | (Lei) **sta** |
| chiamarsi | (tu) ti **chiami** | (Lei) si **chiama** |
| potere | (tu) **puoi** | (Lei) **può** |

- *Tu come stai?* → *Lei come sta?*
- *Tu ti chiami Leo?* → *Lei si chiama Carter?*

## How it works

**Subject pronouns.** You usually leave them out (Italian verbs already show the person), but you often keep *Lei* for clarity and politeness: *Come sta **Lei**?* When it is a direct object, *Lei* becomes **La**; as an indirect object, **Le**: *La ringrazio.* (Thank you.) *Le porto un dolce.* (I'll bring you a dessert.)

**Possessives.** With *tu* you say *il tuo* / *la tua*. With *Lei* you say **Suo / Sua**: *La **Sua** crema è chiara.* (Your cream is pale.) Note the capital *S* in writing.

**Fixed polite phrases**, all built on *Lei*:
- **Buongiorno** / **Buonasera** — used with anyone, but mandatory with *Lei*.
- **Si accomodi.** — Please come in / have a seat.
- **La ringrazio.** — Thank you. (To a friend: *Grazie* / *Ti ringrazio*.)
- **Prego. Scusi. Mi scusi.** — Please. Excuse me. (To a friend: *Scusa.*)
- **Come sta?** — How are you? (To a friend: *Come stai?*)

**Titles live with *Lei*.** *Signora, signore, dottore, direttore, maestra, professore* — these all go with the formal verb: *Buongiorno, **dottore**. Come sta?*

**Children are the exception.** Italian adults normally use *tu* with children, while the child may still use *Lei* with an adult. This is exactly what confuses Leo: he uses *Lei* to the headmaster, the headmaster uses *tu* to him, and neither is wrong.

**Darsi del tu** ("Let's use tu") is an event, not a rule. When two people who have been using *Lei* decide to become informal, one of them says *Possiamo darci del tu* or *Diamoci del tu*, usually the older or higher-status person first. After that, *Lei* and *La ringrazio* are gone. Going back to *Lei* is very rare and usually a sign of real anger.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. **Using *tu* with everyone.** English "you" is universal, so beginners call the baker *tu*. With a shopkeeper, an official or someone older you don't know, use *Lei*: *Buongiorno, **ha** un caffè?*
2. **Mixing *tu* verbs with *Lei* pronouns.** *Lei **stai** bene?* is wrong. *Lei* always takes third-person forms: *Lei **sta** bene?*
3. **Forgetting *La* and *Le*.** "I'll bring you a cake" is not *porto tu una torta* but **Le** *porto una torta.* And *La ringrazio*, never *ringrazio tu*.
4. **Using *tuo* with *Lei*.** *Come sta **tua** madre?* should be *Come sta **Sua** madre?*
5. **Thinking *Lei* means "she."** Context decides: *Lei è americano?* is "Are **you** American?" You can always add *signora* to make it clear.
6. **Rushing *darsi del tu*.** Wait for the older person to propose it — and if they don't, that is not a rejection, just formality.
7. **Answering *tu* with *Lei* and panicking.** Slipping back to *La ringrazio* after *diamoci del tu* happens to learners all the time. Italians find it funny, not rude.

Rewrite each sentence, changing the **tu** form to **Lei**, or the **Lei** form to **tu**.

### Answer key

1. Come **sta**, signora?
2. **Le** porto un caffè, dottore.
3. Buongiorno, Leo. **Hai** fame?
4. Come **ti chiami**, Pietro? (*Lei* → *tu*)
5. Posso provare il **Suo** dolce?
6. Ornella, **La ringrazio** per la visita.
7. **Scusa**, **sai** dov'è la farmacia? (*Lei* → *tu*)
8. Di dove **è**?
