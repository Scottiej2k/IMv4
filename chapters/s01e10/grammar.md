# Present tense: -ere and -ire verbs

## What it is

In Italian, verbs come in three big families, named after their endings: **-are** verbs (like *parlare*), **-ere** verbs (like *leggere*, *scrivere*, *prendere*, *vedere*, *vivere*), and **-ire** verbs (like *dormire*, *aprire*, *partire*, *sentire*).

You already know how *-are* verbs work. This lesson adds the other two families. The good news: the pattern is the same idea, only the vowels change. The endings tell you who is doing the action, so Italian usually drops the subject pronoun.

Inside the *-ire* family there is a small subgroup that adds **-isc-** to four of the six forms. These are verbs like *capire*, *finire* and *preferire*. They are very common, so it's worth learning them as their own little group.

## How it works

### -ere verbs: leggere (to read)

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| io | legg**o** |
| tu | legg**i** |
| lui / lei | legg**e** |
| noi | legg**iamo** |
| voi | legg**ete** |
| loro | legg**ono** |

The same endings go on *scrivere* (scrivo, scrivi, scrive, scriviamo, scrivono), *prendere* (prendo, prendi, prende, prendono), *vedere* (vedo, vede, vedono), *vivere* (vivo, vivi, vive, vivono).

### -ire verbs: aprire (to open)

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| io | apr**o** |
| tu | apr**i** |
| lui / lei | apr**e** |
| noi | apr**iamo** |
| voi | apr**ite** |
| loro | apr**ono** |

Same endings for *dormire* (dormo, dormi, dorme, dormono), *partire* (parto, parti, parte, partono), *sentire* (sento, senti, sente, sentiamo, sentono).

### -isc- verbs: capire, finire, preferire

These add **-isc-** to *io, tu, lui/lei* and *loro*. *Noi* and *voi* stay regular.

| Person | finire | capire | preferire |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | fin**isco** | cap**isco** | prefer**isco** |
| tu | fin**isci** | cap**isci** | prefer**isci** |
| lui / lei | fin**isce** | cap**isce** | prefer**isce** |
| noi | fin**iamo** | cap**iamo** | prefer**iamo** |
| voi | fin**ite** | cap**ite** | prefer**ite** |
| loro | fin**iscono** | cap**iscono** | prefer**iscono** |

Most *-ire* verbs take **-isc-**; a smaller group does not (aprire, sentire, dormire, partire, seguire). When you meet a new *-ire* verb, check a dictionary for the *io* form: if it ends in *-isco*, it's an *-isc-* verb.

## From the story

«Prima parola: **leggere**,» dice Alberto. «Io **leggo**, tu **leggi**, lui **legge**.» — “First word: **leggere**,” Alberto says. “I **read**, you **read**, he **reads**.” (`s01e10-2-025`)
The teacher shows the whole pattern for *leggere*: an *-ere* verb, regular in every form.

«Sì. **Leggo** il giornale con il vocabolario,» dice Kevin. — “Yes. I **read** the newspaper with the dictionary,” Kevin says. (`s01e10-2-029`)
*io leggo*. The ending *-o* already means "I", so the subject pronoun isn't needed.

«**Scrivo** una parola al giorno nel quaderno,» dice Kevin. — “I **write** one word a day in the notebook,” Kevin says. (`s01e10-2-032`)
*scrivere* behaves exactly like *leggere*: *io scrivo*.

«Perché “**finisco**” e non “**fino**”?» chiede Kevin. — “Why ‘**finisco**' and not ‘**fino**'?” Kevin asks. (`s01e10-2-036`)
Kevin spots the *-isc-* group with a real question: why does *finire* become *finisco*?

The rule, stated plainly: *-ire* verbs like these three take *-isc-*.

«Noi **finiamo**, voi **finite**, loro **finiscono**,» dice Alberto. — “We **finish**, you all **finish**, they **finish**,” Alberto says. (`s01e10-2-040`)
The two regular forms (*finiamo*, *finite*) sit next to the *-isc-* form (*finiscono*).

«Io **preferisco** il tè,» risponde Carla. «Il tè è **facile** da preparare.» — “I **prefer** tea,” Carla **answers**. “Tea is **easy** to make.” (`s01e10-2-043`)
*preferire* takes *-isc-*: *io preferisco*, not *prefero*.

*aprire* belongs to the *-ire* group *without* *-isc-*: *io apro*, never *aprisco*.

«**Sento** la **musica**,» dice Kevin. «Dalla **sala** vicina.» — “I **hear** the **music**,” Kevin says. “From the **room** next door.” (`s01e10-2-050`)
*sentire* behaves like *aprire*: *io sento*, *lui sente*, *loro sentono*.

«No,» dice Tommaso. «Tu **scrivi** come una che pensa in inglese.» — “No,” Tommaso says. “You **write** like someone who thinks in English.” (`s01e10-4-035`)
The *tu* form of *scrivere* is *scrivi*, with the *-i* ending.

## Common mistakes

**Adding -isc- to the wrong verbs.** English speakers hear *capisco* and *finisco* a lot, then invent *aprisco*, *sentisco*, *dormisco* for verbs that don't take it. Learn each *-ire* verb with its *io* form.

**Leaving -isc- out of the right verbs.** The opposite error: *io capo*, *io fino* are wrong. It is *capisco*, *finisco*.

**Using -are endings on -ere verbs.** *Io leggo* is correct, not *io lego* or *io leggio*. The *-ere* family keeps a light *-o*, *-i*, *-e* pattern.

**Putting -isc- in noi and voi.** It is *noi finiamo*, *voi finite*, not *noi finisciamo*.

**Keeping the subject pronoun every time.** *Io leggo* is fine for emphasis, but Italians usually say just *Leggo un libro*. The verb ending does the work.

**Confusing -ere and -ire in the tu form.** Both end in *-i*: *tu leggi*, *tu apri*, *tu finisci*. That's fine — the *noi* and *voi* forms are where they clearly differ (*leggiamo* / *apriamo*).

1. Complete: *Io ___ (prendere) un caffè.*
2. Complete: *Tu ___ (scrivere) una frase.*
3. Complete: *Noi ___ (capire) la lezione.*
4. Complete: *Loro ___ (finire) il compito.*
5. Complete: *Lei ___ (aprire) la finestra.*
