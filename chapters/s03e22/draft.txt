# Large numbers, percentages, fractions and money

## What it is

Italian numbers work like English numbers, but there are two things to learn carefully: how to write and say **big numbers** (thousands and millions), and how the words for **money**, **percentages** and **fractions** fit into a sentence. This chapter also gives you the everyday verbs of money: **spendere** (to spend), **risparmiare** (to save), **prestare** (to lend), **pagare** (to pay) and the nouns **il debito**, **il prestito**, **il conto**, **il bilancio**.

## How it works

### Thousands and millions

| Number | Italian | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | **mille** | irregular: never *uno mille* |
| 2,000 | **duemila** | -mila is one word, no space |
| 14,000 | **quattordicimila** | one word |
| 100,000 | **centomila** | one word |
| 1,000,000 | **un milione** | needs *un* |
| 2,000,000 | **due milioni** | plural *milioni*, separate word |

So **mille** behaves like a number (*mille euro*, *duemila lire*), while **milione**/**miliardo** are nouns: they need a number before them (*un milione*) and **di** before the thing counted: *un milione **di** euro*, *due milioni **di** persone*.

### Percentages

**per cento** never changes and never takes a hyphen: *il dieci per cento*, *il sessanta per cento*. Before it you normally use the article: *Il venti **per cento** del nostro reddito*. With a fraction, the article stays with the fraction: ***un terzo** del prestito*.

### Fractions

The pattern is a number plus **-esimo** in the denominator, but at A2 you only need the common ones by heart:

- **la metà** = a half (irregular — *mezzo* is an adjective: *mezza bottiglia*)
- **un terzo** = a third
- **un quarto** = a quarter
- **due terzi**, **tre quarti** = two thirds, three quarters

### Money: verbs and nouns

- **spendere / speso** — spend: *Ho speso cento euro.* *Spendo poco.*
- **risparmiare / i risparmi** — save / savings
- **prestare / il prestito** — lend / a loan
- **pagare / il pagamento** — pay / a payment
- **il conto** (bill, account), **la bolletta** (utility bill), **l'affitto** (rent), **lo stipendio** (salary), **la busta paga** (payslip), **il mutuo** (mortgage), **la rata** (installment), **il contante** (cash), **il resto** (change), **il centesimo** (cent), **il reddito** (income), **il bilancio** (budget/balance), **il debito** (debt), **l'interesse** (interest), **la tassa** (tax).

Verbs of money follow normal A2 patterns: **spendere** takes a direct object without a preposition (*spendo duecento euro*), while **pagare** can also take **per** (*pago per tutti*).

## From the story

[[Mille è mille. Un milione è mille volte mille.]]
→ *Mille* is one word, but *un milione* needs the article and is a separate word.

[[Quindi un milione è tantissimo. È come cento case.]]
→ *Un milione* — singular, with *un*, before a noun it would take *di*: *un milione di euro*.

[[Le entrate del mese: quattordicimila euro.]]
→ *Quattordicimila* is written as one single word, no space.

[[Il sessanta per cento delle entrate è caffè.]]
→ *per cento* is invariable and always preceded by the article.

[[Il venti per cento del nostro reddito.]]
→ Same pattern: *il* + number + *per cento* + *di* + article.

[[Il latte costa un terzo del caffè.]]
→ *un terzo* is a fraction; then *di* + article: *del caffè*.

[[La metà del prezzo del bar.]]
→ *la metà* is the irregular word for "half" — no *mezzo* here.

[[Sono i soldi che tornano indietro.]]
→ *i soldi* is always plural in this everyday meaning.

[[Un amico è gratis. Non ha prezzo.]]
→ *gratis* is an invariable adverb: it never agrees.

[[Franco. Trentamila. Nessun interesse.]]
→ *trentamila* one word; note **nessun** before a masculine noun starting with a vowel.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

1. ***uno mille*** — wrong. It is simply **mille**. But **un milione**, **un miliardo** need the article.
2. ***due mila euro* (two words)** — wrong: **duemila** is one word. The same goes for *tremila*, *diecimila*.
3. ***due milioni euro*** — wrong: *milione* is a noun, so you need **di**: *due milioni **di** euro*.
4. ***il dieci per cento*** vs *il dieci percento* — the expression is always two words: **per cento**.
5. ***la metà di tutto*** vs ***mezzo di tutto*** — use **la metà** for "half" as a noun; **mezzo/mezza** is an adjective (*mezza torta*).
6. Confusing **prestare** and **prendere in prestito**: *Ho prestato dieci euro a Marco* = I lent Marco ten euros. *Ho preso in prestito dieci euro* = I borrowed ten euros.
7. Forgetting that **i soldi** is plural: *i soldi sono finiti*, not *il soldo è finito*.
