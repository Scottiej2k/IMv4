# Adverbs in *-mente*, and *molto / tanto / troppo / poco*

## What it is

An adverb tells you **how**, **how much** or **when** something happens. In Italian, most adverbs of manner are built from an adjective, and they all end in **-mente** — the equivalent of English **-ly**: *lento* → *lentamente* ("slowly"), *veloce* → *velocemente* ("quickly"), *facile* → *facilmente* ("easily").

The words *molto*, *tanto*, *troppo* and *poco* are special: the same word can be an adjective (describing a noun, and changing ending) or an adverb (describing a verb or an adjective, and never changing).

## How it works

### 1. Forming the -mente adverb

Take the **feminine singular** of the adjective and add **-mente**:

| Adjective | Feminine | Adverb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| lento | lenta | **lentamente** | slowly |
| veloce | veloce | **velocemente** | quickly |
| facile | facile | **facilmente** | easily |
| vero | vera | **veramente** | really |
| chiaro | chiara | **chiaramente** | clearly |
| perfetto | perfetta | **perfettamente** | perfectly |

Adjectives ending in **-le** or **-re** keep just one *l/r* before -mente: *facile → facilmente*, *regolare → regolarmente*.

If the adjective ends in **-e** (like *veloce*, *semplice*), the feminine is the same as the masculine, so you just add -mente: *velocemente*, *semplicemente*.

### 2. Irregular adverbs you must memorize

Some very common adverbs are not formed with -mente:

- **bene** (well) — *Gioca bene.* ("He plays well.")
- **male** (badly) — *Aiuti male.* ("You help badly.")
- **piano** (slowly / quietly) — from *piano* "flat, soft".
- **forte** (hard / loudly) — from *forte* "strong".

*Bene* and *male* are irregular adverbs of *buono* and *cattivo*.

### 3. *molto, tanto, troppo, poco*: adjective or adverb?

| Role | Agrees? | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Adjective (before a noun) | Yes: molto/molti/molta/molte | **molti** amici, **troppa** roba |
| Adverb (with a verb) | No, always the same form | lavoro **molto**, mangio **poco** |
| Adverb (with an adjective) | No | **molto** bene, **troppo** forte |

The rule of thumb: if there is a **noun** right after the word, it agrees with it (*molti amici*). If there is a **verb** or an **adjective**, it stays invariable (*lavoro molto*, *molto bene*).

Compare:
- *Ha comprato **troppi** cornetti.* (adjective, agrees with *cornetti*)
- *Ha comprato **troppo** latte.* (*latte* is uncountable, so no plural)
- *Kevin corre **troppo** **velocemente**.* (adverb + adverb)
- *Ha lavorato **tanto**.* (adverb)

*Tanto* works exactly like *molto*: *tanta forza*, *lavora tanto*.

### 4. Position

The adverb of manner usually goes **after** the verb: *Gioca **lentamente***. In speech, *piano*, *forte*, *molto*, *poco* can also be repeated for rhythm: *piano piano*, *poco poco*.

## From the story

- Due signori giocano **lentamente**. — Two gentlemen play **slowly**. (`s02e18-1-008`) — *lentamente* is built from *lenta* + *-mente* and follows the verb.
- «È una boccia,» risponde Franco. «Il gioco è **lento**.» — “It's a boccia,” Franco answers. “The game is **slow**.” (`s02e18-1-015`) — here it is an adjective agreeing with *gioco*, not an adverb.
- Kevin butta la prima boccia **forte** e va fuori dal campo. — Kevin throws the first boccia **hard** and it goes off the court. (`s02e18-6-029`) — *forte* is an invariable irregular adverb meaning "hard".
- «Devo lavorare **poco**,» dice Nadia. «E devo **riposare** **molto**.» — “I have to work **little**,” says Nadia. “And I have to **rest** **a lot**.” (`s02e18-2-012`) — *poco* is an adverb with the verb *lavorare*, so it does not change.
- «Devo lavorare **poco**,» dice Nadia. «E devo **riposare** **molto**.» — “I have to work **little**,” says Nadia. “And I have to **rest** **a lot**.” (`s02e18-2-012`) — *molto* works the same way: invariable, after the verb.
- Ha comprato **troppi** cornetti. — He has bought **too many** croissants. (`s02e18-5-005`) — *troppo* is now an adjective: it agrees with *cornetti* (masculine plural → *troppi*).
- Ha comprato **troppo** latte. — He has bought **too much** milk. (`s02e18-5-006`) — *latte* is a mass noun (like *too much milk*); *troppo* is used as an adjective but has no plural.
- «Sono un **pochino** nervoso,» dice Kevin. — “I'm a **little** nervous,” says Kevin. (`s02e18-6-008`) — a very common spoken variant of *poco*: *un pochino* = "a little bit".
- «Aiuti **male**,» dice Nadia. — “You help **badly**,” says Nadia. (`s02e18-2-039`) — irregular adverb *male*, formed from *cattivo*.
- «Hai giocato piano,» dice Ornella. «E hai vinto.» — “You played slowly,” says Ornella. “And you won.” (`s02e18-6-073`) — *piano* here means "slowly", not "flat"; used as an adverb.
- «Abbiamo vinto!» dice Leo. — “We won!” says Leo. (`s02e18-6-066`) — from *vincere*; past participle *vinto*. Notice it carries *avere*, not *essere*: *abbiamo vinto*.

## Common mistakes

1. **Agreeing the adverb.** *Lavora molti* ❌ — *molto* with a verb never changes: ***Lavora molto*** ✓.
2. **Adding -mente to the masculine form.** *lentemente* ❌ (from *lento*); the correct form uses the feminine base: ***lentamente*** ✓.
3. **Double -mente adverbs.** *Lei parla velocementes lentamente* ❌ — adjectives do not take -mente twice. Only the last word in a series gets it: ***parla lenta e chiaramente*** ✓.
4. **Using *bene* before the verb.** *Bene gioca* ❌ — like most adverbs, *bene* usually comes after the verb: ***Gioca bene*** ✓.
5. **Forgetting the -e/-i endings on the adjective version.** *Ho troppa amici* ❌ (masculine plural needed) → ***Ho troppi amici*** ✓.
6. **Confusing *piano* as "floor/plan".** Context tells you: *Gioco piano* (adverb), *abito al primo piano* (noun).

## Practice

Translate or complete in Italian. Use the -mente adverb or the right form of *molto / tanto / troppo / poco*.

1. She speaks slowly. → Lei parla ______.
2. He runs quickly. → Lui corre ______.
3. We have too many problems. → Abbiamo ______ problemi.
4. I work a lot and sleep little. → Lavoro ______ e dormo ______.
5. You help badly. → Aiuti ______.
6. They played easily. → Hanno giocato ______.
7. There are very few bocce in the woods. → Le bocce nel bosco sono ______ poche.
8. Drink slowly, please. → Bevi ______, per favore.

### Answer key

1. lentamente
2. velocemente
3. troppi
4. molto / poco
5. male
6. facilmente
7. molto (or *troppo*, but *molto poche* — nota: *molto* with an adjective does not change: *molto poche* ✓)
8. piano
